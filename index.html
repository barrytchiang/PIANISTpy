Anisotropic spherical models, truncated in phase space

PIANISTpy builds spherical, non-rotating, velocity-anisotropic equilibrium models, self-consistently truncated in phase space, for dark matter (sub)halos, dwarf galaxies, stellar spheroids and star clusters. The package provides distribution functions, particle initial conditions, various velocity profiles and line-of-sight observables, with JAX-based end-to-end differentiability for rapid gradient-based Monte Carlo inference.

Install

pip install pianistpy. Python 3.11 or later. Runs on the CPU everywhere, with optional GPU acceleration.

What PIANISTpy does

  • Density profiles. Any double power law in its three slopes, with the Navarro-Frenk-White, Hernquist and Plummer profiles as named presets.
  • Velocity anisotropy. Isotropic, constant, Osipkov-Merritt, and the generalized Osipkov-Merritt (Cuddeford) models, radially biased (Type I) or tangentially biased (Type II).
  • Distribution functions. The distribution function of each model, from the analytic inversion of its density, returned whether or not it is physical, with a statement of where it is not.
  • Truncation in phase space. Method I truncates a model's own distribution function at a user-specified energy, or at the energy that yields a target mass or a target radius. Method II gives a new anisotropy to a density that is already truncated.
  • Particles. Positions and velocities drawn by inversion, with no rejection step, and reproducible particle by particle.
  • Observables. Intrinsic and line-of-sight velocity dispersions, line-of-sight velocity distributions, and their averages over apertures.
  • Superposition. Distribution functions inverted from one truncated density, combined with positive weights that sum to one into one equilibrium model.
  • Inference. The log-likelihood of a model against data, as a JAX function of the components' weights and the scales of the units.
