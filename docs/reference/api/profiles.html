Density profiles.

class pianistpy.profiles.DoublePowerLaw(rho_alpha, rho_beta, rho_gamma)#

Double-power-law density profile in internal units.

Parameters:

  • rho_alpha (float) – Sharpness \(\alpha\) of the transition between the inner and the outer slope. At least 1/2.

  • rho_beta (float) – Outer logarithmic slope \(\beta\). Greater than 2.

  • rho_gamma (float) – Inner logarithmic slope \(\gamma\), with \(0 \leq \gamma < 2\).

Notes

In internal units the scale radius is 1, the central potential is 1 and the potential vanishes at infinity:

\[\varrho(R) = C\,R^{-\gamma}\,(1 + R^{\alpha})^{(\gamma-\beta)/\alpha} .\]

The lower bound on \(\gamma\) excludes a density that decreases towards the center; the upper bound keeps the central potential finite. The bound on \(\beta\) keeps the potential finite at large radii.

The named profiles are parameter sets of this one profile, built by the class methods nfw, hernquist and plummer. They are not separate implementations. Where a profile has a closed form for its mass or its potential, that form is used inside mass and p; the tests hold it to the general incomplete-Beta form.

References

Paper I, Appendix A (labels eqn:double_power_law_MT, eqn:double_power_law_mass, app:P_beta).

Examples

>>> DoublePowerLaw.nfw()
DoublePowerLaw(rho_alpha=1.0, rho_beta=3.0, rho_gamma=1.0)
>>> DoublePowerLaw.hernquist().mass_total
1.0
>>> float(DoublePowerLaw.plummer().p(0.0))
1.0

p(r)#

Return the relative potential \(P(R)\).

Parameters:

r (array-like) – Radius in internal units.

Returns:

p – Relative potential in internal units, with the shape of r. It is 1 at the center and vanishes at infinity.

Return type:

array

Notes

With \(z = R^\alpha/(1+R^\alpha)\), \(a_2 = (2-\gamma)/\alpha\) and \(b_2 = (\beta-2)/\alpha\),

\[P(R) = \frac{\mathcal{M}(R)}{R} + \frac{C}{\alpha}\left[B(a_2, b_2) - B(z;\,a_2, b_2)\right] .\]

The arrays a kernel reads a TruncatedProfile from.

They are the numbers TruncatedProfile.potential, density, mass and radius evaluate, laid out for a kernel that takes them as traced arguments: the cubic Hermite in the logarithms on the interior rows and the edge’s closed forms beyond them.

Parameters:

  • log_p (array, shape (n_interior,)) – ln r, ln P and d ln P / d ln r at the interior rows (every row but an edge row).

  • log_r_all (array, shape (n,)) – ln r, ln M and d ln M / d ln r at every row.

  • log_mass_slope (array, shape (n,)) – ln r, ln M and d ln M / d ln r at every row.

  • log_rho_coefficients (array, shape (n_components, 4, n_interior - 1)) – The coefficients of the cubic spline of ln rho in ln r on each interval, highest power first, in the interval’s own ln r offset.

  • has_edge_row (bool) – Whether the profile carries its edge as its last row; the fields below are read only then.

class pianistpy.profiles.TruncatedProfile(r, p_trunc, rho_trunc, mass_trunc, k_t, r_t, mass_t, spatially_finite, source, source_anisotropy, model, edge_exponent, u_trunc=<factory>, central_deficit=(), override_r=nan, override_r_end=nan, override_rows=0, override_rule='none', origin=<factory>, df_k=<factory>, df_log_f=<factory>, df_slope=<factory>, df_taylor=())#

The self-consistent profile of a truncated model.

It is the profile of a model, and it can be the profile of a component of another model: a component given the truncated profile of a parent model and a new anisotropy is Method II of the paper.

Two profiles are equal where they belong to the same model: the identity of a model determines its profile.

The profile evaluates itself: potential, density, mass and radius are the one reading of the rows that every stage of the package takes, in place of an interpolant of its own. Between the rows the potential and the mass are cubic Hermite interpolants in the logarithms with the exact slopes the rows state (the Poisson equation gives \(\mathrm{d}\ln\mathcal{P}/\mathrm{d}\ln r = -M/(r\mathcal{P})\) and \(\mathrm{d}\ln M/\mathrm{d}\ln r = r^3\rho/M\)); the density of each component is a cubic spline in the logarithms, since the rows state no density slope. On the last interval of a spatially finite profile whose last row is its edge (\(\mathcal{P} = 0\) at r_t), the potential is the Kepler form \(M_t(1/r - 1/r_t)\) and the density follows the edge law \(\rho \propto \mathcal{P}^q\) with the stated edge_exponent; beyond r_t the potential is \(M_t/r\) and the density zero. Inside the first row and, for a profile without an edge, beyond the last, the interpolants continue as the power laws of the end rows.

Parameters:

  • r (array, shape (n,)) – Radius, strictly increasing.

  • p_trunc (array, shape (n,)) – Total relative potential of the truncated model.

  • rho_trunc (array, shape (n_components, n)) – Density of each component.

  • mass_trunc (array, shape (n,)) – Total enclosed mass, in the unit of DoublePowerLaw.mass (the Hernquist profile’s total is 1); 4 pi times it is the integral of the density over the volume.

  • k_t (float) – Truncation energy.

  • r_t (float) – Truncation radius and total mass; infinite where the model is not spatially finite.

  • mass_t (float) – Truncation radius and total mass; infinite where the model is not spatially finite.

  • spatially_finite (bool) – Whether the density reaches zero at a finite radius with a finite total mass. A spatially finite profile whose last row has p_trunc == 0 carries its edge as that row, and then the row’s density is zero and its radius is r_t.

  • source (DoublePowerLaw) – The analytic profile of the first component of the model: its scale radius and its central potential are the internal units the arrays are in.

  • source_anisotropy (Anisotropy) – The anisotropy of that component in the model the profile belongs to. A component built on this profile (Method II) reads here what its parent was.

  • model (str) – Identity string of the model the profile belongs to.

  • edge_exponent (tuple of float) – Exponent \(q\) of each component’s density at the edge of the model, \(\rho \propto \mathcal{P}^q\); empty where the model is not spatially finite and has no edge.

  • u_trunc (array, shape (n,)) – The central potential less the potential, p_trunc[0] + u_trunc[0] less p_trunc, kept exact where p_trunc is close to it: near the center p_trunc alone has lost the digits of the difference that the DF’s singular center is read in. Empty where not computed.

  • central_deficit (tuple of tuple of float) – For each component, (D_0, D_1, …): the central auxiliary density of the orbits the truncation removed as its Taylor series in the depth u, D_0 - D_1 u + D_2 u^2 - … (D_0 to D_4 as made; a record of D_0 and D_1 alone reads as the linear deficit). Empty where not recorded.

  • override_r (float) – Radii between which the closed-form profile replaced the rows of the solve; NaN where no row was replaced.

  • override_r_end (float) – Radii between which the closed-form profile replaced the rows of the solve; NaN where no row was replaced.

  • df_k (array, shape (n_components, m)) – The DF table of the model the profile belongs to: the energies, log10 F and its slope d log10 F / d ln K (NaN where not exact), as truncate made them. A component built on this profile (Method II) reads its sources from the integral of this DF. Empty where not recorded.

  • df_log_f (array, shape (n_components, m)) – The DF table of the model the profile belongs to: the energies, log10 F and its slope d log10 F / d ln K (NaN where not exact), as truncate made them. A component built on this profile (Method II) reads its sources from the integral of this DF. Empty where not recorded.

  • df_slope (array, shape (n_components, m)) – The DF table of the model the profile belongs to: the energies, log10 F and its slope d log10 F / d ln K (NaN where not exact), as truncate made them. A component built on this profile (Method II) reads its sources from the integral of this DF. Empty where not recorded.

  • df_taylor (tuple of tuple of float) – For each component, the model’s Taylor edge (F_1, F_2, K_switch): F = F_1 K + F_2 K^2 / 2 below K_switch. Empty where not recorded.

  • override_rows (int) – Number of rows replaced there.

  • override_rule (str) – What replaced them, "none" where nothing did.

  • origin (array of int8, shape (n,)) – Where each row comes from, a code of pianistpy.df.truncation.ORIGINS (the main solve, the edge continuation, the floor event, the fills, the closed-form outer rows, the edge row): the profile’s checks read each row’s tolerance from it. Empty where not recorded.

potential_log_slope(r)#

Return \(d\ln P / d\ln r\) at radii r, from the profile’s own representation.

The slope is exact in the rows’ quantities: \(dP/dr = -M/r^2\) (the Poisson equation in the profile’s unit), so \(d\ln P/d\ln r = -M/(r P)\) with mass and potential read as every consumer reads them. The knots of the representation are the rows r; between two rows the slope is smooth.

Parameters:

r (Any)

Return type:

Any

potential_log_slope_derivative(r)#

Return \(d(d\ln P/d\ln r)/dr\) at radii r, exact in the rows’ quantities.

With \(q = -M/(rP)\), \(dM/dr = r^2 \varrho\) (the total density of the components) and \(dP/dr = -M/r^2\): \(dq/dr = -(r^3 \varrho P - M P + M^2/r) / (r^2 P^2)\). Beyond the edge the density is zero and the same form holds on the Kepler potential.

Parameters:

r (Any)

Return type:

Any
