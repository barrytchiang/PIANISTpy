Density profiles#

Module pianistpy.profiles

Density profiles: the analytic input profile and the profile of a model.

A user builds the input profile of a component with DoublePowerLaw, for example pp.DoublePowerLaw.nfw(). The self-consistent profile of a model is a TruncatedProfile, read as model.profile. TruncatedProfile.interpolation_tables returns the ProfileTables of a TruncatedProfile.

The input profile is the double power law

\[\varrho(R) = C\,R^{-\gamma}\,(1 + R^{\alpha})^{(\gamma-\beta)/\alpha},\]

where \(R\) is the radius in units of the scale radius \(r_0\), \(\varrho\) is the density in internal units, \(\alpha\) is the sharpness of the transition between the inner and the outer slope, \(\beta\) is the outer logarithmic slope, \(\gamma\) is the inner logarithmic slope, and \(C\) is the normalization that sets the central relative potential \(P(0)\) to 1. Near the center, a model’s profile takes the depth \(P(0) - P(R)\) from the depth’s series in \(R^{\alpha}\), out to the largest radius at which the series’ next term is at most \(10^{-7}\) of the depth. The formulas are in Notes Section T:1.

class pianistpy.profiles.DoublePowerLaw(rho_alpha, rho_beta, rho_gamma)#

Double-power-law density profile, in internal units.

The profile is the input density of a Component. Its methods return the density, the enclosed mass and the relative potential at user-specified 3D radii, in closed form. The class methods nfw, hernquist and plummer return three parameter sets that have names.

Parameters:

  • rho_alpha (float) – Sharpness \(\alpha\) of the transition between the inner and the outer slope. Must be at least 1/2.

  • rho_beta (float) – Outer logarithmic slope \(\beta\). Must be greater than 2. A Component takes \(\beta \geq 3\) only.

  • rho_gamma (float) – Inner logarithmic slope \(\gamma\). Must satisfy \(0 \leq \gamma < 2\).

Raises:

InputError – If \(\alpha < 1/2\), \(\beta \leq 2\), \(\gamma < 0\) or \(\gamma \geq 2\).

See also

Component

A profile with its anisotropy, the input of a model.

TruncatedProfile

The self-consistent profile of a model.

Notes

In internal units the scale radius \(r_0\) is 1, the central relative potential \(P(0)\) is 1, and \(P(R)\) vanishes as \(R \to \infty\). The density is

\[\varrho(R) = C\,R^{-\gamma}\,(1 + R^{\alpha})^{(\gamma-\beta)/\alpha},\]

with \(R\) the radius in units of \(r_0\) and \(C\) the normalization rho_norm. The bound \(\gamma \geq 0\) excludes a density that decreases toward the center. The bound \(\gamma < 2\) keeps the central potential finite. The bound \(\beta > 2\) keeps the integral of \(\varrho R\) over \(R\) from 0 to infinity finite, so that \(P(0)\) is finite.

The profiles with names are parameter sets of the double-power-law profile. Where a profile with a name has a closed form for the profile’s mass or potential in elementary functions, mass and p evaluate the elementary closed form.

References

Chiang+(2026d), Eq. (5), defines the profile. Chiang+(2026d), Eq. (A6), gives \(C\). Chiang+(2026d), Eq. (A7), gives the enclosed mass.

>>> pp.DoublePowerLaw.hernquist().mass_total

mass(r)#

Return the enclosed mass \(\mathcal{M}(R)\).

Parameters:

r (array-like) – User-specified 3D radii \(R\), in internal units (units of the scale radius \(r_0\)).

Returns:

mass – Enclosed mass \(\mathcal{M}(R)\) in internal units, with the shape of r. The mass is the integral of \(\varrho R^2\) over \(R\) from 0 to r, that is, \(1/(4\pi)\) times the integral of the density over the volume.

Return type:

array

See also

mass_total

The total mass.

rho

The density.

p

The relative potential.

Notes

With \(z = R^\alpha/(1+R^\alpha)\) and the incomplete Beta function \(B(z; a, b)\),

\[\mathcal{M}(R) = \frac{C}{\alpha}\, B\!\left(z;\,\frac{3-\gamma}{\alpha},\,\frac{\beta-3}{\alpha}\right) .\]

For \(\beta = 3\) the second argument of \(B\) is zero, and the mass is evaluated from a logarithm and a series. For \(\beta < 3\) the second argument is negative, and the analytic continuation of the formula is evaluated in arbitrary precision.

References

Chiang+(2026d), Eq. (A7).

Examples

>>> float(pp.DoublePowerLaw.hernquist().mass(1.0))

depth_inner(r, terms=4)#

Return the depth \(u = P(0) - P(R)\) near the center, from its series.

The depth is summed term by term from its series in \(R^{\alpha}\), with no subtraction from \(P(0)\), so the depth keeps its relative precision where \(u\) is small. depth_inner also returns the size of the series’ next term relative to the depth, as an estimate of the truncation error of the sum.

Parameters:

  • r (array-like) – User-specified 3D radii \(R\), in internal units (units of the scale radius \(r_0\)).

  • terms (int, optional) – Number of terms of the series summed. Must be at least 1. Default: 4, which means the first four terms.

Returns:

  • u (array) – Depth \(u\) from the first terms terms, in internal units, with the shape of r. Infinite at \(R \geq 1\), where the series in \(R^{\alpha}\) does not converge.

  • bound (array) – Magnitude of the series’ next term divided by u, with the shape of r. Infinite where u is not positive or not finite.

Return type:

tuple[Any, Any]

See also

p

The relative potential in closed form.

Notes

The density term \(C\binom{e}{k}R^{-\gamma+k\alpha}\), with \(e = (\gamma-\beta)/\alpha\), contributes the term \(C\binom{e}{k}R^{2-\gamma+k\alpha}/[(2-\gamma+k\alpha)(3-\gamma+k\alpha)]\) to the depth.

References

Chiang+(2026d), Eq. (A11), gives the first two terms of the series.

Examples

>>> import pianistpy as pp
>>> u, bound = pp.DoublePowerLaw.hernquist().depth_inner([1e-3, 2.0])
>>> round(float(u[0]), 9)
0.000999001
>>> bool(bound[1] == float("inf"))
True

class pianistpy.profiles.ProfileTables(log_r, log_p, log_p_slope, log_r_all, log_mass, log_mass_slope, log_rho_coefficients, has_edge_row, r_last, p_last, rho_last, r_t, mass_t, edge_exponent)#

Arrays from which a JAX function evaluates a TruncatedProfile.

The arrays are the numbers from which TruncatedProfile.potential, TruncatedProfile.density, TruncatedProfile.mass and TruncatedProfile.radius evaluate the profile, laid out so that a JAX function takes them as arguments and is differentiable in them. A node is one tabulated radius of the profile. An interior node is any node other than the edge node of a spatially finite profile.

Parameters:

  • log_r (array, shape (n_interior,)) – \(\ln R\) at the interior nodes, with \(R\) the radius in internal units.

  • log_p (array, shape (n_interior,)) – \(\ln P\) at the interior nodes, with \(P\) the relative potential in internal units.

  • log_p_slope (array, shape (n_interior,)) – \(\mathrm{d}\ln P/\mathrm{d}\ln R\) at the interior nodes.

  • log_r_all (array, shape (n,)) – \(\ln R\) at every node.

  • log_mass (array, shape (n,)) – \(\ln \mathcal{M}\) at every node, with \(\mathcal{M}\) the enclosed mass in internal units.

  • log_mass_slope (array, shape (n,)) – \(\mathrm{d}\ln \mathcal{M}/\mathrm{d}\ln R\) at every node.

  • log_rho_coefficients (array, shape (n_components, 4, n_interior - 1)) – Coefficients of the cubic spline of \(\ln\varrho\) in \(\ln R\) for each component and each interval between interior nodes, highest power first, as polynomials in the offset of \(\ln R\) from the interval’s lower node.

  • has_edge_row (bool) – Whether the last node of the profile is its edge node.

  • r_last (float) – Radius of the last interior node, in internal units.

  • p_last (float) – Relative potential at the last interior node, in internal units.

  • rho_last (array, shape (n_components,)) – Density of each component at the last interior node, in internal units.

  • r_t (float) – Truncation radius \(R_{\rm t}\), in internal units. Infinite where has_edge_row is False.

  • mass_t (float) – Total mass \(\mathcal{M}_{\rm t}\), in internal units. Infinite where has_edge_row is False.

  • edge_exponent (array, shape (n_components,)) – Exponent \(q\) of each component’s density at the edge, \(\varrho \propto P^q\). Empty where the model has no edge.

See also

TruncatedProfile.interpolation_tables

The arrays of a profile.

class pianistpy.profiles.TruncatedProfile(r, p_trunc, rho_trunc, mass_trunc, k_t, r_t, mass_t, spatially_finite, source, source_anisotropy, model, edge_exponent, u_trunc=<factory>, central_deficit=(), override_r=nan, override_r_end=nan, override_rows=0, override_rule='none', origin=<factory>, df_k=<factory>, df_log_f=<factory>, df_slope=<factory>, df_taylor=(), model_level=None)#

Self-consistent profile of a model, tabulated at radii called nodes.

The profile of a model is model.profile. A component given the profile of a parent model and a new anisotropy is Method II of Chiang+(2026d), Appendix B2.2. The methods potential, density, mass and radius evaluate the profile between the nodes.

Two profiles are equal if the identity strings model of the two profiles are equal, because the identity of a model determines its profile.

Parameters:

  • r (array, shape (n,)) – Radius \(R\) of each node, in internal units. The radii are positive and strictly increasing, and there are at least two.

  • p_trunc (array, shape (n,)) – Total relative potential \(P\) of the model at each node, in internal units.

  • rho_trunc (array, shape (n_components, n)) – Density \(\varrho\) of each component at each node, in internal units.

  • mass_trunc (array, shape (n,)) – Total enclosed mass \(\mathcal{M}\) at each node, in internal units, the unit of DoublePowerLaw.mass, in which the total mass of the Hernquist profile is 1. \(4\pi\) times mass_trunc is the integral of the density over the volume.

  • k_t (float) – Truncation energy \(\mathcal{K}_{\rm t}\), in internal units.

  • r_t (float) – Truncation radius \(R_{\rm t}\), in internal units. Infinite where the model is not spatially finite.

  • mass_t (float) – Total mass \(\mathcal{M}_{\rm t}\), in internal units. Infinite where the model is not spatially finite.

  • spatially_finite (bool) – Whether the density reaches zero at a finite radius with a finite total mass. A spatially finite profile whose last node has p_trunc == 0 holds its edge as that last node, the edge node. The edge node’s density is zero and its radius is r_t.

  • source (DoublePowerLaw) – The analytic profile of the first component of the model. The scale radius and the central potential of source define the internal units of the arrays.

  • source_anisotropy (Anisotropy) – The anisotropy of the first component in the model the profile belongs to. A component built on the profile (Method II) reads its parent’s anisotropy from source_anisotropy.

  • model (str) – Identity string of the model the profile belongs to.

  • edge_exponent (tuple of float) – Exponent \(q\) of each component’s density at the edge of the model, \(\varrho \propto P^q\). Empty where the model is not spatially finite.

  • u_trunc (array, shape (n,), optional) – Depth \(u\), the central potential less the potential, at each node, in internal units. Near the center p_trunc has lost the digits of the depth, and u_trunc keeps them. Default: an empty array, which means the depth was not stored.

  • central_deficit (tuple of tuple of float, optional) – For each component, the coefficients \((D_0, D_1, \ldots, D_4)\) of the Taylor series \(D_0 - D_1 u + D_2 u^2 - \ldots\) in the depth \(u\) of the central augmented density of the orbits that the truncation removed. A record that holds only \(D_0\) and \(D_1\) is the linear form of the deficit. Default: (), which means the deficit was not recorded.

  • override_r (float, optional) – Inner radius of the range in which a closed form of the profile replaced the solution of the Poisson equation, in internal units. Default: NaN, which means no node was replaced.

  • override_r_end (float, optional) – Outer radius of the range in which a closed form replaced the solution of the Poisson equation, in internal units. Default: NaN, which means no node was replaced.

  • override_rows (int, optional) – Number of nodes that a closed form replaced. Default: 0, which means no node was replaced.

  • override_rule (str, optional) – Name of the closed form that replaced the nodes. Default: "none", which means no node was replaced.

  • origin (array of int8, shape (n,), optional) – Source of each node, as a code from 0 to 6. The code is 0 for the solution of the Poisson equation, 1 for the continuation of the Poisson solution to the edge, 2 for the radius at which the potential reaches zero, 3 for a node filled in at the inner end, 4 for a node filled in at the outer end, 5 for a closed-form outer node and 6 for the edge node. Default: an empty array, which means the sources were not recorded.

  • df_k (array, shape (n_components, m), optional) – Energies \(\mathcal{K}\) of the DF table of the model the profile belongs to, in internal units. A component built on the profile (Method II) computes its own DF from the parent’s DF table. Default: an empty array, which means the table was not recorded.

  • df_log_f (array, shape (n_components, m), optional) – \(\log_{10} F\) at the energies df_k, with \(F\) the DF in internal units. Default: an empty array, which means the table was not recorded.

  • df_slope (array, shape (n_components, m), optional) – \(\mathrm{d}\log_{10} F/\mathrm{d}\ln\mathcal{K}\) at the energies df_k. Default: an empty array, which means the table was not recorded.

  • df_taylor (tuple of tuple of float, optional) – For each component, the coefficients \((F_1, F_2, \mathcal{K}_{\rm s})\) of the model’s DF near the edge: \(F = F_1 \mathcal{K} + F_2 \mathcal{K}^2/2\) for \(\mathcal{K}\) below \(\mathcal{K}_{\rm s}\). Default: (), which means the coefficients were not recorded.

  • model_level (int or None, optional) – Level of the validation report of the model the profile belongs to (ValidationReport.level), set by the call that made the model. A component built on the profile (Method II) takes model_level as the level of its parent. Default: None, which means the level was not recorded and is read as level 0.

Raises:

  • ResultError – If the radii are fewer than two, not positive or not strictly increasing.

  • ResultError – If p_trunc or mass_trunc does not have one value per radius, or rho_trunc does not have the shape (n_components, n).

  • ResultError – If a spatially finite profile does not give the edge exponent of every component.

  • ResultError – If the edge node does not have zero density or does not lie at r_t.

See also

truncate

The call that makes a model and its profile.

Component

A component built on a profile (Method II).

DoublePowerLaw

The analytic profile of a component.

Notes

Between the nodes, \(\ln P\) and \(\ln \mathcal{M}\) are cubic polynomials in \(\ln R\) that match the values and the slopes at the two nodes of each interval. The slopes are exact, from the Poisson equation and the mass integral:

\[\frac{\mathrm{d}\ln P}{\mathrm{d}\ln R} = -\frac{\mathcal{M}}{R P}, \qquad \frac{\mathrm{d}\ln \mathcal{M}}{\mathrm{d}\ln R} = \frac{R^3\varrho}{\mathcal{M}},\]

with \(\varrho\) the total density. The logarithm of each component’s density is a cubic spline in \(\ln R\). On the last interval of a profile with an edge node, the potential is the Kepler form \(\mathcal{M}_{\rm t}(1/R - 1/R_{\rm t})\), and each component’s density is \(\varrho_L (P/P_L)^q\), with \(\varrho_L\) and \(P_L\) the values at the last interior node and \(q\) from edge_exponent. At and beyond \(R_{\rm t}\), the relative potential and the density are zero, and the enclosed mass is \(\mathcal{M}_{\rm t}\). Inside the first node, and beyond the last node of a profile without an edge node, \(P\) and \(\mathcal{M}\) continue as the power laws of the end nodes.

References

Notes Section T:1.3.

The Poisson equation \(\mathrm{d}P/\mathrm{d}R = -\mathcal{M}/R^2\) gives \(\mathrm{d}\ln P/\mathrm{d}\ln R = -\mathcal{M}/(R P)\). The slope is evaluated from mass and potential with no further approximation. The slope is continuous in \(R\) between the nodes.

ndarray

potential_log_slope_derivative(r)#

Return the derivative in \(R\) of the potential’s logarithmic slope.

With \(s = -\mathcal{M}/(R P)\) the logarithmic slope of the potential, \(\mathrm{d}\mathcal{M}/\mathrm{d}R = R^2 \varrho\) (\(\varrho\) the total density of the components) and \(\mathrm{d}P/\mathrm{d}R = -\mathcal{M}/R^2\), the derivative is

\[\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{\mathrm{d}R} = -\frac{R^3 \varrho P - \mathcal{M} P + \mathcal{M}^2/R}{R^2 P^2},\]

evaluated from mass, potential and density with no further approximation.

Parameters:

r (array-like) – User-specified 3D radii \(R\), positive, in internal units.

Returns:

derivative – \(\mathrm{d}s/\mathrm{d}R\) in internal units (inverse units of \(r_0\)), with the shape of r.

Return type:

ndarray

Raises:

ResultError – If the potential or a density at an interior node, or the enclosed mass at a node, is not positive.

See also

potential_log_slope

The logarithmic slope of the potential.
