Distribution functions and models#

Module pianistpy.df

Distribution functions and equilibrium models of spherical systems.

The pianistpy.df module computes the distribution function (DF) of a spherical density profile with a given velocity anisotropy, and the equilibrium model that the DF supports. A Component holds one density profile with its anisotropy. solve returns the model of a component. compute_df returns the untruncated DF of a component, and truncate builds the model from that DF, with or without a Truncation.

The DF of every anisotropy model of the package has the separable form

\[F(\mathcal{K}, \mathcal{L}) = F_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathcal{K})\, \mathcal{L}^{-2\beta_{\rm c}}\]

(Chiang+(2026d), Eq. (8)). \(\mathcal{K}\) is the energy-like variable in internal units, with \(0 \le \mathcal{K} \le 1\). \(\mathcal{K}\) is the relative energy \(Z\) for a constant anisotropy, and \(\mathcal{K}\) is \(\mathcal{Q}_\pm\) for an Osipkov-Merritt (OM) or Cuddeford-Osipkov-Merritt (COM) model. \(\mathcal{L}\) is the angular momentum in internal units, and \(\beta_{\rm c}\) is the central anisotropy. A truncation at the energy \(\mathcal{K}_{\rm t}\) (Method I of Chiang+(2026d)) gives the truncated DF

\[\hat{F}(\hat{\mathcal{K}}, \mathcal{L}) = F(\hat{\mathcal{K}} + \mathcal{K}_{\rm t}, \mathcal{L}) - F(\mathcal{K}_{\rm t}, \mathcal{L})\]

for \(\hat{\mathcal{K}} = \mathcal{K} - \mathcal{K}_{\rm t} \ge 0\), and \(\hat{F} = 0\) for \(\hat{\mathcal{K}} < 0\).

Each DF and each model carries a validation report. truncate and solve raise AccuracyError if a check of the model’s validation report lies above \(10^{-2}\) (the band “refused”), the round trip of the model’s DF against the model’s density included, unless the settings set write_anyway=True. compute_df, truncate and solve issue an AccuracyWarning if a check of the returned result’s validation report lies above \(10^{-4}\).

The derivation is in Chiang+(2026d), Section 3.1, and the numerical method in Notes Section T:2.

class pianistpy.df.Component(profile, anisotropy=<factory>, mass=1.0, name='component')#

One component of a model: a density profile with its velocity anisotropy.

A model is a sequence of components that share one total potential. A model of one component is a sequence of length one. compute_df and solve accept a model of one component.

A component whose profile is the TruncatedProfile of another model (parent.profile) is Method II of Chiang+(2026d). The Method II component has the density of the parent model in the parent model’s potential, with the component’s own anisotropy.

Parameters:

  • profile (DoublePowerLaw or TruncatedProfile) – Density profile of the component. A DoublePowerLaw is an analytic profile and needs an outer slope \(\beta \ge 3\). A TruncatedProfile must be the profile of a model of one component.

  • anisotropy (Anisotropy, optional) – Velocity anisotropy of the component. Default: Anisotropy(), which means an isotropic velocity distribution.

  • mass (float, optional) – Mass of the component in units of the mass of the first component. Must be positive. Default: 1.0, which means the mass of the first component.

  • name (str, optional) – Name of the component, carried by the component’s results and particles. Default: "component", which means the results and particles carry the name "component".

Raises:

  • InputError – If mass is not positive.

  • InputError – If the outer slope \(\beta\) of the analytic profile lies above 2 and below 3. The inversion assumes a relative potential \(P \propto 1/R\) at large radius \(R\), which holds for \(\beta \ge 3\) only.

  • InputError – If profile is the TruncatedProfile of a model of more than one component.

  • UnphysicalModelError – If profile is a TruncatedProfile with an edge and the central anisotropy \(\beta_{\rm c}\) is not above \(1/2 - q\), with \(q\) the exponent of the density at the edge, \(\varrho \propto \hat{P}^{\,q}\). No distribution function of the given anisotropy exists at the edge of such a profile.

See also

Truncation

The truncation of a model’s distribution function (Method I).

solve

The equilibrium model of a component.

The velocity anisotropy of a component.

References

Chiang+(2026d), Section 3.1 and Appendix B2.2.

class pianistpy.df.DistributionFunction(components, settings, identity, grid, table, form, k_t_min, validation, timing, inversion, units=Units(system='internal', concentration=None, mass_0_msun=None, r_0_pc=None), rows=None, source='inversion', function_name=None)#

Untruncated distribution functions of the components of a model.

compute_df and DistributionFunction.from_function return a DistributionFunction. An untruncated distribution function (DF) may be negative or non-monotonic, and the DistributionFunction states where. A DistributionFunction is not sampled or projected: truncate builds the Model that is.

Parameters:

  • components (tuple of Component) – Components to which the DFs belong.

  • settings (Settings) – Numerical settings with which the DFs were computed.

  • identity (str) – Identity string of the result.

  • grid (EnergyGrid) – Energies of the DF table and the radii at which the untruncated relative potential equals each energy.

  • table (DfTable) – The DF of each component at the energies of grid.

  • form (AsymptoticForm) – Analytic form of each DF below and above the energies of the DF table.

  • k_t_min (float) – Lowest admissible truncation energy \(\mathcal{K}_{\rm t}\), in internal units. k_t_min is zero where every untruncated DF is a valid equilibrium as it stands (is_physical). Otherwise k_t_min is the largest of the highest energy at which a DF is negative, the energy of a DF’s interior minimum and, for a Type II model, the relative potential \(P(R_{\rm a})\) at the anisotropy radius \(R_{\rm a}\). Where \(P(R_{\rm a})\) is the largest of these energies, k_t_min is the next double above \(P(R_{\rm a})\). truncate also checks that the edge of a truncated Type II model lies inside \(R_{\rm a}\).

  • validation (ValidationReport) – Validation report of the DFs.

  • timing (dict of str to float) – Elapsed time of each step of the computation, in seconds.

  • inversion (InversionRecord or None) – Record of how the inversion made the DF table. None where the DF table has a closed form. Every DistributionFunction from compute_df holds an InversionRecord.

  • units (Units, optional) – Unit system in which the result is presented (to). Default: Units(), which means internal units.

  • rows (InversionRows or None, optional) – Values of the inversion at every energy of the inversion’s grid, which truncate reads. Default: None, which means that the DF table was not made by an inversion.

  • source ({"inversion", "function"}, optional) – Origin of the DF table’s values: "inversion" (compute_df) or "function" (DistributionFunction.from_function). Default: "inversion".

  • function_name (str or None, optional) – The __name__ of the function from which the DF table was tabulated. Default: None, which means that the DF table comes from an inversion.

See also

compute_df

The untruncated DF of a component.

truncate

The equilibrium model built from a DistributionFunction.

classmethod from_function(component, f, settings=None)#

Return the distribution function that a user-specified function gives.

The user-specified function is a distribution function (DF) of the energy, such as a fitting function. The package tabulates the function on the energy grid that compute_df uses for the given component and settings, and validates the tabulated DF against the component’s density. Like a DistributionFunction from compute_df, the result holds analytic forms below and above the DF table, a validation report and an identity string, and DistributionFunction.write writes the result to an HDF5 file. The result stores the DF table and does not store the function.

Parameters:

  • component (Component) – The component to which the DF belongs. The component’s profile must be an analytic profile (a DoublePowerLaw).

  • f (callable) – Function f(k, depth) of two arrays of equal shape: the energies \(\mathcal{K}\) of the DF table in internal units, and the depth \(P_0 - \mathcal{K}\) at each energy, with \(P_0 = 1\) the central relative potential. f returns the DF \(F\) (not its logarithm) at each energy, as an array of the shape of k. The package passes depth as 1 - k in double precision, which is exact for every energy \(\mathcal{K} \ge 1/2\). A function of \(\mathcal{K}\) alone may ignore depth.

  • settings (Settings, optional) – Numerical settings. Default: None, which means Settings(), the default settings.

Returns:

df – The tabulated DF. The attribute source is "function" and the attribute function_name is the __name__ of f. The DF table has no column of the derivative of the DF, and the check slope_column of the validation report states that the derivative is not judged. The result holds no inversion record and no inversion values, so truncate does not accept it.

Return type:

DistributionFunction

Raises:

  • InputError – If f is not callable, returns an array whose shape differs from the shape of k, or returns a value that is not finite at an energy of the grid. For a value that is not finite, the message states the first energy with such a value.

  • InputError – If the component is built on a TruncatedProfile.

Warns:

AccuracyWarning – If the DF is non-negative on its table and a check of the DF’s validation report lies above \(10^{-4}\).

See also

compute_df

The DF computed by inversion of the density.

Whether the DF is a valid equilibrium as it stands.

property is_physical: bool#

Whether the untruncated DFs are a valid equilibrium without a truncation.

is_physical is true if k_t_min is zero.

property diverges_at_zero: tuple[bool, ...]#

Whether the DF of each component diverges as the energy goes to zero.

A DF that goes as \(\mathcal{K}^p\) with \(-1 < p < 0\) as the energy \(\mathcal{K}\) goes to zero is integrable and gives a finite density, but such a DF is not monotonic.

to(system, concentration=None, mass_0=None, r_0=None, r_vir=None)#

Return the distribution function presented in another unit system.

The values of the result are not recomputed and stay in internal units. A quantity computed from the returned result is presented in the returned result’s unit system.

Parameters:

  • system ({"internal", "model", "physical"}) – Target unit system: "internal" (\(G = \rho_0/C = r_0 = 1\)), "model" (\(G = M_0 = r_0 = 1\)) or "physical" (Astropy quantities).

  • concentration (float, optional) – Concentration \(c\), positive. The mass unit \(M_0\) is the mass of the untruncated profile inside \(c\,r_0\). The model and the physical systems need concentration. Default: None, which means that no concentration is given.

  • mass_0 (float or Quantity, optional) – Mass unit \(M_0\), positive, for the physical system. A float is in solar masses, and a Quantity of any mass unit is accepted. Default: None, which means that no mass unit is given.

  • r_0 (float or Quantity, optional) – Scale radius \(r_0\), positive, for the physical system. A float is in parsecs, and a Quantity of any length unit is accepted. Default: None, which means that r_vir gives the length scale.

  • r_vir (float or Quantity, optional) – Concentration radius \(c\,r_0\), positive, given in place of r_0 for the physical system. A float is in parsecs, and a Quantity of any length unit is accepted. Default: None, which means that r_0 gives the length scale.

Returns:

df – The DF with unchanged values, with the attribute units set to the target unit system.

Return type:

DistributionFunction

Raises:

InputError – If system is not "internal", "model" or "physical", if a scale that system needs is missing, if a scale is not positive, or if both r_0 and r_vir are given.

See also

write_text(path, overwrite=False)#

Write the DF table to a fixed-width text file.

The text file is an export for reading outside the package, and pianistpy.read does not read it. The file holds one line per energy of each component’s support, with the columns k (the energy \(\mathcal{K}\) in internal units, in which the central relative potential of the untruncated profile is 1), log10_df (the base-10 logarithm of the DF) and dlog10_df_dlnk (the slope \({\rm d}\log_{10} F / {\rm d}\ln \mathcal{K}\)). A model of more than one component has a fourth column, component, with the index of the component. The header states the result, the result’s components and the units. The written file is read back and compared with the written values, to the digits written.

Parameters:

  • path (str or path-like) – Path of the text file to write.

  • overwrite (bool, optional) – Whether to replace an existing file at path. Default: False, which means that an existing file is kept and InputError is raised.

Raises:

  • InputError – If a file exists at path and overwrite is false.

  • ResultError – If the written file does not read back as written.

Return type:

None

class pianistpy.df.Model(components, truncation, settings, identity, parent, table, form, profile, validation, timing, r_trust, inversion, units=Units(system='internal', concentration=None, mass_0_msun=None, r_0_pc=None))#

An equilibrium model of one or more components, truncated or untruncated.

truncate and solve return a Model. The distribution function (DF) of every component of a model is non-negative. A DF that diverges as the energy goes to zero is not monotonic (diverges_at_zero). Whether the model’s density reaches zero at a finite radius is the property spatially_finite.

Parameters:

  • components (tuple of Component) – Components of the model.

  • truncation (Truncation or None) – Truncation of the model. None for an untruncated model and for a model whose component is built on a TruncatedProfile.

  • settings (Settings) – Numerical settings with which the model was computed.

  • identity (str) – Identity string of the result.

  • parent (str) – Identity string of the DistributionFunction from which the model was built.

  • table (DfTable) – The DF of each component of the model.

  • form (AsymptoticForm) – Analytic form of each DF below and above the energies of the DF table.

  • profile (TruncatedProfile) – Self-consistent density, mass and relative potential of the model.

  • validation (ValidationReport) – Validation report of the model.

  • timing (dict of str to float) – Elapsed time of each step of the computation, in seconds.

  • r_trust (mapping of str to float) – Radius, in internal units, inside which the model’s values stay within the bound of a tier of the validation report, keyed by the tier’s name. The key "silent" holds the radius for a relative error of at most \(10^{-6}\), and the key "recorded" the radius for a relative error of at most \(10^{-4}\) (Accuracy tables). Each radius states how far out the model meets the tier’s bound, and is not itself an error of the model. On an untruncated model of an analytic profile, both radii are infinite. On every other model that truncate and solve return, the mapping is empty. A model read from a file holds the radii that the file records.

  • inversion (InversionRecord or None) – Record of how the inversion made the model’s DF table. None where the DF table has a closed form. Every Model from solve holds an InversionRecord.

  • units (Units, optional) – Unit system in which the model is presented (to). Default: Units(), which means internal units.

See also

class pianistpy.df.Truncation(k_t=None, mass_t=None, r_t=None, concentration=None)#

Truncation of a model’s distribution function (Method I).

A truncation shifts the distribution function (DF) in energy by the truncation energy \(\mathcal{K}_{\rm t}\) and lowers the DF by the DF’s value at \(\mathcal{K}_{\rm t}\), which gives a model of finite mass and finite radius (Method I of Chiang+(2026d)). The truncation is given by exactly one of three inputs: the truncation energy k_t, a target mass mass_t or a target radius r_t. A target mass is given together with the concentration that defines the unit of the target mass. Method II needs no Truncation: a Method II model is a Component whose profile is the TruncatedProfile of another model.

Parameters:

  • k_t (float, optional) – Truncation energy \(\mathcal{K}_{\rm t}\), positive, in internal units, in which the central relative potential of the untruncated profile is 1. Default: None, which means that mass_t or r_t is given.

  • mass_t (float, optional) – Target total mass of the truncated model, positive, in units of \(M_0\), the mass of the untruncated profile inside the radius \(c\,r_0\) (model units). A value of 1 is the virial mass. mass_t is the one input of Truncation that is not in internal units. The truncation energy is solved for. mass_t requires concentration. Default: None, which means that k_t or r_t is given.

  • r_t (float, optional) – Target truncation radius \(R_{\rm t}\), positive, in units of the scale radius \(r_0\), the unit of length of the internal and the model systems. The truncation energy is solved for. Default: None, which means that k_t or mass_t is given.

  • concentration (float, optional) – Concentration \(c\) of the first component, positive. The mass unit \(M_0\) of mass_t is the mass of the untruncated profile inside \(c\,r_0\). concentration is given with mass_t only, since k_t and r_t do not depend on the concentration. Default: None, which means that no target mass is given.

Raises:

  • InputError – If not exactly one of k_t, mass_t and r_t is given, or if the one given is not positive.

  • InputError – If mass_t is given without concentration, if concentration is given without mass_t, or if concentration is not positive.

See also

truncate

The model that a truncation gives.

solve

The model of a component, with or without a truncation.

Component

A component on the TruncatedProfile of another model (Method II).

Notes

The truncated DF is \(\hat{F}(\hat{\mathcal{K}}, \mathcal{L}) = F(\hat{\mathcal{K}} + \mathcal{K}_{\rm t}, \mathcal{L}) - F(\mathcal{K}_{\rm t}, \mathcal{L})\) for \(\hat{\mathcal{K}} = \mathcal{K} - \mathcal{K}_{\rm t} \ge 0\), and \(\hat{F} = 0\) for \(\hat{\mathcal{K}} < 0\), with \(F\) the untruncated DF, \(\mathcal{K}\) the energy-like variable and \(\mathcal{L}\) the angular momentum.

References

Chiang+(2026d), Eq. (9). Notes Section T:3.6.

pianistpy.df.compute_df(components, settings=None)#

Return the untruncated distribution function of a component.

The distribution function (DF) is returned whatever its sign. The result states where the DF is negative, and the lowest truncation energy \(\mathcal{K}_{\rm t}\) that gives a valid equilibrium. Call solve for the model itself. Call compute_df to inspect the DF before choosing a truncation, or to truncate one DF in several ways.

Parameters:

  • components (Component or sequence of Component) – The component of the model, or a sequence that holds exactly one Component.

  • settings (Settings, optional) – Numerical settings. Default: None, which means Settings(), the default settings.

Returns:

df – The DF of the component, tabulated on an energy grid, with its analytic forms below and above the energies of the table, the lowest admissible truncation energy k_t_min in internal units, and the validation report of the DF.

Return type:

DistributionFunction

Raises:

  • InputError – If components is an empty sequence, a sequence with an item that is not a Component, or a sequence of more than one component.

  • InputError – If the component is built on a TruncatedProfile whose model has an anisotropy other than isotropic or Osipkov-Merritt Type I, or whose model carries no DF.

  • InputError – If the component is built on a TruncatedProfile, has a Type II anisotropy, and the edge of the TruncatedProfile does not lie inside the component’s anisotropy radius \(R_{\rm a}\).

  • InputError – If the component is built on a TruncatedProfile and the component’s central anisotropy \(\beta_{\rm c}\) is below \(-1\) and is not \(-3/2\).

  • UnphysicalModelError – If the component has a Cuddeford-Osipkov-Merritt (COM) Type II anisotropy and the integrand of the DF’s inversion is not integrable at the anisotropy radius \(R_{\rm a}\).

  • UnphysicalModelError – If the component is built on a TruncatedProfile and the central density slope \(\gamma\) is below \(2\beta_{\rm c}\).

  • UnphysicalModelError – If the component is built on the TruncatedProfile of a model that was truncated at \(\mathcal{K}_{\rm t} > 0\) but has no edge.

  • AccuracyError – If the inversion cannot resolve the DF at the resolution of settings. Settings(write_anyway=True) does not turn this error into a warning.

Warns:

AccuracyWarning – If the DF is non-negative on its table and a check of the DF’s validation report lies above \(10^{-4}\).

See also

solve

truncate

The equilibrium model built from a distribution function.

Notes

The DF has the separable form \(F(\mathcal{K}, \mathcal{L}) = F_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathcal{K})\, \mathcal{L}^{-2\beta_{\rm c}}\), with \(\mathcal{K}\) the energy-like variable and \(\mathcal{L}\) the angular momentum, both in internal units. The result tabulates \(F_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathcal{K})\).

pianistpy.df.truncate(df, truncation=None)#

Return the equilibrium model built from a distribution function.

With a Truncation, the model’s DF is the untruncated DF shifted in energy by the truncation energy \(\mathcal{K}_{\rm t}\) and lowered by the untruncated DF’s value at \(\mathcal{K}_{\rm t}\), which gives a model of finite mass and finite radius (Method I of Chiang+(2026d)). Without a truncation, the untruncated DF is the model’s DF. An untruncated DF is a valid equilibrium only if DistributionFunction.is_physical is true.

Parameters:

  • df (DistributionFunction) – The untruncated DF, as compute_df returns it.

  • truncation (Truncation, optional) – The truncation. Default: None, which means the untruncated model. For a component built on a TruncatedProfile, None means the model that ends at the edge of that TruncatedProfile.

Returns:

model – The equilibrium model: its non-negative DF, its self-consistent density and potential (a TruncatedProfile), and its validation report.

Return type:

Model

Raises:

  • InputError – If df was built by DistributionFunction.from_function. Such a DF holds the function’s values only, and a truncation needs the values of the inversion.

  • UnphysicalModelError – If the truncation energy \(\mathcal{K}_{\rm t}\) is below df.k_t_min, or no admissible truncation energy reaches the target mass or the target radius of truncation.

  • UnphysicalModelError – If the central density slope \(\gamma\) is below \(2\beta_{\rm c}(0)\), with \(\beta_{\rm c}(0)\) the central anisotropy (0 for the isotropic and Osipkov-Merritt models), or if the DF is negative or tends to minus infinity at the center.

  • UnphysicalModelError – If \(\gamma = 2\beta_{\rm c}(0)\) and the DF has no closed form at the center to join the table of the DF.

  • UnphysicalModelError – If the truncated model has no edge, or the edge of a Type II model lies at or beyond the anisotropy radius \(R_{\rm a}\).

  • UnphysicalModelError – If truncation is None and the untruncated DF is not a valid equilibrium (df.is_physical is false), or the untruncated mass diverges.

  • UnphysicalModelError – If the component is built on a TruncatedProfile and the DF is negative at an energy inside the DF’s support.

  • AccuracyError – If a check of the model’s validation report lies above \(10^{-2}\) (the band “refused”), the round trip of the model’s DF against the model’s density included. With Settings(write_anyway=True) in the settings of df, the model is returned with an AccuracyWarning instead.

  • AccuracyError – If the truncated DF, or the potential solved from the truncated DF, is not finite at the resolution of the settings. write_anyway=True does not turn this error into a warning.

Warns:

AccuracyWarning – If a check of the model’s validation report lies above \(10^{-4}\).

See also

compute_df

The untruncated distribution function.

solve

compute_df and truncate in one call.

Truncation

The truncation energy, target mass or target radius.

Notes

An UnphysicalModelError raised by truncate holds a result in its result attribute. The result attribute holds the model without an edge where the truncation gives no edge, and df otherwise.

The truncated DF is

\[\hat{F}(\hat{\mathcal{K}}, \mathcal{L}) = F(\hat{\mathcal{K}} + \mathcal{K}_{\rm t}, \mathcal{L}) - F(\mathcal{K}_{\rm t}, \mathcal{L})\]

for \(\hat{\mathcal{K}} = \mathcal{K} - \mathcal{K}_{\rm t} \ge 0\), and \(\hat{F} = 0\) for \(\hat{\mathcal{K}} < 0\). Here \(F\) is the untruncated DF, \(\mathcal{K}\) the energy-like variable and \(\mathcal{L}\) the angular momentum, all in internal units. The truncated relative potential \(\hat{P}(R)\) solves Poisson’s equation with the density of \(\hat{F}\), from \(\hat{P}(0) = 1 - \mathcal{K}_{\rm t}\) to the truncation radius \(R_{\rm t}\), where \(\hat{P}(R_{\rm t}) = 0\).

References

Chiang+(2026d), Eqs. (9), (10) and (11). Notes Section T:3.

Examples

>>> df = pp.compute_df(pp.Component(pp.DoublePowerLaw.hernquist()))
>>> model = pp.truncate(df, pp.Truncation(k_t=0.05))

True

Module pianistpy.df.gradient

JAX functions that return the tabulated distribution function in terms of the anisotropy parameters, and the Jacobians of those functions (End-to-end differentiability).

Derivatives of a component’s distribution function in its anisotropy.

This module returns the distribution function (DF) \(F(Q)\) that compute_df makes for a component, at fixed relative energies \(Q\), together with the derivatives of \(F\) in the anisotropy parameters \(\theta = (\beta_{\rm c}, R_{\rm a})\). inversion_nodes returns the energies at which compute_df evaluates \(F\). rows_jacobian returns \(F\) and its derivatives at the energies of inversion_nodes as arrays, and table_jacobian returns every array of the DF’s table and its derivatives. make_inversion_rows returns \(F\), and make_inversion_table returns the arrays of the DF’s table, as JAX functions of \(\theta\), which jax.grad and jax.vjp differentiate.

The DF is \(F = T_b[g]\). Here \(b = \beta_{\rm c}\) is the central anisotropy. The source \(g\) is the set of derivatives \({\rm d}^N \varrho_{\rm aux} / {\rm d}P^N\) of the auxiliary density \(\varrho_{\rm aux}\) in the relative potential \(P\). The operator \(T_b\) is the inversion of the auxiliary density at \(\beta_{\rm c} = b\), and \(T_b\) is linear in \(g\). The derivatives of \(F\) are

\[\frac{\partial F}{\partial R_{\rm a}} = T_b\left[\partial_{R_{\rm a}} g\right], \qquad \frac{\partial F}{\partial \beta_{\rm c}} = T_b\left[\partial_b g\right] + E_b[g].\]

The derivative \(\partial_\theta g\) of the source in \(\theta\) at a fixed \(P\) is in closed form. The term \(E_b[g]\) is the derivative of the operator \(T_b\) in \(b\) at a fixed source \(g\). \(E_b[g]\) has no closed form and is taken by finite differences of \(T_b[g]\) in \(b\). The record returned with each derivative in \(\beta_{\rm c}\) states the residual of the finite differences in \(b\), for comparison with the accuracy bounds \(10^{-6}\), \(10^{-4}\) and \(10^{-2}\) of the package’s validation reports.

The derivative of the DF’s table follows by the chain rule through each array of the table. The energies and every discrete choice of the table are held at the parameters the call is given. For a component on a TruncatedProfile (Method II), the profile is held, and the derivative of the source is the derivative of the inverse anisotropy factor in closed form.

The derivatives are available for constant anisotropy (\(\beta_{\rm c}\)), Cuddeford-Osipkov-Merritt (COM) Type I (\(\beta_{\rm c}\) and \(R_{\rm a}\)), and Osipkov-Merritt Type I and Type II (\(R_{\rm a}\)). The derivatives are not available for COM Type II, whose interior point at the potential \(P(R_{\rm a})\) moves with both parameters. The derivatives are not available for a DF whose center lies on the critical branch, where the inner slope \(\gamma\) of the density equals \(2\beta_{\rm c}\) and the form of the center changes with \(\beta_{\rm c}\). The derivation is Notes Eq. (T:7.1).

pianistpy.df.gradient.make_inversion_rows(component, settings, q=None)#

Return the DF at fixed energies as a JAX function of the anisotropy.

The returned function maps the anisotropy parameters \(\theta\) to the DF \(F(Q)\) that compute_df makes at the relative energies q. The reverse-mode derivative of the returned function at each \(\theta\) is the derivative that rows_jacobian returns at that \(\theta\). For the derivatives as arrays, call rows_jacobian instead.

Parameters:

  • component (Component) – Component on an analytic profile (not a TruncatedProfile), with constant, isotropic, Osipkov-Merritt Type I or Type II, or COM Type I anisotropy. The anisotropy parameters of component are the point at which the steps of the finite differences in \(\beta_{\rm c}\) are measured.

  • settings (Settings) – Numerical settings, the same as those given to compute_df.

  • q (array-like, shape (n,), optional) – Relative energies \(Q\), increasing, in internal units. Default: None, which means the energies that inversion_nodes returns for component and settings.

Returns:

  • rows (callable) – Function rows(theta). The argument theta is an array of the parameter values in the order of params. The value is a JAX array of shape (n,) that holds \(F(Q)\) at q, in internal units. jax.grad and jax.vjp differentiate rows.

  • params (tuple of str) – Names of the anisotropy parameters in theta: ("beta_c",), ("r_a",) or ("beta_c", "r_a").

  • q (ndarray, shape (n,)) – Relative energies \(Q\), in internal units: q as given, or the energies of inversion_nodes.

Raises:

  • InputError – If the anisotropy is COM Type II.

  • InputError – If q is None and the energies that settings places include two energies that float64 cannot separate.

  • ResultError – If q is None, the component is Osipkov-Merritt Type II or COM, and the coarse inversion that places the energies finds a derivative of the auxiliary density that is not finite.

Return type:

tuple[Any, tuple[str, …], ndarray]

See also

rows_jacobian

The DF at fixed energies and its derivatives as arrays.

make_inversion_table

Every array of the DF’s table as a JAX function.

inversion_nodes

The energies at which compute_df evaluates the DF.

Notes

Each call of rows inverts the component again at the new parameters, at the energies q. Each derivative of rows calls rows_jacobian, which raises InputError for a component on a TruncatedProfile.

The steps of the finite differences in \(\beta_{\rm c}\) are measured once, at the parameters of component, and are used at every theta. The derivative at one theta therefore does not depend on the values of theta in earlier calls.

pianistpy.df.gradient.make_inversion_table(component, settings, q=None)#

Return the arrays of the DF’s table as a JAX function of the anisotropy.

The returned function maps the anisotropy parameters \(\theta\) to the arrays of the table that compute_df makes. The energies of the table and the set of its arrays are held at the component’s own parameters. The other discrete choices of the table, the anchor of the form at the center and the nodes kept, are made again at each \(\theta\), except that a component on a TruncatedProfile also holds the nodes kept at the component’s own parameters. The reverse-mode derivative of the returned function at each \(\theta\) is the derivative that table_jacobian returns at that \(\theta\). For the derivatives as arrays, call table_jacobian instead.

Parameters:

  • component (Component) – Component with constant, isotropic, Osipkov-Merritt Type I or Type II, or COM Type I anisotropy, on an analytic profile or on a TruncatedProfile (Method II).

  • settings (Settings) – Numerical settings, the same as those given to compute_df.

  • q (array-like, shape (n,), optional) – Relative energies \(Q\), increasing, in internal units, for a component on an analytic profile. Default: None, which means the energies that inversion_nodes returns for component and settings. A component on a TruncatedProfile takes the energies of its own solve, and q is not used.

Returns:

  • table (callable) – Function table(theta). The argument theta is an array of the parameter values in the order of params. The value is a dict of JAX arrays with every key of table_jacobian’s values except the energies k and k_below. jax.grad and jax.vjp differentiate table.

  • params (tuple of str) – Names of the anisotropy parameters in theta: ("beta_c",), ("r_a",) or ("beta_c", "r_a").

  • values (dict of ndarray) – Arrays of the table at the component’s own parameters, energies included, as table_jacobian returns them.

Raises:

  • InputError – If the anisotropy is COM Type II, or the DF’s center lies on a branch that table_jacobian rejects.

  • InputError – If component is on a TruncatedProfile that Method II does not invert, as in table_jacobian.

  • AccuracyError – If a value of the DF inside the table is not finite, is 0 or exceeds \(10^{300}\), or the tail’s logarithmic exponent below the floor is not finite.

  • UnphysicalModelError – If component is on a TruncatedProfile whose model is truncated but not spatially finite.

  • ResultError – If a derivative of the auxiliary density is not finite at the energies, or if the DF of a component on a TruncatedProfile has a defective band at the top of its energies.

Return type:

tuple[Any, tuple[str, …], dict[str, ndarray]]

See also

Each derivative of table calls table_jacobian. The steps of the finite differences in \(\beta_{\rm c}\) are measured once, at the component’s own parameters, and are used at every theta.

pianistpy.df.gradient.rows_jacobian(component, settings, q=None, steps=None)#

Return the DF at fixed energies and its derivatives in the anisotropy.

The values are the DF \(F(Q)\) that compute_df makes, at the relative energies q. The derivatives are those of \(F\) in the component’s anisotropy parameters at fixed q. For the derivatives of every array of the DF’s table (the nodes below the table, the form at the center and the tail), call table_jacobian instead.

Parameters:

  • component (Component) – Component on an analytic profile (not a TruncatedProfile), with constant, isotropic, Osipkov-Merritt Type I or Type II, or COM Type I anisotropy.

  • settings (Settings) – Numerical settings, the same as those given to compute_df.

  • q (array-like, shape (n,), optional) – Relative energies \(Q\), increasing, in internal units. Default: None, which means the energies that inversion_nodes returns for component and settings.

  • steps (dict, optional) – Dictionary that keeps the steps of the finite differences in \(\beta_{\rm c}\) between calls. A call reads the steps from steps where steps holds steps for the call’s profile, anisotropy family, anisotropy kind, settings and form of the inversion. A call otherwise measures the steps and writes them into steps. Passing one dictionary to a sequence of calls makes every later call use the steps that the first call measured. Default: None, which means each call measures its own steps and keeps them nowhere.

Returns:

  • q (ndarray, shape (n,)) – Relative energies \(Q\), in internal units: q as given, or the energies of inversion_nodes.

  • f (ndarray, shape (n,)) – DF \(F(Q)\) at q, as compute_df makes it, in internal units.

  • jac (dict of ndarray) – Derivatives of \(F\) at q, each with the shape of q, in internal units. The key "beta_c" holds \(\partial F/\partial\beta_{\rm c}\) and the key "r_a" holds \(\partial F/\partial R_{\rm a}\), for the parameters that the anisotropy family has.

  • record (dict) – Description of the finite differences in \(\beta_{\rm c}\). The record is an empty dict for an Osipkov-Merritt component, which has no parameter \(\beta_{\rm c}\). The entry "exponent" is "central" where the form of the inversion on one side of \(\beta_{\rm c}\) equals the form on the other side, and "one-sided" at a \(\beta_{\rm c}\) where the form changes (such as \(\beta_{\rm c} = 0\) or a half-integer \(\beta_{\rm c}\)). The entry "steps" holds the steps, "pilots" the trial steps from which the steps were derived, "regions" the widths in \(\beta_{\rm c}\) over which the form does not change, and "measured" whether the call measured the steps instead of reading them from steps. The entry "accuracy" is the inversion’s own accuracy, the change of the operator’s values when the number of quadrature nodes doubles. The entry "residual" is the Richardson residual \(\max|E(d) - E(d/2)|\) of the finite differences at the step \(d\). A one-sided record also holds "sides", the number of sides (2), and "spread", the largest difference between the derivatives from the two sides. The entries "accuracy", "residual" and "spread" are relative to \(\max|\partial F/\partial\beta_{\rm c}|\) over q, so the relative error of an entry of \(\partial F/\partial\beta_{\rm c}\) far below that maximum can exceed "residual".

Raises:

  • InputError – If component is on a TruncatedProfile.

  • InputError – If the anisotropy is COM Type II, whose interior point at the potential \(P(R_{\rm a})\) moves with both parameters.

  • ResultError – If a derivative of the auxiliary density is not finite at q.

Return type:

tuple[ndarray, ndarray, dict[str, ndarray], dict[str, Any]]

See also

inversion_nodes

A call inverts the component once for \(F\) and once per parameter. In \(\beta_{\rm c}\), a call also inverts the component at every point of the finite differences, at settings and at twice the quadrature nodes of settings.

pianistpy.df.gradient.table_jacobian(component, settings, q=None, steps=None)#

Return the arrays of the DF’s table and their derivatives in the anisotropy.

The values are the arrays of the table that compute_df makes for the component. The derivatives are those of each array in the component’s anisotropy parameters, with the energies and every discrete choice of the table held at the component’s own parameters. The discrete choices are the placement of the energies, the form of the inversion, the anchor of the form at the center and the nodes kept. For the DF at the energies of the inversion alone, call rows_jacobian instead.

Parameters:

  • component (Component) – Component with constant, isotropic, Osipkov-Merritt Type I or Type II, or COM Type I anisotropy, on an analytic profile or on a TruncatedProfile (Method II).

  • settings (Settings) – Numerical settings, the same as those given to compute_df.

  • q (array-like, shape (n,), optional) – Relative energies \(Q\), increasing, in internal units, for a component on an analytic profile. Default: None, which means the energies that inversion_nodes returns for component and settings. A component on a TruncatedProfile takes the energies of its own solve and accepts only None.

  • steps (dict, optional) – Dictionary that keeps the steps of the finite differences in \(\beta_{\rm c}\) between calls, as in rows_jacobian. Default: None, which means each call measures its own steps and keeps them nowhere.

Returns:

  • values (dict of ndarray) – Arrays of the table, in internal units. For a component on an analytic profile, k holds the energies of the table, log_df the base-10 logarithm of the DF at k, and log_df_slope the slope \({\rm d}\log_{10} F/{\rm d}\ln Q\) at k. Where the table has nodes below its first energy, k_below, log_df_below and log_df_slope_below hold the energy, the logarithm of the DF and the slope at those nodes. Where the table has a closed form above its anchor, \(F = a_{\rm anchor} (u/u_{\rm anchor})^\kappa + b + d\,u\) in the depth \(u = 1 - Q\), central holds the array \((\kappa, a_{\rm anchor}, b, d)\). Where the table has a tail below its lowest node, tail holds the tail’s exponent, amplitude and logarithmic exponent. For a component on a TruncatedProfile, the keys are k, log_df, edge_law (the power and the two coefficients of the edge law below the floor, where the component has one) and center (the exponent and the amplitude of the center’s closed form, and the next term’s exponent and coefficient where the center’s form states them).

  • jac (dict of dict of ndarray) – Derivatives jac[name][key] of values[key] in the parameter name ("beta_c" or "r_a"), for every key of values except the energies k and k_below, each with the shape of values[key]. Where a Type II DF is outside its support, the derivatives of log_df and log_df_slope are 0.

  • record (dict) – Description of the finite differences in \(\beta_{\rm c}\), with the entries of the record of rows_jacobian in absolute values, not relative ones. For a component on an analytic profile, record["rows"] describes the finite differences of the DF and its slope at the energies of the inversion, and record["below"], where present, describes the finite differences at the nodes below the table. For a component on a TruncatedProfile, record["rows"] describes the finite differences of the DF, and record["grid"] states the number of energies, the number of energies kept, and the energy around which the energies are placed.

Raises:

  • InputError – If the anisotropy is COM Type II, whose interior point at the potential \(P(R_{\rm a})\) moves with both parameters.

  • InputError – If component is on an analytic profile and the DF’s center lies on the critical branch, where the inner slope \(\gamma\) of the density equals \(2\beta_{\rm c}\), with a divergent center of a Type I model or with a closed form above the anchor.

  • InputError – If component is on a TruncatedProfile and the DF’s center is not on the regular branch \(\gamma > 2\beta_{\rm c}\), or the solve of component finds the center in the band of \(\beta_{\rm c}\) around the critical branch.

  • InputError – If q is given for a component on a TruncatedProfile.

  • InputError – If component is on a TruncatedProfile that Method II does not invert. Method II does not invert a profile that carries no DF of its own model, a profile whose model’s anisotropy is neither isotropic nor Osipkov-Merritt Type I, a component whose \(\beta_{\rm c}\) needs sources of an order above 3, or a Type II component whose \(R_{\rm a}\) does not lie outside the profile’s edge.

  • AccuracyError – If a value of the DF inside the table is not finite or is 0, or exceeds \(10^{300}\) where no closed form covers the center.

  • AccuracyError – If the tail’s logarithmic exponent below the floor is not finite.

  • UnphysicalModelError – If component is on a TruncatedProfile whose model is truncated but not spatially finite.

  • ResultError – If a derivative of the auxiliary density is not finite at the energies, or if the DF of a component on a TruncatedProfile has a defective band at the top of its energies.

Return type:

tuple[dict[str, ndarray], dict[str, dict[str, ndarray]], dict[str, Any]]

See also

make_inversion_table

The arrays of the DF’s table as a JAX function of the parameters.

rows_jacobian

The DF and its derivatives at the energies of the inversion.

inversion_nodes

The energies at which compute_df evaluates the DF.

Notes

The derivative of each array of the table follows by the chain rule from the derivatives of the DF at the energies of the inversion and from the closed-form derivatives of the center’s exponent and amplitude.

A call costs several inversions of the component, as rows_jacobian does, and for a component on a TruncatedProfile one solve of the component in addition.
