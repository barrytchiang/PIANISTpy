Observables#

Module pianistpy.observables

Velocity dispersions and velocity distributions of a model.

The functions of pianistpy.observables return the radial, tangential and line-of-sight velocity dispersions, the line-of-sight velocity distribution, and the local velocity distribution of a model, at user-specified radii or in a user-specified aperture on the plane of the sky.

class pianistpy.observables.Aperture(shape, r_off=0.0, r_ape=None, side_x=None, side_y=None, angle=0.0)#

An aperture on the plane of the sky, circular or rectangular.

The constructors Aperture.circle and Aperture.rectangle build an aperture. The lengths of an aperture are in the unit system of the model or LosTable that the aperture is used with. In the internal and the model systems, the unit of length is the scale radius \(r_0\). In the physical system, the unit of length is the parsec.

Parameters:

  • shape ({"circle", "rectangle"}) – Shape of the aperture. "circle" is a circle of radius r_ape. "rectangle" is a rectangle of full sides side_x and side_y.

  • r_off (float, optional) – Projected distance \(R_{\rm off}\) of the center of the aperture from the center of the model. r_off must be zero or positive. Default: 0.0, which means the aperture is centered on the model.

  • r_ape (float, optional) – Radius \(R_{\rm ape}\) of a circle. r_ape must be positive for a circle and must be None for a rectangle. Default: None.

  • side_x (float, optional) – Full length of the rectangle’s side along the rectangle’s x axis. side_x must be positive for a rectangle and must be None for a circle. Default: None.

  • side_y (float, optional) – Full length of the rectangle’s side along the rectangle’s y axis. side_y must be positive for a rectangle and must be None for a circle. Default: None.

  • angle (float, optional) – Angle, in radians, between the rectangle’s x axis and the line through the center of the model and the center of the rectangle. Default: 0.0, which means the rectangle’s x axis lies on the line through the center of the model and the center of the rectangle. A circle accepts only 0.0.

Raises:

  • InputError – If shape is neither "circle" nor "rectangle".

  • InputError – If r_off is negative.

  • InputError – If a length the shape requires is missing or not positive.

  • InputError – If a length the shape does not use is given, or a circle is given a nonzero angle.

See also

sigma_los_aperture

Line-of-sight velocity dispersion inside an aperture.

aperture_losvd

Line-of-sight velocity distribution inside an aperture.

Examples

>>> Aperture.circle(1.0, r_off=10.0).shape
'circle'
>>> Aperture.rectangle(2.0, 0.5, r_off=10.0, angle=0.3).side_y

arc(r)#

Return the angle, in radians, of the circle of radius r inside the aperture.

The circle of radius r is centered on the model. The angle \({\rm arc}(r)\) is \(2\pi\) where the whole circle lies inside the aperture. The angle is zero where no part of the circle lies inside the aperture. The integral over the aperture of a quantity \(f(R)\) of projected radius is \(\int f(R)\,{\rm arc}(R)\,R\,{\rm d}R\).

Parameters:

r (array-like) – User-specified projected radii, zero or positive, in the unit of length of the aperture.

Returns:

arc – The angle \({\rm arc}(r)\) in radians, from zero to \(2\pi\), with the shape of r.

Return type:

array

See also

Aperture.breaks

The projected radii at which \({\rm arc}(r)\) is not smooth.

Notes

For a circle of radius \(R_{\rm ape}\) at offset \(R_{\rm off} > 0\), the angle is \(2\pi\) for \(r \le R_{\rm ape} - R_{\rm off}\), zero for \(r < |R_{\rm off} - R_{\rm ape}|\) or \(r > R_{\rm off} + R_{\rm ape}\), and otherwise

\[{\rm arc}(r) = 4 \arcsin\sqrt{x/2}, \qquad x = \frac{R_{\rm ape}^2 - (r - R_{\rm off})^2}{2 r R_{\rm off}}.\]

For a centered circle (\(R_{\rm off} = 0\)), the angle is \(2\pi\) for \(r \le R_{\rm ape}\) and zero otherwise.

For a rectangle, each of the rectangle’s four edge lines crosses the circle of radius r at angles given in closed form. These crossing angles cut the circle into arcs. Each arc lies wholly inside or wholly outside the rectangle. The angle \({\rm arc}(r)\) is the sum of the arcs inside the rectangle. The same closed form holds for a rectangle that contains the center of the model, a rectangle whose edge passes through the center of the model, and a rectangle that excludes the center of the model.

breaks()#

Return the projected radii at which Aperture.arc is not smooth.

A quadrature in projected radius whose intervals end at the returned projected radii integrates a smooth function on each interval.

Returns:

breaks – The positive projected radii, in increasing order and in the unit of length of the aperture. For a centered circle, the list holds \(R_{\rm ape}\). For an offset circle, the list holds the positive values among \(|R_{\rm off} - R_{\rm ape}|\), \(R_{\rm ape} - R_{\rm off}\) and \(R_{\rm off} + R_{\rm ape}\). For a rectangle, the list holds at most eight projected radii. Four of the eight are the distances from the center of the model to the rectangle’s four edge lines. Each such distance is included only where the point of the edge line nearest to the center of the model lies on the edge. The other four are the distances from the center of the model to the rectangle’s four corners.

Return type:

list of float

class pianistpy.observables.Differentiable(model, settings=None)#

The observables of a one-component model as JAX functions of the component’s anisotropy.

The anisotropy is the pair \(\theta = (\beta_{\rm c}, R_{\rm a})\) of the central anisotropy \(\beta_{\rm c}\) and the anisotropy radius \(R_{\rm a}\). Each method of Differentiable takes \(\theta\) as the first argument. Each method returns the value of the public function of the same name as the method (pianistpy.sigma_los for Differentiable.sigma_los) on the model rebuilt at \(\theta\). Each method is a JAX function of \(\theta\) and works under jax.jit and jax.grad. To differentiate the observables of a model with several components, call likelihood.

Parameters:

  • model (Model) – Model of one component. The model must meet three conditions: the model is spatially finite (model.spatially_finite is True); the component’s profile is the truncated profile of a parent model and carries the parent’s distribution function (DF); and the component’s anisotropy family is isotropic, constant, Osipkov-Merritt (OM) Type I, OM Type II or Cuddeford-Osipkov-Merritt (COM) Type I.

  • settings (Settings, optional) – Numerical settings of the observables. Default: None, which means the settings of model.

Variables:

  • model (Model) – The model passed to the constructor.

  • theta (array, shape (2,)) – The anisotropy \((\beta_{\rm c}, R_{\rm a})\) of the model’s component. \(\beta_{\rm c}\) is dimensionless. \(R_{\rm a}\) is in internal units in every unit system of the model (internal, model and physical). An isotropic or constant component has an infinite \(R_{\rm a}\).

Raises:

  • InputError – If model is not a Model, or if model has more than one component. The components of a model with several components are differentiated through likelihood.

  • InputError – If the model is not spatially finite, or if the component’s profile is not a truncated profile that carries the DF of a parent model.

  • InputError – If the component’s anisotropy family is COM Type II. The observables of a COM Type II component are not differentiable in the anisotropy.

  • InputError – If a setting of the observables group is outside the setting’s range: n_projection_nodes below 20.

  • AccuracyError – If the validation report of the model has tier "refused" (an error above \(10^{-2}\)) and the settings of the model do not set write_anyway=True.

  • AccuracyError – If the squared line-of-sight dispersion of the model is negative at a tabulated projected radius.

  • ResultError – If an intermediate result fails a check that the next step of the computation needs, for example non-finite values from a degenerate model.

See also

likelihood

The likelihood of data given a model, differentiable in the anisotropy of each component.

truncate

The truncated model whose profile a differentiable component uses.

sigma_los

The line-of-sight velocity dispersion of a model.

losvd

The line-of-sight velocity distribution of a model.

pdf_v

The local velocity distribution of a model.

Notes

Every radius, velocity, aperture length and \(R_{\rm a}\) that a method reads is in internal units in every unit system of the model. Every value that a method returns is in internal units.

The first call of a method at a new \(\theta\) inverts the DF of the component at \(\theta\) on the component’s truncated profile. The first call then builds the tables of the observable at \(\theta\). A Differentiable keeps the tables of at most eight values of \(\theta\): the model’s own \(\theta\) and the seven other values of \(\theta\) first met most recently.

At a \(\theta\) where rebuilding the model raises InputError, UnphysicalModelError, AccuracyError or ResultError, a method returns NaN and a zero gradient. Any other package error raised during the computation of a method reaches the caller as jax.errors.JaxRuntimeError. The message of the jax.errors.JaxRuntimeError carries the message of the package error. Three checks run before the computation and raise the package error directly: the check of a concrete radius against the tabulated radii in sigma_r, sigma_t and sigma_los; the check that the component’s DF is differentiable in losvd, pdf_v and aperture_losvd; and the check that the aperture passed to aperture_losvd is an Aperture.

The derivative of a method holds fixed the radii and velocities at which the tables at \(\theta\) are tabulated. The value of a method follows the change of the tabulated radii and velocities from one \(\theta\) to another. The derivative of a method omits the change of the tabulated radii and velocities. A method is not differentiable in the radii or velocities that the method reads. The gradient in a radius or a velocity is NaN.

The parameters that carry a derivative depend on the method:

  • sigma_r, sigma_t, sigma_los and sigma_los_aperture carry the derivative in \(\beta_{\rm c}\) for every accepted family, and in \(R_{\rm a}\) for the OM and COM families.

  • losvd, pdf_v and aperture_losvd carry the derivative in \(\beta_{\rm c}\) for the constant and COM Type I families, and in \(R_{\rm a}\) for the OM and COM Type I families. The gradient of losvd, pdf_v or aperture_losvd in \(\beta_{\rm c}\) is NaN for an OM component.

  • For an isotropic or constant component, every method holds \(R_{\rm a}\) at infinity, and the gradient in \(R_{\rm a}\) is zero.

References

Notes Section T:7.2. Notes Section T:7.3.

Notes

The derivative of \(\sigma_{\rm ape}\) in a parameter \(\theta_i\) (\(\beta_{\rm c}\) or \(R_{\rm a}\)) is

\[\frac{\partial \sigma_{\rm ape}}{\partial \theta_i} = \frac{1}{2\sigma_{\rm ape}} \frac{\int_A \Sigma\, \partial \sigma_{\rm los}^2/\partial \theta_i \, {\rm d}A}{\int_A \Sigma \, {\rm d}A},\]

with \(A\) the aperture and \(\Sigma\) the surface density of the component. The surface density does not depend on \(\theta\), since the component’s profile is fixed. Both integrals use the quadrature nodes and weights of pianistpy.sigma_los_aperture.

class pianistpy.observables.ProjectedSampler(source, R_range, v_range=None)#

Sampler of pairs of projected radius and line-of-sight velocity.

The sampler draws pairs \((R, v)\) of projected radius \(R\) and line-of-sight velocity \(v\) from the probability density \(p(R, v) \propto 2\pi R\,\Sigma(R)\,D(v\,|\,R)\) on the annulus R_range and in the user-specified range v_range of line-of-sight velocity. \(\Sigma(R)\) is the surface density, and \(D(v\,|\,R)\) is the line-of-sight velocity distribution at \(R\). The distribution of \(R\) is weighted at each radius by the share of \(D(v\,|\,R)\) inside v_range. \(D(v\,|\,R)\) is the distribution that the model’s LosTable holds, for every anisotropy model, including Cuddeford-Osipkov-Merritt (COM) models. Call ProjectedSampler rather than Sampler to draw a projected sample on an annulus without drawing 3D particles.

Parameters:

  • source (Model or LosTable) – The model, of one component or a superposition of components, or the model’s LosTable. Given a Model, the sampler builds the model’s LosTable first. For several samplers of one model, pass pp.LosTable(model).

  • R_range ((float, float)) – The annulus \(R_{\rm lo} \leq R_{\rm 2D} \leq R_{\rm hi}\), with \(0 \leq R_{\rm lo} < R_{\rm hi}\). The unit depends on the model’s unit system (Model.to): units of the scale radius \(r_0\) in the internal and the model systems, parsecs in the physical system. In the physical system, Astropy quantities of any length unit are also accepted. An end of the annulus inside the table’s innermost projected radius moves to the innermost projected radius. An end beyond the table’s outermost projected radius moves to the outermost projected radius. The sampler moves an end only if the annulus’s mass cut off is at most \(10^{-6}\) of the annulus’s mass.

  • v_range ((float, float), optional) – The user-specified range \(v_{\rm lo} < v < v_{\rm hi}\) of signed line-of-sight velocity. The unit depends on the model’s unit system: internal units, units of \((G M_0/r_0)^{1/2}\) in the model system, km/s in the physical system. Default: None, which means the whole velocity distribution.

Variables:

  • mass (float) – The annulus’s mass in the velocity range, in internal units.

  • InputError, AccuracyError, ResultError, WorkerError – Given a Model, if LosTable raises the exception.

  • AccuracyError – If the LosTable’s validation report is above \(10^{-2}\) and the settings do not set write_anyway=True.

  • InputError – If R_range is not \(0 \leq R_{\rm lo} < R_{\rm hi}\).

  • InputError – If v_range is not \(v_{\rm lo} < v_{\rm hi}\).

  • InputError – If the annulus lies wholly inside the table’s innermost projected radius or wholly beyond the table’s outermost projected radius.

  • InputError – If the annulus’s mass outside the table’s projected radii is more than \(10^{-6}\) of the annulus’s mass.

  • AccuracyError – If R_range starts inside the table’s innermost projected radius and the surface density’s logarithmic slope between the table’s two innermost projected radii is 2 or steeper, so that the mass inside the innermost projected radius diverges.

  • InputError – If the annulus overlaps an interval where the table did not meet the table’s tolerance (table.table.r_uncertified).

  • AccuracyError – If the inverse cumulative distribution of \(|v|\) at a tabulated radius has no positive finite slope.

  • AccuracyError – If the sampler’s validation report is above \(10^{-2}\) and the settings do not set write_anyway=True.

The line-of-sight velocity dispersion \(\sigma_{\rm los}(R_{\rm 2D})\) at projected radius \(R_{\rm 2D}\) is the square root of the second moment of the line-of-sight velocity, weighted by the density along the line of sight.

Parameters:

  • source (Model or LosTable) – The model, or the LosTable of the model. A Model and the LosTable of the same model return the same values.

  • r_2d (float, array-like or Quantity) – User-specified projected radii \(R_{\rm 2D}\). The unit is the scale radius \(r_0\) in the internal and the model systems, and the parsec in the physical system (Model.to). In the physical system, an Astropy quantity of any length unit is also accepted.

Returns:

sigma_los – Line-of-sight velocity dispersion \(\sigma_{\rm los}\), with the shape of r_2d. In the internal system, a plain array in internal units. In the model system, a plain array in units of \((G M_0/r_0)^{1/2}\). In the physical system, an Astropy quantity in km/s.

Return type:

array or Quantity

Raises:

  • InputError – If source is neither a Model nor a LosTable.

  • InputError – If source is a Model with several components in one potential. A model of one component and a superposition (superpose) are accepted.

  • AccuracyError – If source is a Model whose relative error is above \(10^{-2}\) and whose settings do not set write_anyway=True.

  • InputError – If a radius lies inside the innermost or beyond the outermost projected radius that the model tabulates. The call does not extrapolate.

  • ResultError – If source is a Model and an intermediate result fails a check that the next step of the computation needs, such as values that are not finite for a degenerate model.

Velocity distribution at one radius#

Module pianistpy.observables.stages

velocity_distribution computes the local velocity distribution at one radius, with its accuracy report; pianistpy.observables.pdf_v() computes the same distribution at many radii (Local velocity distribution).

pianistpy.observables.stages.velocity_distribution(model, r, settings, profile=None)#

Return the local velocity distribution of a model at one 3D radius.

The distribution \(p(V\,|\,R)\) is the probability density of the velocity \(V\) of the model’s particles at the 3D radius \(R\). The call returns the distribution at one radius, together with the checks of the distribution and their validation report. pdf_v evaluates the same distribution at many radii and velocities, in the model’s unit system.

Parameters:

  • model (Model) – The model. The model has one component.

  • r (float) – 3D radius \(R\). The radius is in internal units in all three unit systems: a model set to the model system or to the physical system (Model.to) still takes r in internal units. An Astropy quantity is not accepted. The radius must lie at or beyond the innermost radius of the model’s tabulated profile and below the outermost radius of the model’s tabulated profile. For a Type II model, the radius must also lie below the anisotropy radius \(R_{\rm a}\). A radius outside that range raises InputError.

  • settings (Settings) – The numerical settings. The call reads three fields of the observables group: n_velocity_angle_nodes, n_velocity_angle_panels and velocity_tolerance. The settings table (Numerical settings) describes the three fields.

  • profile (DispersionProfile, optional) – The intrinsic velocity dispersions of model, as the attribute profile of pp.LosTable(model) holds them. The second moment of the distribution is checked against these dispersions. Default: None, which means the call computes the intrinsic velocity dispersions of model.

Returns:

distribution – The distribution at r. Every value is in internal units in all three unit systems of the model: velocities in the internal unit of velocity, and the distribution per unit internal velocity. The object holds the 3D radius r; the velocity nodes v_nodes and their quadrature weights v_weights; the distribution pdf at the nodes; the escape velocity v_esc; the largest velocity of the distribution’s support v_max; and the share of the distribution above the escape velocity unbound_fraction. The method read(v) returns the distribution at any velocities v, and zero at negative velocities and at velocities above v_max. The integral of the distribution over velocity is 1 to within record["norm"]. record holds the relative errors of three checks ("norm", "second_moment" and "halving"), their validation report record["report"] and the report’s tier record["tier"]. The call raises no error for a large check error: a check error above \(10^{-2}\) gives the tier "refused" in record["tier"].

Return type:

VelocityDistribution

Raises:

  • InputError – If model has more than one component.

  • InputError – If r lies inside the innermost radius or at or beyond the outermost radius of the model’s tabulated profile.

  • InputError – If model is a Type II model and r lies at or beyond the anisotropy radius \(R_{\rm a}\).

  • InputError – If r lies outside the radii at which the intrinsic velocity dispersions, given as profile or computed by the call, are tabulated.

  • ResultError – If profile is not given and the call cannot form the model’s radial velocity dispersion: the contribution of the DF below the tabulated potential is not finite, or the dispersion is positive at no tabulated radius.

See also

The local velocity distribution at many radii and velocities, in the model’s unit system.

The radial velocity dispersion, which the second-moment check reads.

The tables of a model, whose attribute profile can be passed as profile.

Notes

The distribution is

\[p(V\,|\,R) = \frac{4\pi}{\varrho(R)}\, R^{-2b} V^{2-2b} \int_0^1 (1-t^2)^{-b} F(\mathcal{Q})\,{\rm d}t,\]

with \(\varrho\) the density, \(F\) the distribution function (DF), \(t = \cos\eta\) and \(\eta\) the angle between the velocity and the radius vector. The exponent \(b\) is the central anisotropy \(\beta_{\rm c}\) for a constant-anisotropy model and for a Cuddeford-Osipkov-Merritt (COM) model, and \(b = 0\) for an isotropic model and for an Osipkov-Merritt model. The argument of the DF is

\[\mathcal{Q} = P(R) - \frac{V^2}{2}(1 + s\xi) + \frac{s\xi V^2}{2}\,t^2,\]

with \(P\) the relative potential, \(\xi = R^2/R_{\rm a}^2\), and \(s = +1\) for a Type I model, \(s = -1\) for a Type II model and \(s = 0\) for a model without an anisotropy radius. The integral runs over the DF’s support, \(\mathcal{Q} > K_{\rm c}\), with \(K_{\rm c}\) the DF’s floor energy (0 where the DF has no floor).

The support excludes part of the directions above the velocity \(V_*\): \(V_* = \sqrt{2(P - K_{\rm c})/(1 + \xi)}\) for a Type I model, and \(V_* = \sqrt{2(P - K_{\rm c})}\) for a Type II model. The support ends at \(V_{\rm max} = \sqrt{2(P - K_{\rm c})/(1 - \xi)}\) for a Type II model and at \(V_{\rm max} = \sqrt{2(P - K_{\rm c})}\) for every other model. The escape velocity is \(V_{\rm esc} = \sqrt{2P}\). For a Type II model, \(V_{\rm max}\) is above \(V_{\rm esc}\) where \(K_{\rm c}\) is below \(\xi P(R)\). unbound_fraction is the share of the distribution between \(V_{\rm esc}\) and \(V_{\rm max}\).

The angle integral starts with n_velocity_angle_nodes nodes per interval. The nodes double, at most three times, until the largest change of the distribution between the integral and the integral with doubled nodes is at most velocity_tolerance times the largest absolute value of the distribution with doubled nodes. record["halving"] is the ratio of the two maxima for the kept distribution. velocity_tolerance is \(10^{-6}\) in the default settings and \(10^{-4}\) in the quick settings. Velocities within a relative distance of \(10^{-9}\) from \(V_*\) are left out of that comparison. On a DF that diverges at its floor energy, the distribution diverges at \(V_*\), and the divergence is integrable.
