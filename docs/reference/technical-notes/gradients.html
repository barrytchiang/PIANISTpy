T:7 Gradients#

T:7.1 The inversion’s rule#

The rows of the inversion are \(F = T_b[g](q)\) on a fixed grid \(q\), where \(T_b\) is the operator of Section T:2.3 with its completion below the floor, applied to the source \(g\), namely the rows of \(\mathrm{d}^N\varrho_\mathrm{aux}/\mathrm{d} P^N\). At a fixed central anisotropy \(b = \beta_\mathrm{c}\), the operator is linear in its source, so that

\begin{equation} \frac{\partial F}{\partial {R_\mathrm{a}}} = T_b\!\left[\partial_{R_\mathrm{a}} g\right], \qquad \frac{\partial F}{\partial {\beta_\mathrm{c}}} = T_b\!\left[\partial_{b}\, g\right] + E_b[g] , \tag{T:7.1} \end{equation}

where \(\partial_\theta g\) denotes the mixed derivatives at fixed \(P\), formed symbolically in the same way as the derivatives of Section T:1.2.1, and \(E_b\) is the derivative of the operator itself with respect to \(b\) at fixed \(g\), through its prefactor and the exponent of its kernel. \(E_b\) has no closed form and is taken from the values of the operator at nearby \(b\): a central Richardson quotient over a step and its half inside the region of one form, or the one-sided third-order combinations of both sides and their mean at a corner where the form changes on one side (half-integer \(\beta_\mathrm{c}\), or the switch of the pair). The step balances the measured accuracy of the kernel against the truncation term of the quotient. The record states the Richardson residual of the quotient and, at a corner, the spread between its two sides, both relative to the scale of the column, and the residual is read in the tiers of Section T:0.5.

The table that the lookup reads is a function of the rows and of closed forms in \(\theta\), so its derivative is the chain rule through each piece, with every discrete choice of the forward pass held fixed: the grid, the anchor, the window of the fit, and the nodes below the written floor. The contribution of the rows is Eq. (T:7.1) along the rows. The form above the anchor is its least-squares fit linearized in the rows, with the exponent \(\kappa(b)\) and the amplitude \(A(b)\) of the center in closed form on the regular branch. The nodes below the floor are the integral, which is linear in its source, with the source’s cubic in \((\ln P, \ln|g|)\) linearized; in \(b\), the exponent and the prefactor of the floor pair are differenced in the same way as \(E_b\). The tail contributes through its amplitude, exponent, and scale from the first node below the floor. On a Method II child the parent is held fixed, and the sources of the child are the Leibniz sum of Section T:2.6, whose derivative is the same sum with \(\partial h/\partial\theta\) in closed form; the leaves are the rows, the edge law, and the stated form of the center, each with its derivative written out. The derivative is that of the solve with its choices fixed; between two parameters at which a choice changes, the solve at the second differs from the held one by its own tolerance.

T:7.2 What is served#

Gradients are served for constant anisotropy (\(\beta_\mathrm{c}\)), Cuddeford Type I (\(\beta_\mathrm{c}\) and \(R_\mathrm{a}\)), and Osipkov-Merritt Type I and II (\(R_\mathrm{a}\)). Three cases are refused, each with a named reason. The first is Cuddeford Type II, whose kernel carries the factor \((P - P(R_\mathrm{a}))^{s}\) with \(s = 1 - \beta_\mathrm{c} - j\), an interior point that moves with \(R_\mathrm{a}\) and an exponent that moves with \(\beta_\mathrm{c}\), so that the derivative of the factor with respect to \(R_\mathrm{a}\) is not integrable at the interior point for \(s \leq 0\), and the mixed rows do not carry the derivative there. The second is a center on its critical branch, where the branch itself changes with \(\beta_\mathrm{c}\). The third is a component on a profile that carries no DF of its own model. The derivative of the observables of a model with respect to \((\beta_\mathrm{c}, R_\mathrm{a})\) is the full derivative for a Method II model, whose profile is held fixed. A truncated Method I model is refused by name, since its profile is that of its own DF and moves with the anisotropy, so that neither the Jeans path at a fixed profile nor the DF’s path at a fixed potential carries the full derivative; an untruncated model is refused on both paths, since its edge terms read its DF.

T:7.3 The observables’ tangent rows#

Each observable is served as a JAX function of \(\theta = (\beta_\mathrm{c}, R_\mathrm{a})\) with a custom derivative. Its value is the public call on the model rebuilt at \(\theta\) on the host, and its derivative is given by the tangent rows of the observable in \(\theta\) on the tables’ own rows, with their placement frozen. The tangent rows of the radial dispersion are the derivative of the Jeans rule Eq. (T:6.1) on the same rows and nodes on the fixed profile, with the limit of the edge reading unmoved. The tangent rows of the projection are the derivative of the line-of-sight rule on the same nodes, through the derivative of the profile’s reading, and zero where the squared dispersion is clipped. The derivative of an aperture is the aperture’s integral of the tangent rows of the squared dispersion on the nodes of the plain call. The velocity distributions take their derivatives through the derivative of the DF itself: the lookup is differentiated by JAX on its own expressions, and the transpose carries a cotangent on \(F\) at the energies back to the numbers of the table through the three maps between them (the padded nodes, the spline slopes where a table has no exact ones, and the derived numbers of the forms), with the energies held fixed. The rows of the line-of-sight tables are recomputed level by level for their transpose and are held to the forward’s row bit for bit, which the order of summation on a GPU does not promise; a row that differs is refused by name there, whereas on the CPU every row is served. At a \(\theta\) where the model is refused, the value is not a number and its gradient is zero.

T:7.4 The likelihood#

The log-likelihood of a model against data is a JAX function of the weights of the components and of the mass and length scales of the model, traceable and differentiable in both, and with the anisotropy of the named free components as parameters through the tangent rows above. The observables predicted by a model are read on the host once, as tables over projected radius; a projected radius in the units of the data is \(R = L\,l\,x\) and a speed is \(v = V(m/l)^{1/2}u\), with \(x\) and \(u\) in internal units, \(L\) and \(V\) the factors of the model’s units, and \(m\) and \(l\) the scales relative to the model’s own. For a superposed model, the composite profile for a new set of weights is one matrix-vector product over the members’ tabulated moments (Section T:6.9); the likelihood is linear in the weights up to their normalization, so its gradient in the weights is exact. A refused input inside the traced function (a negative weight, a radius outside the table) gives minus infinity with a zero gradient, never an exception, so that a sampler over the parameters always sees a value. A binned distribution holds the length scale at the model’s own value and carries no gradient in it, which a caller reads as not a number rather than as a number.
