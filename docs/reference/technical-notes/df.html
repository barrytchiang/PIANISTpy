T:2 The DF#

T:2.1 The general and simplified forms of the Cuddeford inversion#

The general form of the Cuddeford inversion (Chiang+(2026d), Eq. (B22)), with the derivative outside the integral, can be analytically simplified to the commonly adopted form (Chiang+(2026d), Eq. (B9)), with the derivative inside the integral and a surface term at \(P = 0\), via integration by parts only if

\begin{equation} \frac{\mathrm{d}^m\varrho_\mathrm{aux}}{\mathrm{d} P^m}\ \text{continuous on}\ [0, \mathcal{K}] \qquad\text{and}\qquad \int_0^{\mathcal{K}}\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}^{m+1}\varrho_\mathrm{aux}}{\mathrm{d} P^{m+1}}\right|(\mathcal{K}-P)^{-n}\,\mathrm{d} P < \infty . \tag{T:2.1} \end{equation}

Further simplifications are possible, for instance, for half-integer \(\beta_\mathrm{c}\), where \(n = 0\), giving

\begin{equation} F_\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{K}) = \frac{2^{\beta_\mathrm{c}}}{(2\pi)^{3/2}\,\Gamma(1-\beta_\mathrm{c})}\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}^m\varrho_\mathrm{aux}}{\mathrm{d} P^m}\right|_{P=\mathcal{K}} . \tag{T:2.2} \end{equation}

However, neither condition in Eq. (T:2.1) is guaranteed; the continuity condition, for example, can fail at the outer end \(P = 0\) (Section T:2.2.6) or at the anisotropy radius of a Type II model (Section T:2.2.8). PIANISTpy therefore evaluates the simplified form wherever applicable for the given inputs and otherwise defaults to the general form (Chiang+(2026d), footnote to Eq. (B22)), as further discussed in Section T:2.3.1.

Regardless of the specific form of the Cuddeford inversion used, PIANISTpy inverts the density integral of Chiang+(2026d), Eq. (B8), which defines the auxiliary density from the DF, with \(P = 0\) at precisely \(R = \infty\) (for an untruncated model) or \(R = R_\mathrm{t}\) (for a finitely truncated one):

\begin{equation} \varrho_\mathrm{aux}(P) = \frac{2\pi^{3/2}\,\Gamma(1-\beta_\mathrm{c})}{\Gamma(3/2-\beta_\mathrm{c})} \int_0^{P} F_\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{K})\,\left[2(P - \mathcal{K})\right]^{1/2-\beta_\mathrm{c}}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{K} . \tag{T:2.3} \end{equation}

T:2.2 Asymptotic behavior of the DF#

The inversion of Section T:2.3 tabulates the DF over a finite range of \(\mathcal{K}\); PIANISTpy extends it analytically to the center, \(\mathcal{K} \to P_0\), and separately to \(\mathcal{K} \to 0\), which require the asymptotic behavior of the DF in closed form. Section T:2.2.1 analyzes the order-by-order expansion of the DF about the center. At the center, the Cusp Slope–Central Anisotropy theorem (An & Evans 2006) states that a density with a power-law cusp \(\varrho \propto R^{-\gamma}\) admits a non-negative DF only if \(\gamma \geq 2\beta_\mathrm{c}\), which leaves three cases: \(\gamma > 2\beta_\mathrm{c}\), where the DF diverges at the center (Section T:2.2.2); the boundary case \(\gamma = 2\beta_\mathrm{c}\) (Section T:2.2.3); and \(\gamma < 2\beta_\mathrm{c}\), where the DF is negative at the center for every truncation (Section T:3.5). Section T:2.2.4 adds the powers that the anisotropy factor of a COM model contributes; Section T:2.2.5 checks the closed-form DF of the NFW profile at \(\beta_\mathrm{c} = 1/2\); Sections T:2.2.6 and T:2.2.7 treat the outer end, the surface term as \(P \to 0\) and the \(\mathcal{K} \to 0\) tail of the DF with its contribution to the density; Section T:2.2.8 treats the anisotropy radius of a Type II model, an interior point where \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}\) is not analytic.

T:2.2.1 The order-by-order expansion of the DF about the center#

Consider a single term \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux} \supset A_s\,(P_0 - P)^{s}\) of the expansion of \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}\) in the depth about the center. Its \((m+1)\)-th derivative is \((-1)^{m+1} A_s\,[\Gamma(s+1)/\Gamma(s-m)]\,(P_0-P)^{s-m-1}\). With \(t = \mathcal{K} - P\), the integral of Chiang+(2026d), Eq. (B9) becomes \(\int_0^{\mathcal{K}} t^{-n} (u+t)^{s-m-1}\,\mathrm{d} t\), whose behavior as \(u \to 0\) follows from the Mellin-type integral

\begin{equation} \int_0^{\infty} t^{a-1}(u+t)^{-b}\,\mathrm{d} t = u^{a-b}\,\frac{\Gamma(a)\,\Gamma(b-a)}{\Gamma(b)}, \tag{T:2.4} \end{equation}

with \(a = 1-n\) and \(b = m+1-s\). Extending the upper limit from \(\mathcal{K}\) to infinity, as in Eq. (T:2.4), changes the integral only by the part over \(t \geq \mathcal{K}\), which is analytic at \(u = 0\). Using \(\Gamma(s-m)\,\Gamma(m+1-s) = \pi/\sin[\pi(s-m)]\) and \(\Gamma(-s)\,\Gamma(1+s) = -\pi/\sin(\pi s)\), this term contributes to \(F_\mathcal{K}\) the singular part, i.e., the terms non-analytic at the center (negative or non-integer powers and logarithms of \(u\)),

\begin{equation} F_\mathcal{K}^{\rm sing}(\mathcal{K}) = \frac{2^{\beta_\mathrm{c}}}{(2\pi)^{3/2}\,\Gamma(1-\beta_\mathrm{c})}\; A_s\,\frac{\Gamma(\nu - s)}{\Gamma(-s)}\; u^{\,s-\nu}, \tag{T:2.5} \end{equation}

plus a part analytic in \(u\), i.e., a power series in the depth, which is regular at the center and is carried later as the remainder of Section T:2.5.2. The singular part takes one of three forms, by the value of \(s\).

  1. For \(s\) a non-negative integer, \(1/\Gamma(-s) = 0\): the power \((P_0 - P)^{s}\) is a polynomial in \(P\), and this term contributes no singular part.

  2. For \(s = \nu + k\) with \(k\) a non-negative integer and \(\nu\) not an integer, \(\Gamma(\nu - s)\) has a pole, and the power \(u^{\,s-\nu} = u^{k}\) of Eq. (T:2.5) is replaced by a logarithm,

    \begin{equation} F_\mathcal{K}^{\rm sing}(\mathcal{K}) = \frac{2^{\beta_\mathrm{c}}}{(2\pi)^{3/2}\,\Gamma(1-\beta_\mathrm{c})}\,\frac{(-1)^{k}\,A_s}{k!\;\Gamma(-s)}\;u^{k}\,(-\ln u) , \tag{T:2.6} \end{equation}

    which for \(k = 0\) reduces to \(-\,2^{\beta_\mathrm{c}}\,A_\nu\,\ln u\,/\,[(2\pi)^{3/2}\,\Gamma(1-\beta_\mathrm{c})\,\Gamma(-\nu)]\).

  3. Otherwise this term contributes the power law \(u^{\,s-\nu}\), whose sign is that of \(A_s\,\Gamma(\nu-s)/\Gamma(-s)\).

Since the inversion is linear, every power in the expansion of \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}\) contributes a term of the form of Eq. (T:2.5) or Eq. (T:2.6), and the singular part of \(F_\mathcal{K}\) at the center is their sum in order of increasing \(s\); only the non-negative integer powers drop out, and a negative integer \(s\) is singular like any other. The coefficients \(A_s\) are those of the series of Section T:1.1. PIANISTpy forms each ratio of two Gamma functions as the exponential of the difference of their log-Gamma values, with the sign carried separately, so that a steep center whose Gamma values overflow in double precision keeps a finite amplitude.

T:2.2.2 The central DF for γ > 2β_c#

The leading term of \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}\) is \(A\,(P_0-P)^{s_0}\) with \(s_0 = (2\beta_\mathrm{c} - \gamma)/(2-\gamma) < 0\). Applied to the leading term, Eq. (T:2.5) gives \(F_\mathcal{K} \propto u^{\kappa}\) with \(\kappa = s_0 - \nu\), the exponent of Chiang+(2026d), Eq. (B36), together with its amplitude,

\begin{equation} F_\mathcal{K} \simeq \frac{2^{\beta_\mathrm{c}}}{(2\pi)^{3/2}\,\Gamma(1-\beta_\mathrm{c})}\,A\,\frac{\Gamma(\nu - s_0)}{\Gamma(-s_0)}\,u^{\kappa}, \tag{T:2.7} \end{equation}

whose amplitude is positive. For the isotropic Hernquist profile, \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux} \simeq (2\pi u)^{-1}\) and \(F \simeq 3\,u^{-5/2}/(16\sqrt{2}\,\pi^2)\), which is the leading term of its closed form (Hernquist 1990).

As \(\gamma \to 2\beta_\mathrm{c}\), the amplitude of this term vanishes linearly, \(\Gamma(\nu - s_0)/\Gamma(-s_0) = -s_0\,\Gamma(\nu) + \mathcal{O}(s_0^2)\), while its exponent tends to \(-\nu\), not to the exponent \(\kappa'\) of the boundary case. The divergent term of the generic case therefore switches off continuously at \(\gamma = 2\beta_\mathrm{c}\), where the leading term is instead that of Section T:2.2.3, with a different exponent.

T:2.2.3 The central DF in the boundary case γ = 2β_c#

By Eq. (T:1.1), \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux} = C_1 + C_2\,(P_0-P)^{\delta} + \dots\): for constant anisotropy it is a series in \((P_0-P)^{\delta}\); for OM (\(\gamma = 0\)) the factor \(1 \pm R^2/R_\mathrm{a}^2\) adds the powers \((P_0-P)^{1 + j\delta}\), \(j \geq 0\), of which the integer power (\(j = 0\)) contributes no singular part and the rest (\(j \geq 1\)) are of higher order than \((P_0-P)^{\delta}\). The constant term has \(s = 0\) and no singular part. The next-to-leading-order term has \(s = \delta\) and the exponent \(\kappa' = \delta - \nu\) of Chiang+(2026d), Eq. (B37). T:2.2.4 The central DF of COM models#

For COM models, the anisotropy factor \((1 \pm R^2/R_\mathrm{a}^2)^{1-\beta_\mathrm{c}}\) of \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}\) is, about the center, a power series in \(R^2 \propto (P_0-P)^{2/(2-\gamma)}\). The expansion of \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}\) then contains the powers

\begin{equation} s_{kj} = k\,\delta + \frac{2j}{2-\gamma}, \qquad k, j \geq 0, \tag{T:2.8} \end{equation}

and the leading singular term is the smallest \(s_{kj}\) that is not an integer. The result of Section T:2.2.3 is modified when \(2/(2-\gamma)\) is not an integer and, in addition, either \(2/(2-\gamma) \leq \delta\) or \(\delta\) is an integer. At \(2/(2-\gamma) = \delta\) (that is, \(\alpha = 2\)) the exponent is unchanged and only the amplitude is modified: \(C_2 \to C_2\,[1 \mp 2(1-\beta_\mathrm{c})/((\beta-\gamma)\,R_\mathrm{a}^2)]\), with \(-\) for Type I and \(+\) for Type II. Since \(2/(2-\gamma)\) is an integer at \(\gamma = 0\) and at \(\gamma = 1\), OM models (which are critical only at \(\gamma = 0\)) and all \(\gamma = 1\) profiles are unaffected.

Where the modification applies, the exponent is \(2/(2-\gamma) - \nu\), which is negative for \(0 < \gamma < (5-\sqrt{17})/2 \simeq 0.438\). A center that is finite under constant anisotropy is then divergent under COM Type I. For example, \((\alpha, \gamma, \beta_\mathrm{c}) = (1.8, 0.2, 0.1)\) has \(\delta = 1\) and a finite center under constant anisotropy, but \(F_\mathcal{K} \propto u^{-13/45}\) under COM Type I.

T:2.2.5 The closed-form DF of the NFW profile at β_c = 1/2#

With \(\varrho = 1/[R(1+R)^2]\) and \(P = \ln(1+R)/R\), Eq. (T:2.2) gives

\begin{equation} F_\mathcal{K} = \frac{1}{2\pi^2}\frac{\mathrm{d}(R\varrho)}{\mathrm{d} P} = \frac{1}{\pi^2}\,\frac{R^2}{(1+R)^3\left[\ln(1+R) - R/(1+R)\right]}, \tag{T:2.9} \end{equation}

evaluated at \(R(\mathcal{K})\). Its expansion about the center contains integer powers only,

\begin{equation} \pi^2 F_\mathcal{K} = 2 - \frac{20}{3}u + \frac{28}{3}u^2 - \frac{1024}{135}u^3 + \mathcal{O}(u^4), \tag{T:2.10} \end{equation}

so the central DF is finite, \(F_\mathcal{K}(P_0) = 2/\pi^2\).

T:2.2.6 The limit P → 0#

Let \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux} \propto P^{\,y}\) as \(P \to 0\), where \(y\) is not a non-negative integer smaller than \(m\). Then \(\mathrm{d}^m\varrho_\mathrm{aux}/\mathrm{d} P^m \propto P^{\,y-m}\), and the surface term of Chiang+(2026d), Eq. (B9)

\begin{equation} \text{vanishes for}\ y > m, \qquad \text{is finite for}\ y = m, \qquad \text{diverges for}\ y < m . \tag{T:2.11} \end{equation}

Where \(y\) is a non-negative integer smaller than \(m\), the \(m\)-th derivative of the leading term vanishes and the next term of the expansion decides. For integer \(\alpha\) and integer \(\beta > 3\), a (C)OM Type I \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}\) is analytic in \(P\) at \(P = 0\), and the surface term is finite.

For an untruncated profile with \(\beta > 3\), Eq. (T:1.2) gives

\begin{equation} y = \beta - 2\beta_\mathrm{c} \ \ \text{(constant anisotropy)}, \qquad y = \beta - 2 \ \ \text{[(C)OM Type~I]}. \tag{T:2.12} \end{equation}

Table T:2.2 lists the consequences. The bound \(\beta > m+2\) is sharp.

PIANISTpy does not accept an analytic input profile with \(2 < \beta < 3\): the outer end of the inversion and the tail exponent of Section T:2.2.7 assume \(P \propto 1/R\) at large radius, which holds for \(\beta \geq 3\) only, the cases the outer forms of Chiang+(2026d) treat.

T:2.2.7 The outer tail of the DF#

For an untruncated (C)OM Type I model with \(\beta > 3\), Eq. (T:2.12) and Chiang+(2026d), Eq. (B25) give a power-law tail,

\begin{equation} F_\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{K}) \simeq A\,\mathcal{K}^{-a} \quad (\mathcal{K} \to 0), \qquad a = \nu - (\beta - 2) = \tfrac{7}{2} - \beta_\mathrm{c} - \beta , \tag{T:2.13} \end{equation}

which diverges for \(a > 0\); a profile with exactly \(\beta = 3\) carries a logarithmic factor beside the power. By Eq. (T:2.3), the part of the tail below a value \(q\) of \(\mathcal{K}\) contributes to the auxiliary density at \(P > q\) the amount

\begin{equation} \varrho_\mathrm{aux}^{\rm tail}(P) = \frac{2\pi^{3/2}\,\Gamma(1-\beta_\mathrm{c})}{\Gamma(3/2-\beta_\mathrm{c})}\; 2^{\,\nu-1} A\, P^{\,\nu-a}\; B\!\left(\frac{q}{P};\, 1-a,\, \nu\right), \tag{T:2.14} \end{equation}

where \(B(x; p, r) = \int_0^x t^{p-1}(1-t)^{r-1}\,\mathrm{d} t\) is the incomplete Beta function. The integral converges for \(a < 1\). For \(a \geq 1\) the density integral diverges at \(\mathcal{K} = 0\), and the untruncated model does not exist; a truncated model does exist, since the truncated DF of Chiang+(2026d), Eq. (B27), \(\hat F(\hat{\mathcal{K}}) = F(\hat{\mathcal{K}} + \mathcal{K}_\mathrm{t}) - F(\mathcal{K}_\mathrm{t})\), vanishes at the truncation boundary \(\hat{\mathcal{K}} = 0\).

T:2.2.8 Behavior of the inversion at the anisotropy radius of a Type II model#

For Type II models, \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux} \propto w^{1-\beta_\mathrm{c}}\) as \(w \equiv 1 - R^2/R_\mathrm{a}^2 \to 0\), and \(w\) is linear in \(P - P(R_\mathrm{a})\) there. Hence

\begin{equation} \frac{\mathrm{d}^m\varrho_\mathrm{aux}}{\mathrm{d} P^m} \propto |P - P(R_\mathrm{a})|^{\,1-\beta_\mathrm{c}-m} = |P - P(R_\mathrm{a})|^{\,n - 1/2}, \tag{T:2.15} \end{equation}

which diverges at \(P(R_\mathrm{a})\) for \(n < 1/2\). The divergence is approached from inside \(R_\mathrm{a}\) and does not depend on how \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}\) is continued beyond it (Section T:3.1). For OM Type II, \(n = 1/2\) and the factor is a polynomial, so the conditions of Eq. (T:2.1) hold. For COM Type II with integer \(\beta_\mathrm{c}\), \(n = 1/2\) as well. Where \(1 - \beta_\mathrm{c}\) is odd, the continued factor \(\mathrm{sign}(w)\,|w|^{1-\beta_\mathrm{c}}\) equals \(w^{1-\beta_\mathrm{c}}\), a polynomial, and the conditions hold. Where \(1 - \beta_\mathrm{c}\) is even (\(\beta_\mathrm{c} = -1, -3, \dots\)), the continuation makes \(\mathrm{d}^m\varrho_\mathrm{aux}/\mathrm{d} P^m\) jump at \(P(R_\mathrm{a})\), so the first condition of Eq. (T:2.1) fails there. Unlike the divergence for \(n < 1/2\), this failure is set by the continuation. A truncated model with \(R_\mathrm{t} < R_\mathrm{a}\) does not contain the point. At \(R_\mathrm{t} = R_\mathrm{a}\) the point is the model’s edge, and PIANISTpy admits only \(\mathcal{K}_\mathrm{t} > P(R_\mathrm{a})\), i.e., \(R_\mathrm{t} < R_\mathrm{a}\).

T:2.3 Evaluating the inversion on the grid of the energy-like variable#

PIANISTpy evaluates the Cuddeford inversion row by row on the grid of values of \(\mathcal{K}\) of Section T:2.4: a row of the DF table is one grid value of \(\mathcal{K}\), at which the inversion is an integral over \(P\) from the floor \(q_0\) to \(\mathcal{K}\). Section T:2.3.1 fixes the derivative order and the form of the inversion from the model parameters; Section T:2.3.2 treats the floor \(q_0\), where the table of \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}\) starts; Section T:2.3.3 splits the integral of the general form at an interior point; Section T:2.3.4 gives the quadrature of each row.

T:2.3.1 Choice of the derivative order and of the inversion form#

PIANISTpy fixes the form of the inversion from the model parameters alone, before any integral is evaluated, by five rules.

  1. Order pair. From \(\beta_\mathrm{c}\) PIANISTpy forms the pair \((m, n)\) of Eq. (T:0.1). For \(n > 1/2\) it replaces the pair by

    \begin{equation} (j, n') = (m+1,\; n-1), \tag{T:2.16} \end{equation}

    so that the kernel exponent always satisfies \(|n'| \leq 1/2\); otherwise \((j, n') = (m, n)\). With the higher order, the surface term can diverge as \(P \to 0\) (Section T:2.2.6) and cancel against the integral, so a replaced pair is evaluated in the general form, Chiang+(2026d), Eq. (B22), on every row.

  2. Half-integer \(\beta_\mathrm{c}\). For \(n = 0\) the closed form of Eq. (T:2.2) is used, and no quadrature is performed.

  3. Interior point. For an untruncated COM Type II model, the point \(P(R_\mathrm{a})\) and the exponent of Eq. (T:2.15) are known in closed form. Rows with \(\mathcal{K} \leq P(R_\mathrm{a})\) take the form that items 1, 4 and 5 fix; rows above it take the general form, with the lower integral of Eq. (T:2.18) split at \(P(R_\mathrm{a})\) and evaluated on either side by a Gauss–Jacobi rule in \(\ln P\) that carries the exponent as its weight (Section T:2.3.3). For odd integer \(1-\beta_\mathrm{c}\) the factor is a polynomial, and there is no such point.

  4. An unbounded outer end. Where \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}^{(j)}\) is unbounded at \(P = 0\), the rows above a stated value of \(\mathcal{K}\) use the general form.

  5. All other rows use the form with the surface term.

PIANISTpy applies the same operator one order higher to obtain \(\mathrm{d} F_\mathcal{K}/\mathrm{d}\mathcal{K}\) on the same rows, so that the table carries an exact slope column.

T:2.3.2 The floor of the DF grid#

The table of \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}\) and its derivatives starts at the floor \(q_0 = 10^{-10}\,P_0\), not at \(P = 0\). Splitting the integral of the general form at \(q_0\) and integrating its upper part by parts gives, with no assumption about the behavior at \(P = 0\),

\begin{equation} \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{K}}\int_0^{\mathcal{K}}\frac{\varrho_\mathrm{aux}^{(j)}(P)}{(\mathcal{K}-P)^{n}}\,\mathrm{d} P = \int_{q_0}^{\mathcal{K}}\frac{\varrho_\mathrm{aux}^{(j+1)}(P)}{(\mathcal{K}-P)^{n}}\,\mathrm{d} P + \frac{\varrho_\mathrm{aux}^{(j)}(q_0)}{(\mathcal{K}-q_0)^{n}} - n\int_0^{q_0}\frac{\varrho_\mathrm{aux}^{(j)}(P)}{(\mathcal{K}-P)^{n+1}}\,\mathrm{d} P , \tag{T:2.17} \end{equation}

where \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}^{(j)} \equiv {\mathrm{d}}^j\varrho_\mathrm{aux}/\mathrm{d} P^j\). The first two terms need only the table; the third is the contribution of the stretch below the floor, so that the floor of the table is not the floor of the model. For Method I, PIANISTpy evaluates this third term in the floor pair \((m, n)\) from the exact derivatives of Section T:1.2.1, over \([P_\mathrm{min}, q_0]\) with \(P_\mathrm{min} = 10^{-42}\), on Gauss–Legendre nodes in \(\ln P\) up to \(q_0/10\) and in \(w = (q_0 - P)^{1-n}\) above, integrated once by parts (or as written where \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}^{(m)}\) is unbounded at \(P = 0\)), with \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}^{(m)}(P_\mathrm{min})\) standing for the stretch below \(P_\mathrm{min}\). For Method II, the third term is the closed form of Section T:2.6. No such completion is needed for a half-integer \(\beta_\mathrm{c}\), nor for a model whose outer end is bounded with a tail exponent of at least 1. The rows of an untruncated table start at the first grid node at or above \(2\,q_0\), written together with the nodes below the floor (Section T:2.5); where the model has no such nodes, the rows start at \(q_0\).

T:2.3.3 Splitting the integral of the general form#

The rows (grid values of \(\mathcal{K}\)) that use the general form (Section T:2.3.1) split the integral at an interior point \(c < \mathcal{K}\). Below \(c\) the derivative is taken under the integral sign; above \(c\) the integral is integrated by parts:

\begin{equation} \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{K}}\int_{q_0}^{\mathcal{K}}\frac{\varrho_\mathrm{aux}^{(j)}(P)}{(\mathcal{K}-P)^{n}}\,\mathrm{d} P = -n\int_{q_0}^{c}\frac{\varrho_\mathrm{aux}^{(j)}(P)}{(\mathcal{K}-P)^{n+1}}\,\mathrm{d} P + \frac{\varrho_\mathrm{aux}^{(j)}(c)}{(\mathcal{K}-c)^{n}} + \int_{c}^{\mathcal{K}}\frac{\varrho_\mathrm{aux}^{(j+1)}(P)}{(\mathcal{K}-P)^{n}}\,\mathrm{d} P . \tag{T:2.18} \end{equation}

The right-hand side requires \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}^{(j)}\) only on \([q_0, c]\), where it is bounded except at an interior point, and \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}^{(j+1)}\) only above \(c\). For a replaced pair, Eq. (T:2.16), this avoids \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}^{(m+1)}\) at the floor, which the form with the surface term would require and which can diverge as \(P \to 0\). For rows above the interior point of a COM Type II model, \(c\) lies above \(P(R_\mathrm{a})\), and the lower integral is evaluated on the two sides of that point with a rule that carries as its weight the exponent \(1-\beta_\mathrm{c}-j\) of \(\mathrm{d}^j\varrho_\mathrm{aux}/\mathrm{d}P^j\) at \(P(R_\mathrm{a})\), which is Eq. (T:2.15) at the order \(j\) in use: \(n - 1/2\) for a pair that is not replaced and \(n - 3/2\) for a replaced one.

T:2.3.4 Quadrature of the inversion integral#

Each row is integrated on three sub-intervals: from the floor to one tenth of the split point, on Gauss–Legendre nodes in \(\ln P\); from there to the split point, on Gauss–Legendre nodes in \(P\); and from the split point to \(\mathcal{K}\), where the kernel is singular. The split point is \(c = 0.9\,\mathcal{K}\) on the rows of \(F_\mathcal{K}\) that use the surface form, and the largest grid node at or below \(0.9\,\mathcal{K}\) on the rows of \(\mathrm{d}F_\mathcal{K}/\mathrm{d}\mathcal{K}\), on every row of a replaced pair and on the rows of an unbounded outer end. On the rows above the interior point of a COM Type II model, \(c = \max(0.9\,\mathcal{K}, [P(R_\mathrm{a}) + \mathcal{K}]/2)\), moved down to the largest grid node at or below it where that node lies at least halfway from \(P(R_\mathrm{a})\) to \(c\), and the stretch below \(c\) is the two Gauss–Jacobi pieces of Section T:2.3.3 in place of the first two sub-intervals. On the last sub-interval the integration variable is \(v\), with

\begin{equation} P = P_0 - (P_0 - \mathcal{K})\,e^{y}, \qquad y = v^{k}, \qquad k = \frac{1}{1-n} . \tag{T:2.19} \end{equation}

Near the upper limit, \(\mathcal{K} - P \simeq (P_0 - \mathcal{K})\,y\), so that

\begin{equation} (\mathcal{K} - P)^{-n}\,\mathrm{d} P \;\propto\; y^{-n}\,\mathrm{d} y = k\,v^{\,k(1-n)-1}\,\mathrm{d} v = k\,\mathrm{d} v , \tag{T:2.20} \end{equation}

and the kernel singularity is removed for every exponent that the order pair allows. Away from the upper limit, \(\ln(P_0 - P)\) is a power of \(v\), so Gauss–Legendre nodes in \(v\) are graded logarithmically in \(P_0 - P\), which resolves an integrand that steepens as a power of \(P_0 - P\) toward the center.

T:2.4 The grid of the energy-like variable#

PIANISTpy tabulates the DF on a grid of values of \(\mathcal{K}\) in five zones, from the floor \(q_0\) to the gap below the central potential: zone A, logarithmic in \(\mathcal{K}\) from the floor; zone AB, a buffer; zone B, linear (where zone B starts below \(0.01\,P_0\), logarithmic at 120 nodes per decade up to \(0.05\,P_0\) and linear above); zone BC, a buffer; and zone C, logarithmic in \(P_0 - \mathcal{K}\), cut at the gap, whose node is added. A buffer carries the step geometrically from the spacing on one side to the spacing on the other, with the number of steps whose total comes closest to the width of the buffer and the steps rescaled to sum to that width exactly, so that no jump in spacing enters the grid. The linear zone and the two logarithmic zones take their node counts from the settings, and the logarithmic zones extend over a stated fraction of the central potential. Zones A and AB are placed around the potential of the anisotropy radius for an OM Type I model and, for an OM Type II or a COM model, around the feature that a pilot inversion on a coarse grid finds: the least value of the DF below a stated value of \(\mathcal{K}\), among the nodes where the DF exceeds \(10^{-3}\) of its median positive value, refined by a parabola through that node and its two neighbors (where the DF crosses zero, this is the least value above that threshold, not the crossing); where no such feature lies between \(10^{3}\,q_0\) and that value, the standard zones are used, and the end toward \(P_0\) is never moved. An untruncated COM Type II model with an interior point (Section T:2.3.1) adds nodes around \(P(R_\mathrm{a})\): a band of half-width \(P(R_\mathrm{a})/2\) at a fifth of the local spacing, and pairs at \(P(R_\mathrm{a})\,(1 \pm 10^{-k/4})\), \(k = 0, \dots, 24\), within twice the local spacing. The values of \(\mathcal{K}\) below the floor are those of the completion below the floor (Section T:2.5), where the rule below the table takes over (Section T:2.5.4). Nodes that coincide in double precision are merged, so that the grid is strictly increasing.

T:2.5 The DF beyond the ends of its grid#

The inversion tabulates \(F_\mathcal{K}\) on the grid of Section T:2.4, from the floor \(q_0\) toward \(\mathcal{K} \to 0\) to an end node a small depth below the center toward \(\mathcal{K} \to P_0\), whereas a DF must be evaluable at every \(\mathcal{K}\) in \((0, P_0]\): the self-consistency checks, the velocity distributions and the particle sampler all read it through one lookup. This section states where the table ends and what PIANISTpy evaluates beyond it. Section T:2.5.1 fixes the end of the grid toward the center from the three limits that stop the rows of the inversion before \(P_0\); Section T:2.5.2 continues the DF between the end of the grid and the center by the central form of Section T:2.2; Section T:2.5.3 continues it below the floor, by the tail of Section T:2.2.7 for an untruncated model and by the forms of a truncated one; Section T:2.5.4 gives the interpolation inside the table and the two entry points of the lookup, by \(\mathcal{K}\) and by the depth; Section T:2.5.5 lists the checks written with the table, which decide whether PIANISTpy refuses it.

T:2.5.1 The end of the DF grid toward the center#

The rows of the inversion reach the node of the grid nearest the center, a gap of \(10^{-9}\) below \(P_0\), unless one of three limits stops them first. The first is the top limit of the table: each term \(C_s u^{s}\) of the central series of \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}\) gives \(\mathrm{d}^N\varrho_\mathrm{aux}/\mathrm{d} P^N = (-1)^N C_s\, s(s-1)\cdots(s-N+1)\,u^{s-N}\), which exceeds the largest double below \(u = (|C_s\,s\cdots(s-N+1)|/\mathrm{DBL\_MAX})^{1/(N-s)}\) for \(s < N\), and the top limit is the largest such depth over the terms and over the orders that the form needs (zero where no term grows). The second is the host floor, the depth at the smallest radius whose square double precision represents (\(2^{-537}\) with subnormals). The third is the cap of \(10^{300}\) on the DF: where the center’s closed form is a power \(A\,u^{\kappa}\) with \(\kappa < 0\) and \(A > 0\) on the regular or the critical divergent branch of an isotropic, constant-anisotropy or Type I model, the depth at which that form reaches the cap, above which the closed form of the center owns the values of \(\mathcal{K}\). The rows end at the shallowest of the three depths. Where no closed form covers the top, a row above the cap is refused with an error stating the cap and the first such row; where the leading exponent of the series vanishes (\(\gamma = 2\beta_\mathrm{c}\)), there is no closed form to join to, and a model whose host floor alone would cut its table is refused with the reason stated.

T:2.5.2 Analytical continuation of the DF to the center#

Toward the center, the table of \(F_\mathcal{K}\) ends at a node a small depth \(u_{\rm top}\) below \(P_0\). Between that node and the center, PIANISTpy continues the DF by its central form (Sections T:2.2.1 to T:2.2.4), computed from the model alone and stored with the table: the leading singular term \(S_1(u)\) and the next one, \(S_2(u)\), each a power or a logarithm with the coefficient of Eq. (T:2.5) or Eq. (T:2.6), with the coefficients of \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}\) taken from the exact series of Section T:1.1 to the second power that is not a non-negative integer. No exponent is fitted to the table, and the amplitude is the closed form wherever the center states one.

What remains of \(F_\mathcal{K}\) once the two singular terms are removed is the sum of a part analytic in \(u\) and the higher singular terms, each smaller than \(S_1\) by the factor \(u^{\kappa_i - \kappa_1}\), with \(\kappa_i\) the exponent of the \(i\)-th singular term. This remainder is held at its value at that end node, which errs relative to \(F_\mathcal{K}\) by an amount of order \(u_{\rm top}\) (the analytic part, relative to the value at the center) or \(u_{\rm top}^{\kappa_3 - \kappa_1}\) (the third singular term), whichever is larger:

\begin{equation} F_\mathcal{K}(u) = S_1(u) + S_2(u) + \left[F_\mathcal{K}(u_{\rm top}) - S_1(u_{\rm top}) - S_2(u_{\rm top})\right], \qquad u < u_{\rm top} . \tag{T:2.21} \end{equation}

Two cases depart from Eq. (T:2.21). Where the leading power is positive and the inversion is local (the last row of Table T:2.1), \(F_\mathcal{K}\) vanishes at the center, and the bracket is multiplied by \(u/u_{\rm top}\) so that the continuation vanishes with it. Where the center is analytic, there is no singular term, and \(F_\mathcal{K}\) is held at its top value. For a divergent power, the expression is evaluated in logarithms relative to \(S_1\), so that a DF whose central values exceed the range of double precision is continued without overflow.

For an isotropic, constant-anisotropy or Type I model whose center is a divergent power (the regular and the critical divergent branches), the table ends at an anchor wherever the potential at the innermost radius of the settings, \(R = 10^{-9}\) by default (log10_r_min), lies more than \(10^{-10}\) below \(P_0\): the anchor is the node of largest \(\mathcal{K}\) at or below that potential, and at least one node below the grid’s end toward the center. Above the anchor the DF is \(A\,u^{\kappa} + B + D\,u\), with \(A\) the closed-form amplitude and \(\kappa\) the singular exponent, never fitted; \(B\) and \(D\) are fitted to the rows with \(u_\mathrm{anchor} \leq u \leq W u_\mathrm{anchor}\), \(W\) doubling from 2 until the amplitudes of the three-term fit (\(A\), \(B\), \(D\)) and of the four-term fit (with \(E\,u^2\)) agree to \(10^{-7}\), and they are kept only where the three-term fit is positive at every node above the anchor (else \(B\) joins the form to the anchor and \(D = 0\)). A Type II model and a Method II model carry no anchor, and the other branches carry one only where a band at the top is excluded (below); without an anchor, the DF is continued by Eq. (T:2.21) from the last row. A band of rows at the top that is not positive is excluded only where the form holds there, as judged by the local slope \(\mathrm{d}\ln F/\mathrm{d}\ln u\) against \(\kappa\).

A truncated Method I model has \(\hat{F}_\mathcal{K}(\hat{\mathcal{K}}) = F_\mathcal{K}(\hat{\mathcal{K}} + \mathcal{K}_\mathrm{t}) - F_\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{K}_\mathrm{t})\), which differs from the untruncated DF by a constant. Its singular terms and their coefficients are those of the untruncated model, with \(u\) measured from the truncated central potential.

The stored form is tested against the table that it continues. Across the top decade of the table, \(u_{\rm top} \leq u \leq 10\,u_{\rm top}\), the table less its singular terms may change only by the variation of the analytic part. A change above \(10^{-6}\) is reported with the product; a change above \(10^{-2}\) means that the stored form belongs to a different model, and the product is refused.

T:2.5.3 Analytical continuation of the DF to the outer edge#

Below its floor node, the DF follows a rule recorded with the table: for an untruncated model whose exponent \(a\) is positive, the tail \(A\,\mathcal{K}^{-a}[\ln(E_0/\mathcal{K})]^{-b}\) of Eq. (T:2.13), with \(E_0 = P_0\) and with \(A\) and \(b\) chosen so that the tail joins the node nearest \(\mathcal{K} = 0\) below the written floor in value and in logarithmic slope (\(b = 0\) and a join in value only where those nodes carry no slope or, where there are none, at the written floor); for a truncated Method I model, its Taylor form \(F_1\hat{\mathcal{K}} + F_2\hat{\mathcal{K}}^2/2\) at the edge, together with the value of \(\hat{\mathcal{K}}\) up to which it holds; for a Method II model, its form \(\hat{\mathcal{K}}^{p}(C_0 + C_1\hat{\mathcal{K}})\) at the truncation boundary (Section T:2.6.1); and zero otherwise. The integral of the rule from zero to any value of \(\mathcal{K}\) below the table has the same closed form, so that a density integral never truncates the DF at its table.

T:2.5.4 Interpolation of the DF table#

Inside the table, the base-10 logarithm of the DF is interpolated by piecewise cubic Hermite polynomials in \(\ln{\mathcal{K}}\) through the nodes with the exact slope column. Where a table carries no exact slopes (a DF supplied as input, Section T:2.7, and a Method II model, Section T:2.6), the slopes are those of the not-a-knot cubic spline through the nodes. The lookup has two entry points, by \(\mathcal{K}\) and by the depth \(u = P_0 - \mathcal{K}\), and a caller asks by whichever argument it holds exactly. For example, a velocity draw near the top holds the depth \(u = (P_0 - \Psi) + \Psi x^2\) exactly, whereas the value \(\Psi(1 - x^2)\) of \(\mathcal{K}\) formed in doubles loses it. In the entry by \(u\), the cell position is formed from \(u_i - u\) with \(u_i = P_0 - \mathcal{K}_i\), which is exact for \(\mathcal{K}_i \geq P_0/2\), and the form above the table is read at \(u\) itself. The nodes and every number of the forms are arrays, so that under JAX the table is a traced argument, and two tables with the same node bucket and the same kinds of forms share one compiled function.

T:2.5.5 Checks and sign report of a DF table#

A table is written together with its checks: the stated slope column against the table’s own \(F\), the stated center against the top decade of the table, and the joins of the forms below the floor with the row at the floor. PIANISTpy refuses a table whose slope column or stated center fails beyond \(10^{-2}\), or whose forms below the floor miss the row at the floor beyond \(10^{-6}\). The sign report states where the DF is negative and the smallest truncation above which it is not (Section T:3.5). The validity certificate is a closed form: from the model, the central exponent and the closed-form amplitude, it states whether the columns of the table are representable in double precision, judged at the table’s own row nearest the center (the anchor, or the shallowest of the gap, the top limit, the host floor, and the cap on the DF); a Method II model carries none.

T:2.6 Method II: inversion of a truncated potential–density pair#

Method II is not a second pipeline: a Method II model runs through the same stages as a Method I model, on the truncated potential–density pair of its Method I model, with a different source of the derivatives (Section T:1.2.1) and four different steps: no slope column is made, a defective band at the top is refused rather than repaired, the stretch below the floor has the closed form below, and at \(\beta_\mathrm{c} = 1/2\) on a Method I model with \(\gamma = \alpha = 1\) the rows nearest the center are a closed-form band. The integral of the Method I model’s DF is its augmented density, the density \(\hat\varrho\) itself for an isotropic Method I model and \(h_\mathrm{I}\,\hat\varrho\), \(h_\mathrm{I} = 1 + R^2/R_{\mathrm{a},\mathrm{I}}^2\), for an OM Type I Method I model:

\begin{equation} h_\mathrm{I}\,\hat\varrho(\hat{P}) = 4\pi\sqrt{2}\int_0^{\hat{P}} \hat F(\hat{\mathcal{K}})\,(\hat{P} - \hat{\mathcal{K}})^{1/2}\,\mathrm{d}\hat{\mathcal{K}} , \tag{T:2.22} \end{equation}

so that its derivatives with respect to \(\hat{P}\) are integrals of the DF and of its derivatives against \((\hat{P} - \hat{\mathcal{K}})^{-1/2}\), with the boundary term \(F_1 \hat{P}^{-1/2}\) at order three, evaluated from the Method I model’s table, its exact slope column, and its Taylor edge; the second derivative of the DF at order three is the derivative of the monotone cubic through the slope column, the only spline that PIANISTpy differentiates. The auxiliary density of the Method II model is \(\hat\varrho_\mathrm{aux} = \hat\varrho/g(R)\), with \(g\) the Method II model’s own separability factor, and its derivatives follow by the Leibniz rule with \(\mathrm{d} R/\mathrm{d} \hat{P}\) taken from the rows. Orders up to three are served, the third only where the Method I model states its Taylor edge. The Method I model must be isotropic or OM Type I, and a Method II model of Type II must have its \(R_\mathrm{a}\) beyond the Method I model’s edge.

The grid is the five-zone grid of Section T:2.4 on the first potential of the Method I model, compact, with no extension below the floor, since the support of the Method II model ends at the Method I model’s edge. The stretch below the floor has a closed form. For each power \(\hat{P}^{q}\) of the two-term edge expansion of \(\hat\varrho_\mathrm{aux}\) (Section T:2.6.1), with \(a = q - m_f\), \(b = 1 - n_f\), and \(x = q_0/\hat{\mathcal{K}}\),

\begin{equation} C(n_f)\,a_q\,\frac{\Gamma(q+1)}{\Gamma(a+1)}\;\hat{\mathcal{K}}^{\,a-n_f}\,x^{a}\left[{}_2F_1(a, n_f;\, a+1;\, x) - 1\right] \tag{T:2.23} \end{equation}

is the exact contribution of \([0, q_0]\) to the row at \(\hat{\mathcal{K}}\), relative to the boundary form that the kernel carries, and the boundary term is the sources’ own value of \(\hat\varrho_\mathrm{aux}^{(m_f)}\) at the floor node: the Method I model’s derivatives there (in closed form from its Taylor edge where the node lies below the switch), its density from its rows, and the Method II model’s factor, by the Leibniz rule.

At \(\beta_\mathrm{c} = 1/2\) on a Method I model with \(\gamma = \alpha = 1\), the inversion is a derivative whose two terms are both of order \(1/u\) toward the center, so the rows there carry the Method I model’s accuracy multiplied by \(C/[2(\beta - 1)\,u]\), with \(\hat\varrho \simeq C/R\) at the center (\(1/(4u)\) for NFW, \(1/(3u)\) for Hernquist). Above an anchor, the rows are the closed form \(F = A\sum_{k \leq 4} a_k u^{k}\) with \(A = 1/(2\pi^2)\) (Section T:2.2.5), which is exact in the truncation through the density that the Method I model’s truncation removes near the center (Section T:3.4). The anchor is the smaller of the series’ own range at \(10^{-6}\) and the top zone of the grid, and the median relative deviation of the computed rows from the form over the outermost decade inside it, on at least ten rows, must be within \(10^{-6}\); otherwise the anchor steps inward one decade at a time until it is, and where no decade is, the rows stand as computed and the record says so.

T:2.6.1 The Method II DF at the truncation boundary#

A truncated density that ends as \(\hat{P}^{q}\) at its edge has a DF of central anisotropy \(\beta_\mathrm{c}\) if and only if \(\beta_\mathrm{c} > 1/2 - q\), and the DF then behaves as \(\hat{\mathcal{K}}^{\,q - 3/2 + \beta_\mathrm{c}}\) there. This statement holds for every profile and every anisotropy model of PIANISTpy, and a Method II model that violates it is refused before any stage runs, with the admissible range stated. Below the Method I model’s switch value of \(\hat{\mathcal{K}}\), the Method I model’s DF is its Taylor form \(F_1\hat{\mathcal{K}} + F_2\hat{\mathcal{K}}^2/2\), so the Method I model’s augmented density near the edge is \(4\pi\sqrt{2}\,[\tfrac{4}{15}F_1 \hat{P}^{5/2} + \tfrac{8}{105}F_2 \hat{P}^{7/2}]\) to the order kept. The Method II model’s augmented density, with \(1/g = 1/g_\mathrm{t} + G_1 \hat{P}\), \(G_1 = (1/g)'(R_\mathrm{t})\,R'(\hat{P})\), and \(R' = -R_\mathrm{t}^2/M_\mathrm{t}\), is then

\begin{equation} \hat\varrho_{\rm aug}(\hat{P}) = a_{5/2}\,\hat{P}^{5/2} + a_{7/2}\,\hat{P}^{7/2} + \mathcal{O}(\hat{P}^{9/2}) . \tag{T:2.24} \end{equation}

Each power inverts to a power, so the Method II model’s DF below its switch is \(\hat{\mathcal{K}}^{p}(C_0 + C_1\hat{\mathcal{K}})\) with \(p = 1 + \beta_\mathrm{c}\), and the same coefficients give the floor terms of Eq. (T:2.23).

T:2.7 A user-supplied DF#

A DF obtained elsewhere, from a fitting function or from another code, is checked in the same way as an inverted one. Its values on the \(\mathcal{K}\) grid that the inversion would place for the model enter where the rows of the inversion would enter, and everything downstream is unchanged: the forms beyond the table, the sign report, the checks, and the result’s record, which carries no inversion record and no rows. Such a table has no exact slope column, which its report states, and a value that is not finite is refused with the first such node stated. Truncation needs the rows of the inversion, which such a table does not carry, and is therefore refused with the reason stated. The round trip of Section T:8.3 accepts such a DF as it does an inverted one.
