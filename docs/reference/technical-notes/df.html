T:2 The distribution function#

T:2.1 The two forms of the inversion and the forward relation#

Chiang+(2026d) gives the inversion in two forms: with the derivative outside the integral, Eq. (B22), and with the derivative inside the integral together with a surface term at \(P = 0\), Eq. (B9). The second form follows from the first by one integration by parts and is simpler to evaluate. The integration by parts requires that

\begin{equation} \frac{\mathrm{d}^m\varrho_\mathrm{aux}}{\mathrm{d} P^m}\ \text{continuous on}\ [0, \mathcal{K}] \qquad\text{and}\qquad \int_0^{\mathcal{K}}\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}^{m+1}\varrho_\mathrm{aux}}{\mathrm{d} P^{m+1}}\right|(\mathcal{K}-P)^{-n}\,\mathrm{d} P < \infty . \tag{T:2.1} \end{equation}

Neither condition is guaranteed. The first can fail at the outer end, \(P = 0\) (Section T:2.2.6), and at the anisotropy radius of a Type II model (Section T:2.2.8).

For half-integer \(\beta_\mathrm{c}\) we have \(n = 0\), and the general form reduces to a derivative with no integral,

\begin{equation} F_\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{K}) = \frac{2^{\beta_\mathrm{c}}}{(2\pi)^{3/2}\,\Gamma(1-\beta_\mathrm{c})}\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}^m\varrho_\mathrm{aux}}{\mathrm{d} P^m}\right|_{P=\mathcal{K}} . \tag{T:2.2} \end{equation}

The forward relation is a Volterra equation whose lower limit lies at \(P = 0\), that is, at the outer edge of the model (\(R = \infty\) for an untruncated model, \(R = R_\mathrm{t}\) for a truncated one):

\begin{equation} \varrho_\mathrm{aux}(P) = \frac{2\pi^{3/2}\,\Gamma(1-\beta_\mathrm{c})}{\Gamma(3/2-\beta_\mathrm{c})} \int_0^{P} F_\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{K})\,\left[2(P - \mathcal{K})\right]^{1/2-\beta_\mathrm{c}}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{K} . \tag{T:2.3} \end{equation}

T:2.2 Asymptotic behavior of the DF#

T:2.2.1 The limit P → P₀: the contribution of one power-law term#

Consider a single term \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux} \supset A_s\,(P_0 - P)^{s}\) of the expansion of \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}\) about the center. Its \((m+1)\)-th derivative is \((-1)^{m+1} A_s\,[\Gamma(s+1)/\Gamma(s-m)]\,(P_0-P)^{s-m-1}\). With \(t = \mathcal{K} - P\), the integral of Chiang+(2026d), Eq. (B9) becomes \(\int_0^{\mathcal{K}} t^{-n} (u+t)^{s-m-1}\,\mathrm{d} t\), whose behavior as \(u \to 0\) follows from the Mellin-type integral

\begin{equation} \int_0^{\infty} t^{a-1}(u+t)^{-b}\,\mathrm{d} t = u^{a-b}\,\frac{\Gamma(a)\,\Gamma(b-a)}{\Gamma(b)}, \tag{T:2.4} \end{equation}

with \(a = 1-n\) and \(b = m+1-s\). The finite upper limit adds only a part that is analytic at \(u = 0\). Using \(\Gamma(s-m)\,\Gamma(m+1-s) = \pi/\sin[\pi(s-m)]\) and \(\Gamma(-s)\,\Gamma(1+s) = -\pi/\sin(\pi s)\), the term contributes

\begin{equation} F_\mathcal{K}^{\rm sing}(\mathcal{K}) = \frac{2^{\beta_\mathrm{c}}}{(2\pi)^{3/2}\,\Gamma(1-\beta_\mathrm{c})}\; A_s\,\frac{\Gamma(\nu - s)}{\Gamma(-s)}\; u^{\,s-\nu}, \tag{T:2.5} \end{equation}

plus a part that is analytic in \(u\). Three properties of Eq. (T:2.5) decide what follows.

  1. For \(s\) a non-negative integer, \(1/\Gamma(-s) = 0\), and the term has no singular part.

  2. For \(s = \nu + k\) with \(k\) a non-negative integer and \(\nu\) not an integer, \(\Gamma(\nu - s)\) has a pole, and the power is replaced by a logarithm,

    \begin{equation} F_\mathcal{K}^{\rm sing}(\mathcal{K}) = \frac{2^{\beta_\mathrm{c}}}{(2\pi)^{3/2}\,\Gamma(1-\beta_\mathrm{c})}\,\frac{(-1)^{k}\,A_s}{k!\;\Gamma(-s)}\;u^{k}\,(-\ln u) , \tag{T:2.6} \end{equation}

    which for \(k = 0\) reduces to \(-\,2^{\beta_\mathrm{c}}\,A_\nu\,\ln u\,/\,[(2\pi)^{3/2}\,\Gamma(1-\beta_\mathrm{c})\,\Gamma(-\nu)]\).

  3. Otherwise the term is a power law \(u^{\,s-\nu}\), whose sign is that of \(A_s\,\Gamma(\nu-s)/\Gamma(-s)\).

Since the inversion is linear, every power in the expansion of \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}\) contributes its own term of the form Eq. (T:2.5) or Eq. (T:2.6), and the singular part of \(F_\mathcal{K}\) at the center is the sum of these terms in order of increasing \(s\). Only the non-negative integer powers drop out; a negative integer \(s\) is singular like any other. The coefficients \(A_s\) follow from the expansion of Section T:1.1. A ratio of two Gamma functions is formed as the exponential of a difference of log-Gamma values together with its sign, never as a direct quotient, so that a steep center whose Gamma values overflow still has a finite amplitude.

T:2.2.2 The central DF for γ > 2β_c#

The leading term of \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}\) is \(A\,(P_0-P)^{s_0}\) with \(s_0 = (2\beta_\mathrm{c} - \gamma)/(2-\gamma) < 0\). Eq. (T:2.5) reproduces the exponent \(\kappa\) of Chiang+(2026d), Eq. (B36) and supplies its amplitude,

\begin{equation} F_\mathcal{K} \simeq \frac{2^{\beta_\mathrm{c}}}{(2\pi)^{3/2}\,\Gamma(1-\beta_\mathrm{c})}\,A\,\frac{\Gamma(\nu - s_0)}{\Gamma(-s_0)}\,u^{\kappa}, \tag{T:2.7} \end{equation}

which is positive. For the isotropic Hernquist profile in units \(G = M = a = 1\), \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux} \simeq (2\pi u)^{-1}\) and \(F \simeq 3\,u^{-5/2}/(16\sqrt{2}\,\pi^2)\), which is the leading term of its known closed form.

As \(\gamma \to 2\beta_\mathrm{c}\), the amplitude vanishes linearly, \(\Gamma(\nu - s_0)/\Gamma(-s_0) = -s_0\,\Gamma(\nu) + \mathcal{O}(s_0^2)\), while the exponent tends to \(-\nu\). The regular and the critical branch therefore join in amplitude but not in exponent.

T:2.2.3 The central DF in the boundary case γ = 2β_c#

By Eq. (T:1.1), \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux} = C_1 + C_2\,(P_0-P)^{\delta} + \dots\): for constant anisotropy it is a series in \((P_0-P)^{\delta}\); for OM (\(\gamma = 0\)) the factor \(1 \pm R^2/R_\mathrm{a}^2\) adds the powers \((P_0-P)^{1 + j\delta}\), \(j \geq 0\), of which the integer power drops out and the others lie above \(\delta\). The constant term has \(s = 0\) and no singular part. The next term has \(s = \delta\) and the exponent \(\kappa' = \delta - \nu\) of Chiang+(2026d), Eq. (B37). T:2.2.4 The central DF of COM models#

For COM models, the factor \((1 \pm R^2/R_\mathrm{a}^2)^{1-\beta_\mathrm{c}}\) of \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}\) contributes a series in \(R^2 \propto (P_0-P)^{2/(2-\gamma)}\). The expansion of \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}\) then contains the powers

\begin{equation} s_{kj} = k\,\delta + \frac{2j}{2-\gamma}, \qquad k, j \geq 0, \tag{T:2.8} \end{equation}

and the leading singular term is the smallest \(s_{kj}\) that is not an integer. The result of Section T:2.2.3 is modified when \(2/(2-\gamma)\) is not an integer and, in addition, either \(2/(2-\gamma) \leq \delta\) or \(\delta\) is an integer. At \(2/(2-\gamma) = \delta\) (that is, \(\alpha = 2\)) the exponent is unchanged and only the amplitude is modified: \(C_2 \to C_2\,[1 \mp 2(1-\beta_\mathrm{c})/((\beta-\gamma)\,R_\mathrm{a}^2)]\), with \(-\) for Type I and \(+\) for Type II. Since \(2/(2-\gamma)\) is an integer at \(\gamma = 0\) and at \(\gamma = 1\), OM models (which are critical only at \(\gamma = 0\)) and all \(\gamma = 1\) profiles are unaffected.

Where the modification applies, the exponent is \(2/(2-\gamma) - \nu\), which is negative for \(0 < \gamma < (5-\sqrt{17})/2 \simeq 0.438\). A center that is finite under constant anisotropy is then divergent under COM Type I. For example, \((\alpha, \gamma, \beta_\mathrm{c}) = (1.8, 0.2, 0.1)\) has \(\delta = 1\) and a finite center under constant anisotropy, but \(F_\mathcal{K} \propto u^{-13/45}\) under COM Type I.

T:2.2.5 The closed-form DF of the NFW profile at β_c = 1/2#

With \(\varrho = 1/[R(1+R)^2]\) and \(P = \ln(1+R)/R\), Eq. (T:2.2) gives

\begin{equation} F_\mathcal{K} = \frac{1}{2\pi^2}\frac{\mathrm{d}(R\varrho)}{\mathrm{d} P} = \frac{1}{\pi^2}\,\frac{R^2}{(1+R)^3\left[\ln(1+R) - R/(1+R)\right]}, \tag{T:2.9} \end{equation}

evaluated at \(R(\mathcal{K})\). Its expansion about the center contains integer powers only,

\begin{equation} \pi^2 F_\mathcal{K} = 2 - \frac{20}{3}u + \frac{28}{3}u^2 - \frac{1024}{135}u^3 + \mathcal{O}(u^4), \tag{T:2.10} \end{equation}

so the central DF is finite, \(F_\mathcal{K}(P_0) = 2/\pi^2\).

T:2.2.6 The limit P → 0#

Let \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux} \propto P^{\,y}\) as \(P \to 0\), where \(y\) is not a non-negative integer smaller than \(m\). Then \(\mathrm{d}^m\varrho_\mathrm{aux}/\mathrm{d} P^m \propto P^{\,y-m}\), and the surface term of Chiang+(2026d), Eq. (B9)

\begin{equation} \text{vanishes for}\ y > m, \qquad \text{is finite for}\ y = m, \qquad \text{diverges for}\ y < m . \tag{T:2.11} \end{equation}

Where \(y\) is a non-negative integer smaller than \(m\), the \(m\)-th derivative of the leading term vanishes and the next term of the expansion decides. For integer \(\alpha\) and integer \(\beta > 3\), a (C)OM Type I \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}\) is analytic in \(P\) at \(P = 0\), and the surface term is finite.

For an untruncated profile with \(\beta > 3\), Eq. (T:1.2) gives

\begin{equation} y = \beta - 2\beta_\mathrm{c} \ \ \text{(constant anisotropy)}, \qquad y = \beta - 2 \ \ \text{[(C)OM Type~I]}. \tag{T:2.12} \end{equation}

Table T:2.2 lists the consequences. The bound \(\beta > m+2\) is sharp.

T:2.2.7 The low-energy tail of the DF and its contribution to the density#

For an untruncated (C)OM Type I model with \(\beta > 3\), Eq. (T:2.12) and Chiang+(2026d), Eq. (B25) give a power-law tail,

\begin{equation} F_\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{K}) \simeq A\,\mathcal{K}^{-a} \quad (\mathcal{K} \to 0), \qquad a = \nu - (\beta - 2) = \tfrac{7}{2} - \beta_\mathrm{c} - \beta , \tag{T:2.13} \end{equation}

which diverges for \(a > 0\); a profile with an outer slope of exactly 3 carries a logarithmic factor beside the power. By Eq. (T:2.3), the part of the tail below an energy \(q\) contributes to the auxiliary density at \(P > q\) the amount

\begin{equation} \varrho_\mathrm{aux}^{\rm tail}(P) = \frac{2\pi^{3/2}\,\Gamma(1-\beta_\mathrm{c})}{\Gamma(3/2-\beta_\mathrm{c})}\; 2^{\,\nu-1} A\, P^{\,\nu-a}\; B\!\left(\frac{q}{P};\, 1-a,\, \nu\right), \tag{T:2.14} \end{equation}

where \(B(x; p, r) = \int_0^x t^{p-1}(1-t)^{r-1}\,\mathrm{d} t\) is the incomplete Beta function. The integral converges for \(a < 1\). For \(a \geq 1\) the density integral diverges at \(\mathcal{K} = 0\), and the untruncated model does not exist; a truncated model does exist, since the truncated DF of Chiang+(2026d), Eq. (B27) vanishes at its lower end.

T:2.2.8 Behavior of the inversion at the anisotropy radius of a Type II model#

For Type II models, \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux} \propto w^{1-\beta_\mathrm{c}}\) as \(w \equiv 1 - R^2/R_\mathrm{a}^2 \to 0\), and \(w\) is linear in \(P - P(R_\mathrm{a})\) there. Hence

\begin{equation} \frac{\mathrm{d}^m\varrho_\mathrm{aux}}{\mathrm{d} P^m} \propto |P - P(R_\mathrm{a})|^{\,1-\beta_\mathrm{c}-m} = |P - P(R_\mathrm{a})|^{\,n - 1/2}, \tag{T:2.15} \end{equation}

which diverges at \(P(R_\mathrm{a})\) for \(n < 1/2\). The divergence is approached from inside \(R_\mathrm{a}\) and does not depend on how \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}\) is continued beyond it (Section T:3.1). For OM Type II, \(n = 1/2\) and the factor is a polynomial, so the conditions of Eq. (T:2.1) hold. For COM Type II with integer \(\beta_\mathrm{c}\), \(n = 1/2\) as well. Where \(1 - \beta_\mathrm{c}\) is odd, the continued factor \(\mathrm{sign}(w)\,|w|^{1-\beta_\mathrm{c}}\) equals \(w^{1-\beta_\mathrm{c}}\), a polynomial, and the conditions hold. Where \(1 - \beta_\mathrm{c}\) is even (\(\beta_\mathrm{c} = -1, -3, \dots\)), the continuation makes \(\mathrm{d}^m\varrho_\mathrm{aux}/\mathrm{d} P^m\) jump at \(P(R_\mathrm{a})\), so the first condition of Eq. (T:2.1) fails there. Unlike the divergence for \(n < 1/2\), this failure is set by the continuation. A truncated model with \(R_\mathrm{t} < R_\mathrm{a}\) does not contain the point. At \(R_\mathrm{t} = R_\mathrm{a}\) the point is the model’s edge, and the code admits only \(\mathcal{K}_\mathrm{t} > P(R_\mathrm{a})\), that is, \(R_\mathrm{t} < R_\mathrm{a}\).

T:2.3 Evaluating the inversion at each tabulated energy#

T:2.3.1 Choice of the derivative order and of the inversion form#

The form of the inversion is fixed from the model parameters alone, before any integral is evaluated.

  1. Order pair. From \(\beta_\mathrm{c}\) the code forms the pair \((m, n)\) of Eq. (T:0.1). For \(n > 1/2\) it replaces the pair by

    \begin{equation} (j, n') = (m+1,\; n-1), \tag{T:2.16} \end{equation}

    so that the kernel exponent always satisfies \(|n'| \leq 1/2\); otherwise \((j, n') = (m, n)\). With the higher order, the surface term can diverge as \(P \to 0\) (Section T:2.2.6) and cancel against the integral, so a replaced pair is evaluated in the general form, Chiang+(2026d), Eq. (B22), on every row.

  2. Half-integer \(\beta_\mathrm{c}\). For \(n = 0\) the closed form of Eq. (T:2.2) is used, and no quadrature is performed.

  3. Interior point. For an untruncated COM Type II model, the point \(P(R_\mathrm{a})\) and the exponent of Eq. (T:2.15) are known in closed form. Rows with \(\mathcal{K} \leq P(R_\mathrm{a})\) take the form that items 1, 4 and 5 fix; rows above it take the general form, with the lower integral of Eq. (T:2.18) split at \(P(R_\mathrm{a})\) and evaluated on either side by a Gauss-Jacobi rule in \(\ln P\) that carries the exponent as its weight (Section T:2.3.3). For odd integer \(1-\beta_\mathrm{c}\) the factor is a polynomial, and there is no such point.

  4. An unbounded outer end. Where \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}^{(j)}\) is unbounded at \(P = 0\), the rows above a stated energy use the general form.

  5. All other rows use the form with the surface term.

Applying the same operator one derivative higher gives \(\mathrm{d} F_\mathcal{K}/\mathrm{d}\mathcal{K}\) on the same rows, so the table carries an exact slope column.

T:2.3.2 The lowest tabulated energy#

The table of \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}\) and its derivatives starts at a floor \(q_0 > 0\), not at \(P = 0\). Splitting the integral of the general form at \(q_0\) and integrating its upper part by parts gives, with no assumption about the behavior at \(P = 0\),

\begin{equation} \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{K}}\int_0^{\mathcal{K}}\frac{\varrho_\mathrm{aux}^{(j)}(P)}{(\mathcal{K}-P)^{n}}\,\mathrm{d} P = \int_{q_0}^{\mathcal{K}}\frac{\varrho_\mathrm{aux}^{(j+1)}(P)}{(\mathcal{K}-P)^{n}}\,\mathrm{d} P + \frac{\varrho_\mathrm{aux}^{(j)}(q_0)}{(\mathcal{K}-q_0)^{n}} - n\int_0^{q_0}\frac{\varrho_\mathrm{aux}^{(j)}(P)}{(\mathcal{K}-P)^{n+1}}\,\mathrm{d} P , \tag{T:2.17} \end{equation}

where \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}^{(j)} \equiv {\mathrm{d}}^j\varrho_\mathrm{aux}/\mathrm{d} P^j\). The first two terms need only the table. The third term is the contribution of the stretch below the floor. In Method I it is evaluated in the floor pair \((m, n)\) from the exact derivatives of Section T:1.2.1, over \([P_\mathrm{min}, q_0]\) with \(P_\mathrm{min} = 10^{-42}\): on Gauss-Legendre nodes in \(\ln P\) up to \(q_0/10\) and on Gauss-Legendre nodes in \(w = (q_0 - P)^{1-n}\) above it, integrated once by parts (or, where \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}^{(m)}\) is unbounded at \(P = 0\), as written), with \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}^{(m)}(P_\mathrm{min})\) standing for the stretch below \(P_\mathrm{min}\); so the floor of the table is not the floor of the model. A half-integer \(\beta_\mathrm{c}\) needs no completion, and a model whose outer end is bounded and whose tail exponent is at least 1 has none. In Method II it is the closed form of Section T:2.6. The floor is \(q_0 = 10^{-10}\) in units of the central potential. An untruncated table’s rows start at the first grid energy at or above \(2\,q_0\), and the nodes below the written floor (Section T:2.5) are written with them; where the model has no such nodes, the rows start at \(q_0\).

T:2.3.3 Splitting the integral of the general form#

Rows that use the general form (Section T:2.3.1) split the integral at an interior point \(c < \mathcal{K}\). Below \(c\) the derivative is taken under the integral sign; above \(c\) the integral is integrated by parts:

\begin{equation} \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{K}}\int_{q_0}^{\mathcal{K}}\frac{\varrho_\mathrm{aux}^{(j)}(P)}{(\mathcal{K}-P)^{n}}\,\mathrm{d} P = -n\int_{q_0}^{c}\frac{\varrho_\mathrm{aux}^{(j)}(P)}{(\mathcal{K}-P)^{n+1}}\,\mathrm{d} P + \frac{\varrho_\mathrm{aux}^{(j)}(c)}{(\mathcal{K}-c)^{n}} + \int_{c}^{\mathcal{K}}\frac{\varrho_\mathrm{aux}^{(j+1)}(P)}{(\mathcal{K}-P)^{n}}\,\mathrm{d} P . \tag{T:2.18} \end{equation}

The right-hand side requires \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}^{(j)}\) only on \([q_0, c]\), where it is bounded except at an interior point, and \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}^{(j+1)}\) only above \(c\). For a replaced pair, Eq. (T:2.16), this avoids \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}^{(m+1)}\) at the floor, which the form with the surface term would require and which can diverge as \(P \to 0\). For rows above the interior point of a COM Type II model, \(c\) lies above \(P(R_\mathrm{a})\), and the lower integral is evaluated on the two sides of that point with a rule that carries as its weight the exponent \(1-\beta_\mathrm{c}-j\) of \(\mathrm{d}^j\varrho_\mathrm{aux}/\mathrm{d}P^j\) at \(P(R_\mathrm{a})\), which is Eq. (T:2.15) at the order \(j\) in use: \(n - 1/2\) for a pair that is not replaced and \(n - 3/2\) for a replaced one.

T:2.3.4 Quadrature of the inversion integral#

Each row is integrated on three sub-intervals: from the floor to one tenth of the split point, on Gauss-Legendre nodes in \(\ln P\); from there to the split point, on Gauss-Legendre nodes in \(P\); and from the split point to \(\mathcal{K}\), where the kernel is singular. The split point is \(c = 0.9\,\mathcal{K}\) on the rows of \(F_\mathcal{K}\) that use the surface form, and the largest grid energy at or below \(0.9\,\mathcal{K}\) on the rows of \(\mathrm{d}F_\mathcal{K}/\mathrm{d}\mathcal{K}\), on every row of a replaced pair and on the rows of an unbounded outer end. On the rows above the interior point of a COM Type II model, \(c = \max(0.9\,\mathcal{K}, [P(R_\mathrm{a}) + \mathcal{K}]/2)\), moved down to the largest grid energy at or below it where that energy lies at least halfway from \(P(R_\mathrm{a})\) to \(c\), and the stretch below \(c\) is the two Gauss-Jacobi pieces of Section T:2.3.3 in place of the first two sub-intervals. On the last sub-interval the integration variable is \(v\), with

\begin{equation} P = P_0 - (P_0 - \mathcal{K})\,e^{y}, \qquad y = v^{k}, \qquad k = \frac{1}{1-n} . \tag{T:2.19} \end{equation}

Near the upper limit, \(\mathcal{K} - P \simeq (P_0 - \mathcal{K})\,y\), so that

\begin{equation} (\mathcal{K} - P)^{-n}\,\mathrm{d} P \;\propto\; y^{-n}\,\mathrm{d} y = k\,v^{\,k(1-n)-1}\,\mathrm{d} v = k\,\mathrm{d} v , \tag{T:2.20} \end{equation}

and the kernel singularity is removed for every exponent that the order pair allows. Away from the upper limit, \(\ln(P_0 - P)\) is a power of \(v\), so Gauss-Legendre nodes in \(v\) are graded logarithmically in \(P_0 - P\), which resolves an integrand that steepens as a power of \(P_0 - P\) toward the center.

T:2.4 The energy grid#

The DF is tabulated on a grid of energies in five zones, from the floor to the gap below the central potential: zone A, logarithmic in \(\mathcal{K}\) from the floor; zone AB, a buffer; zone B, linear (where it starts below \(0.01\,P_0\), logarithmic at 120 nodes per decade up to \(0.05\,P_0\) and linear above); zone BC, a buffer; and zone C, logarithmic in \(P_0 - \mathcal{K}\), cut at the gap, whose node is added. An untruncated COM Type II model with an interior point (Section T:2.3.1) adds nodes around \(P(R_\mathrm{a})\): a band of half-width \(P(R_\mathrm{a})/2\) at a fifth of the local spacing, and pairs at \(P(R_\mathrm{a})\,(1 \pm 10^{-k/4})\), \(k = 0, \dots, 24\), within twice the local spacing. A buffer carries the step geometrically from the spacing on one side to the spacing on the other. Its number of steps is the one whose total comes closest to the width of the buffer, and the steps are rescaled to sum to that width exactly, so that no jump in spacing enters the rows. The linear zone and the two logarithmic zones take their row counts from the settings, and the logarithmic zones extend over a stated fraction of the central potential. An OM Type I model places zones A and AB around the potential of its anisotropy radius; an OM Type II or COM model places them around the feature that a pilot inversion on a coarse grid finds: the least value of its DF below a stated energy, among the nodes where the DF exceeds \(10^{-3}\) of its median positive value, refined by a parabola through that node and its two neighbors in that set (where the DF crosses zero, this is the least value above that threshold, not the crossing). Where no such feature lies between \(10^{3}\,q_0\) and that energy, the standard zones are used. The upper end is never moved. The grid starts at the floor \(q_0\); the energies below it are those of the completion below the floor (Section T:2.5), where the rule below the table takes over (Section T:2.5.4). Energies that coincide in double precision are merged into one node, so the grid is strictly increasing.

T:2.5 The distribution function outside the tabulated energies#

T:2.5.1 The highest tabulated energy#

The inversion’s rows reach the last energy of the grid, a gap of \(10^{-9}\) below \(P_0\), unless the exact derivatives required by the inversion cannot be formed that deep, or the host floor or the DF’s ceiling stops them first (below). Each term \(C_s u^{s}\) of the central series of \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}\) gives \(\mathrm{d}^N\varrho_\mathrm{aux}/\mathrm{d} P^N = (-1)^N C_s\, s(s-1)\cdots(s-N+1)\,u^{s-N}\), which exceeds the largest double below \(u = (|C_s\,s\cdots(s-N+1)|/\mathrm{DBL\_MAX})^{1/(N-s)}\) for \(s < N\). The largest such depth over the terms and over the orders that the form needs is the top limit of the table, and it is zero where no term grows. The rows end at the shallowest of three depths: that depth, the depth at the smallest radius whose square the host represents (Section T:0.4), and, where the center’s closed form is a power \(A\,u^{\kappa}\) with \(\kappa < 0\) and \(A > 0\) on the regular or the critical divergent branch of an isotropic, constant-anisotropy or Type I model, the depth at which that form reaches the package’s ceiling \(10^{300}\) on the DF; the closed form of the center owns the energies above. Where no closed form covers the top, a row above the ceiling is refused with an error that states the ceiling and the first such row. Where the leading exponent of the series vanishes (\(\gamma = 2\beta_\mathrm{c}\) at the center), there is no closed form to join to, and a model whose host floor alone would cut its table is refused with the reason stated.

T:2.5.2 Between the highest tabulated energy and P₀#

The table of \(F_\mathcal{K}\) ends at a top node, a small depth \(u_{\rm top}\) below the center. Between that node and the center, the DF is continued by its central form, Section T:2.2.1 to Section T:2.2.4, which is computed from the model alone and stored with the table. The stored form consists of the leading singular term \(S_1(u)\) and the next one, \(S_2(u)\), each a power or a logarithm with the coefficient of Eq. (T:2.5) or Eq. (T:2.6). The coefficients of \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}\) that they need come from the exact expansion of Section T:1.1, carried to the second power that is not a non-negative integer. No exponent is fitted to the table, and the amplitude is the closed form wherever the center states one.

What remains of \(F_\mathcal{K}\) once the two singular terms are removed is the sum of a part analytic in \(u\) and the higher singular terms, each smaller than \(S_1\) by the factor \(u^{\kappa_i - \kappa_1}\), with \(\kappa_i\) the exponent of the \(i\)-th singular term; held at its value at the top node, it errs relative to \(F_\mathcal{K}\) by an amount of order \(u_{\rm top}\) (the analytic part, relative to the value at the center) or \(u_{\rm top}^{\kappa_3 - \kappa_1}\) (the third singular term), whichever is larger. It is therefore held at its value at the top node,

\begin{equation} F_\mathcal{K}(u) = S_1(u) + S_2(u) + \left[F_\mathcal{K}(u_{\rm top}) - S_1(u_{\rm top}) - S_2(u_{\rm top})\right], \qquad u < u_{\rm top} . \tag{T:2.21} \end{equation}

Two cases depart from Eq. (T:2.21). Where the leading power is positive and the inversion is local (the last row of Table T:2.1), \(F_\mathcal{K}\) vanishes at the center, and the bracket is multiplied by \(u/u_{\rm top}\) so that the continuation vanishes with it. Where the center is analytic, there is no singular term, and \(F_\mathcal{K}\) is held at its top value. For a divergent power, the expression is evaluated in logarithms relative to \(S_1\), so that a DF whose central values exceed the range of double precision is continued without overflow.

For an isotropic, constant-anisotropy or Type I model whose center is a divergent power (the regular and the critical divergent branches), the table ends at an anchor wherever the potential at the innermost radius of the settings, \(R = 10^{-9}\) by default (log10_r_min), lies more than \(10^{-10}\) below \(P_0\): the anchor is the last node at or below that potential, and at least one node below the grid’s top. Above the anchor the DF is \(A\,u^{\kappa} + B + D\,u\), with \(A\) the closed-form amplitude and \(\kappa\) the singular exponent, never fitted; \(B\) and \(D\) are fitted to the rows with \(u_\mathrm{anchor} \leq u \leq W u_\mathrm{anchor}\), \(W\) doubling from 2 until the amplitudes of the three-term fit (\(A\), \(B\), \(D\)) and of the four-term fit (with \(E\,u^2\)) agree to \(10^{-7}\), and they are kept only where the three-term fit is positive on every energy above the anchor (else \(B\) joins the form to the anchor and \(D = 0\)). A Type II model and a component on a tabulated pair carry no anchor, and the other branches carry one only where a band at the top is excluded (below); without an anchor, the DF is continued by Eq. (T:2.21) from the last row. A band of rows at the top that is not positive is excluded only where the form holds there, as judged by the local slope \(\mathrm{d}\ln F/\mathrm{d}\ln u\) against \(\kappa\).

A truncated Method I model has \(\hat{F}_\mathcal{K}(\hat{\mathcal{K}}) = F_\mathcal{K}(\hat{\mathcal{K}} + \mathcal{K}_\mathrm{t}) - F_\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{K}_\mathrm{t})\), which differs from the untruncated DF by a constant. Its singular terms and their coefficients are those of the untruncated model, with \(u\) measured from the truncated central potential.

The stored form is tested against the table that it continues. Across the top decade of the table, \(u_{\rm top} \leq u \leq 10\,u_{\rm top}\), the table less its singular terms may change only by the variation of the analytic part. A change above \(10^{-6}\) is reported with the product; a change above \(10^{-2}\) means that the stored form belongs to a different model, and the product is refused.

T:2.5.3 Below the lowest tabulated energy#

Below its first energy, the DF follows a stated rule that is recorded with the table: the tail \(A\,\mathcal{K}^{-a}[\ln(E_0/\mathcal{K})]^{-b}\) of Eq. (T:2.13) for an untruncated model whose exponent \(a\) is positive, with \(E_0 = P_0\) and with \(A\) and \(b\) chosen so that the tail joins the lowest node below the written floor in value and in logarithmic slope (\(b = 0\), and the join in value only, where those nodes carry no slope, or where there are none, at the written floor); the Taylor form \(F_1\mathcal{K} + F_2\mathcal{K}^2/2\) of a truncated Method I model at its edge, together with the energy up to which it holds; the edge law \(\mathcal{K}^{p}(C_0 + C_1\mathcal{K})\) of a Method II model (Section T:2.6.1); or zero. The integral of the rule from zero up to an energy below the table is the same set of numbers in closed form, so a density integral never truncates the DF at its table.

T:2.5.4 Interpolation between tabulated energies#

Inside the table, the base-10 logarithm of the DF is the cubic Hermite interpolant in \(\ln{\mathcal{K}}\) through the nodes with the exact slope column. Where a table carries no exact slopes (a DF from a function, Section T:2.7, and a component on a tabulated pair, Section T:2.6), the slopes are those of the not-a-knot spline through the nodes. The lookup has two entry points, by the energy and by the depth \(u = P_0 - \mathcal{K}\), and a caller asks by whichever argument it holds exactly. For example, a velocity draw near the top holds the depth \(u = (P_0 - \Psi) + \Psi x^2\) exactly, whereas the energy \(\Psi(1 - x^2)\) formed in doubles loses it. In the entry by \(u\), the cell position is formed from \(u_i - u\) with \(u_i = P_0 - \mathcal{K}_i\), which is exact for \(\mathcal{K}_i \geq P_0/2\), and the form above the table is read at \(u\) itself. The nodes and every number of the forms are arrays, so that under JAX the table is a traced argument, and two tables with the same node bucket and the same kinds of forms share one compiled function.

T:2.5.5 Checks stored with the table#

A table is written together with its checks: the stated slope column against the table’s own \(F\), the stated center against the top decade of the table, and the joins of the forms below the floor with the bottom row. A reader refuses a table whose slope column or stated center fails beyond \(10^{-2}\), or whose forms below the floor miss the bottom row beyond \(10^{-6}\). The sign report states where the DF is negative and the smallest truncation above which it is not (Section T:3.5). The validity certificate is a closed form: from the model, the central exponent and the closed-form amplitude, it states whether the columns of the table are representable in double precision, judged at the table’s own top row (the anchor, or the shallowest of the gap, the top limit, the host floor and the DF’s ceiling); a component on a tabulated pair carries none.

T:2.6 Method II: inversion of a truncated potential–density pair#

Method II is not a second pipeline. A component whose profile is the truncated profile of another model runs through the same stages; the source of the derivatives differs, and so do four steps: no slope column is made, a defective band at the top is refused rather than repaired, the stretch below the floor has the closed form below, and at \(\beta_\mathrm{c} = 1/2\) on a Method I model with \(\gamma = \alpha = 1\) the top rows are a closed-form band. The integral of the Method I model’s DF is its augmented density, the density itself for an isotropic Method I model and \(h_\mathrm{I}\,\hat\varrho\), \(h_\mathrm{I} = 1 + R^2/R_{\mathrm{a},\mathrm{I}}^2\), for an OM Type I Method I model:

\begin{equation} h_\mathrm{I}\,\hat\varrho(P) = 4\pi\sqrt{2}\int_0^{P} F(\mathcal{K})\,(P - \mathcal{K})^{1/2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{K} , \tag{T:2.22} \end{equation}

so its derivatives with respect to \(P\) are integrals of the DF and of its derivatives against \((P - \mathcal{K})^{-1/2}\), with the boundary term \(F_1 P^{-1/2}\) at order three, evaluated from the Method I model’s table, its exact slope column, and its Taylor edge; the second derivative of the DF at order three is the derivative of the monotone cubic through the slope column, the only spline that is differentiated. The auxiliary density of the Method II model is \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux} = h(R)\,\hat\varrho\) with the Method II model’s own factor, and its derivatives follow by the Leibniz rule with \(\mathrm{d} R/\mathrm{d} P\) taken from the rows. Orders up to three are served, the third only where the Method I model states its Taylor edge. An isotropic or OM Type I Method I model is served, and a Method II model of Type II must have its \(R_\mathrm{a}\) beyond the Method I model’s edge.

The grid is the five-zone grid of Section T:2.4 on the first potential of the Method I model, compact, with no extension below the floor, since the support of the Method II model ends at the Method I model’s edge. The stretch below the floor has a closed form. For each power \(P^{q}\) of the two-term edge expansion of \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}\) (Section T:2.6.1), with \(a = q - m_f\), \(b = 1 - n_f\), and \(x = q_0/\mathcal{K}\),

\begin{equation} C(n_f)\,a_q\,\frac{\Gamma(q+1)}{\Gamma(a+1)}\;\mathcal{K}^{\,a-n_f}\,x^{a}\left[{}_2F_1(a, n_f;\, a+1;\, x) - 1\right] \tag{T:2.23} \end{equation}

is the exact contribution of \([0, q_0]\) to the row at \(\mathcal{K}\), relative to the boundary form that the kernel carries, and the boundary term is the sources’ own value of \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}^{(m_f)}\) at the floor node: the Method I model’s derivatives there (in closed form from its Taylor edge where the node lies below the switch), its density from its rows, and the Method II model’s factor, by the Leibniz rule.

At \(\beta_\mathrm{c} = 1/2\) on a Method I model with \(\gamma = \alpha = 1\), the inversion is a derivative whose two terms are both of order \(1/u\) toward the center, so the rows there carry the Method I model’s accuracy multiplied by \(C/[2(\beta - 1)\,u]\), with \(\hat\varrho \simeq C/R\) at the center (\(1/(4u)\) for NFW, \(1/(3u)\) for Hernquist). Above an anchor, the rows are the closed form \(F = A\sum_{k \leq 4} a_k u^{k}\) with \(A = 1/(2\pi^2)\) (Section T:2.2.5), which is exact in the truncation through the Method I model’s central deficit. The anchor is the smaller of the series’ own range at \(10^{-6}\) and the top zone of the grid, and the median relative deviation of the computed rows from the form over the outermost decade inside it, on at least ten rows, must be within \(10^{-6}\); otherwise the anchor steps inward one decade at a time until it is, and where no decade is, the rows stand as computed and the record says so.

T:2.6.1 The Method II DF at the truncation boundary#

A density that ends as \(P^{q}\) at its edge has a DF of central anisotropy \(\beta_\mathrm{c}\) if and only if \(\beta_\mathrm{c} > 1/2 - q\), and the DF then behaves as \(\mathcal{K}^{\,q - 3/2 + \beta_\mathrm{c}}\) there. This statement holds for every profile and every anisotropy model of the package, and a Method II model that violates it is refused before any stage runs, with the admissible range stated. Below the Method I model’s switch energy, the Method I model’s DF is its Taylor form \(F_1\mathcal{K} + F_2\mathcal{K}^2/2\), so the Method I model’s augmented density near the edge is \(4\pi\sqrt{2}\,[\tfrac{4}{15}F_1 P^{5/2} + \tfrac{8}{105}F_2 P^{7/2}]\) to the order kept. The Method II model’s augmented density, with \(h = h_\mathrm{t} + H_1 P\), \(H_1 = h'(R_\mathrm{t})\,R'(P)\), and \(R' = -R_\mathrm{t}^2/M_\mathrm{t}\), is then

\begin{equation} \varrho_{\rm aug}(P) = a_{5/2}\,P^{5/2} + a_{7/2}\,P^{7/2} + \mathcal{O}(P^{9/2}) . \tag{T:2.24} \end{equation}

Each power inverts to a power, so the Method II model’s DF below its switch is the edge law \(\mathcal{K}^{p}(C_0 + C_1\mathcal{K})\) with \(p = 1 + \beta_\mathrm{c}\), and the same coefficients give the floor terms of Eq. (T:2.23).

T:2.7 A distribution function supplied as input#

A DF obtained elsewhere, from a fitting function or from the output of another code, is checked in the same way. Its values on the energy grid that the inversion would place for the component enter at the point where the rows of the inversion would enter, and everything downstream is unchanged: the forms beyond the table, the sign report, the checks and the result’s record, which carries no inversion record and no rows. Such a table has no exact slope column, which its report states, and a value that is not finite is refused, with the first such energy stated. Truncation needs the rows of the inversion, which such a table does not carry, and it is refused with the reason stated.
