.. _tn-sec-principles:

T:0 Principles and conventions
==============================

.. _tn-sec-notation:

T:0.1 Notation
--------------

We adopt the notation of |paper|. The relative potential is :math:`P`, with the central value :math:`P_0`; the energy-like variable is :math:`\mathcal{K}`; the distribution function (DF) is :math:`F(\mathcal{K}, L) = F_\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{K})\,L^{-2\beta_\mathrm{c}}`; the density is the double power law of |paper|, Eq. (5) with exponents :math:`(\alpha, \beta, \gamma)`; and :math:`\varrho_\mathrm{aux}` is the auxiliary density, which for the (C)OM models is given by |paper|, Eq. (B24). Three derived quantities are used throughout these notes. The first is the order of the inversion,

.. _tn-eq-order:

.. math::
   :nowrap:

   \begin{equation}
     \nu \equiv \tfrac{3}{2} - \beta_\mathrm{c} = m + n, \qquad m \equiv \lfloor \nu \rfloor, \qquad 0 \leq n < 1 ,
     \tag{T:0.1}
   \end{equation}

and the other two are the exponent of the inner expansion and the depth below the center,

.. _tn-eq-delta-u:

.. math::
   :nowrap:

   \begin{equation}
     \delta \equiv \frac{\alpha}{2-\gamma}, \qquad u \equiv P_0 - \mathcal{K} .
     \tag{T:0.2}
   \end{equation}

Internally, every model is expressed in the units of |paper|, in which the scale radius and the central potential of the untruncated profile are both unity; a truncated model therefore has :math:`P_0 = 1 - \mathcal{K}_\mathrm{t}`. The depth :math:`u` is carried as a variable of its own wherever the difference :math:`P_0 - \mathcal{K}` would lose significant digits.

.. _tn-sec-rules:

T:0.2 Rules derived from the inputs, not fitted constants
---------------------------------------------------------

Every numerical choice in the package is either a function of its inputs (the model and the settings tier) or a property of the host arithmetic; none is a fitted constant. Wherever a threshold appears, it is derived from the problem at hand. For example, the depth at which a table ends is the depth at which the exact derivatives leave the range of a double (:ref:`Section T:2.5.2 <tn-sec-top>`); the radius out to which the inner series is trusted is the radius at which its next term reaches the tolerance (:ref:`Section T:1.2.2 <tn-sec-series>`); and the radius of trust of an untruncated product follows from the offset of the solver (:ref:`Section T:3.7 <tn-sec-trust>`). A settings tier changes only node counts and tolerances, and a runtime option (verbosity, timing, workers, device) changes no number at all. Whatever cannot be computed is refused with a named reason, and the object computed so far is returned as the result of the error; nothing is silently approximated.

.. _tn-sec-exact:

T:0.3 Exact arithmetic
----------------------

Wherever a quantity has a closed form, we use that closed form, and we form its derivatives symbolically rather than by finite differences. The derivatives of the auxiliary density with respect to the potential are built by computer algebra from the closed-form density, enclosed mass, and anisotropy factor (:ref:`Section T:1.2.1 <tn-sec-derivatives>`). They are evaluated in double precision where that evaluation is stable, and in arbitrary precision where it suffers from cancellation: at small and at large radii, at high orders far out, and within a band around :math:`R_\mathrm{a}` for a Type II model. The coefficients of the central series of :math:`\varrho_\mathrm{aux}` are exact (:ref:`Section T:2.2.1 <tn-sec-center>`); the amplitude of the central term of the DF is a closed form; and the enclosed mass is the incomplete Beta function, evaluated in arbitrary precision where its closed form fails. Every exact form is certified against an arbitrary-precision reference in the test suite (:ref:`Section T:8 <tn-sec-verification>`).

.. _tn-sec-host:

T:0.4 Host floors
-----------------

Three limits of double precision shape every table, and all three are read from the host rather than assumed. The energy grid has a floor at :math:`q_0 = 10^{-10}` in units of :math:`P_0`, and it ends at a gap of :math:`10^{-9}` below :math:`P_0`, which is the depth at which energies are still separable from the center; no node lies above this gap, and the closed form of the center carries the DF beyond the last node (:ref:`Section T:2.5.2 <tn-sec-top>`). The top of a table may end deeper still, at the shallower of two depths: the depth at which the exact derivatives needed by the inversion exceed the largest double, and the depth at the smallest radius whose square the host can represent (:math:`2^{-537}` with subnormals). Both depths are computed from the model alone. Near the center, the depth is formed without subtraction, from the inner series (:ref:`Section T:1.2.2 <tn-sec-series>`) and, in the lookup, from node differences that are exact by Sterbenz's lemma (:ref:`Section T:2.5.4 <tn-sec-lookup>`).

.. _tn-sec-tiers:

T:0.5 Accuracy tiers, reports, and refusals
-------------------------------------------

Every product is judged against three levels of relative error: a goal of :math:`10^{-6}`, a ceiling of :math:`10^{-4}`, and a hard limit of :math:`10^{-2}`. A check within the goal is silent. A check above the goal is returned with a warning that stays attached to the result and to everything computed from it. A check beyond the hard limit refuses the result, unless the settings explicitly ask for it. Every result carries a report with a fixed set of checks (:ref:`Section T:8.1 <tn-sec-reports>`); a derived result holds its own checks together with the worst tier of its parents, never a copy of their reports. A component of a product that feeds a projected quantity is held to one tenth of the goal, which is its share of the error budget of what is computed from it.

Two settings tiers are tested, the default tier and a quick tier for exploration. Both are held to the same checks by the test suite, and the quick tier changes only node counts. The CPU-only mode gives the same numbers on every machine, and every file records the backend that produced it. A stage whose wall time scales with a sequential count (the steps of a solve, or rows computed one at a time) is latency-bound on a GPU, and it therefore stays on the CPU on every device; a stage whose wall time scales with a batch (the speed table, the particle draw) runs on a GPU when one is present. The rows of the line-of-sight tables are computed in a pool of CPU processes, one row per process, and never reach a GPU. This classification rests on measured scaling, with no margin constant.
