# T:1 The potential–density pair and the auxiliary density

Source: https://pianistpy.com/docs/reference/technical-notes/model

## T:1.1 Asymptotic forms of the potential–radius relation

The DF near the center ([Sections T:2.2.1](https://pianistpy.com/docs/reference/technical-notes/df#tn-sec-center) and [T:2.5.1](https://pianistpy.com/docs/reference/technical-notes/df#tn-sec-top-limit)) requires the auxiliary density \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}(R)\) as an explicit series in the depth \(u\) of [Eq. (T:0.2)](https://pianistpy.com/docs/reference/technical-notes/principles#tn-eq-delta-u). To this aim, starting from the inner (\(P \to P_0\)) analytic expansion of the potential for double-power-law density profiles given in [Chiang+(2026d)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28758), Eq. (A11), we have \(u \propto R^{2-\gamma}\,[1 + \mathcal{O}(R^{\alpha})]\), which in turn gives \(R^{\alpha} \propto u^{\delta}\), where the exponent \(\delta\) is as defined in [Eq. (T:0.2)](https://pianistpy.com/docs/reference/technical-notes/principles#tn-eq-delta-u). Hence, any function of \(R\) that is a series in \(R^{\alpha}\) can be recast into a series in \(u^{\delta}\):

\begin{equation}
  R^{\alpha} \propto u^{\delta}\left[1 + \mathcal{O}\!\left(u^{\delta}\right)\right] .
  \tag{T:1.1}
\end{equation}
Explicitly, the inner expansion gives \(u = c_u R^{2-\gamma}\,[1 + D(R)]\) with \(D\) a series in \(R^{\alpha}\). Here \(c_u = C/[(2-\gamma)(3-\gamma)]\), and \(C\) is the amplitude of the density fixed by the unit normalization of [Widrow (2000)](https://doi.org/10.1086/317367) as defined in [Chiang+(2026d)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28758), Eq. (A6). To express \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}\) in terms of \(u\) rather than \(R\), the above relation \(u(R)\) is solved for \(R\) order by order, giving \(R = t\,[1 + S(t)]\) with \(S\) a series in \(t^{\alpha}\). Here \(t \equiv (u/c_u)^{1/(2-\gamma)}\) is the leading-order estimate of \(R\) from the depth, with \(t(u \to 0) \to R\). Substituting \(R(t)\) into the density and into the separability factor of \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}\) then yields \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}\) as a series in \(u\) whose coefficients are finite closed-form combinations of generalized binomial coefficients and rational functions of \((\alpha, \beta, \gamma)\). Every coefficient is therefore exact at every order, without requiring any numerical fitting. PIANISTpy computes these coefficients by truncated power-series arithmetic (Cauchy products, binomial series and series reversion) and keeps every term up to the power \(3 \times \max(\alpha, 2)\) in \(t\) beyond the leading power, i.e., up to \(u^{3[\max(\alpha,2)/(2-\gamma)]}\) beyond the leading power of \(u\). The exact range in \(\mathcal{K}\) over which the analytic truncated series is used instead of the numerical tabulated inversion is discussed in [Sections T:2.2.1](https://pianistpy.com/docs/reference/technical-notes/df#tn-sec-center) and [T:2.5.2](https://pianistpy.com/docs/reference/technical-notes/df#tn-sec-top).

In parallel, the outer (\(P \to 0\)) asymptotic expansion of the potential of untruncated double-power-law density profiles for \(\beta > 2\) scales as (cf. [Chiang+(2026d)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28758), Eq. (A13))

\begin{equation}
  P \propto \begin{cases} R^{-1} & (\beta > 3),\\
   R^{-1}\ln R & (\beta = 3),\\
   R^{-(\beta-2)} & (2 < \beta < 3). \end{cases}
  \tag{T:1.2}
\end{equation}

## T:1.2 Derivatives of the auxiliary density

### T:1.2.1 Exact derivatives with respect to the potential

For the Cuddeford inversion, which requires the derivatives \(\mathrm{d}^k\varrho_\mathrm{aux}/\mathrm{d}P^k\) ([Section T:2.1](https://pianistpy.com/docs/reference/technical-notes/df#tn-sec-forms)), PIANISTpy uses the chain rule ([Chiang+(2026d)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28758), Appendix C1.1)

\begin{equation}
  \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d} P} = \left(\frac{\mathrm{d} P}{\mathrm{d} R}\right)^{-1}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d} R} = -\frac{R^2}{M(R)}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d} R},
  \tag{T:1.3}
\end{equation}
to feed the exact analytical expressions in \(R\) of the enclosed mass \(M(R)\) ([Chiang+(2026d)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28758), Eq. (A7)) and of the auxiliary density \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}(R) = \varrho(R)/g(R)\) into the symbolic derivatives of [Section T:0.3](https://pianistpy.com/docs/reference/technical-notes/principles#tn-sec-exact), bypassing numerical finite differences. Here \(g(R)\) is the separability factor defined in [Chiang+(2026d)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28758), Eq. (B44). Specifically, the expressions are evaluated pointwise at \(R(P)\), found by bisection in \(\ln R\) on the monotonic \(P(R)\) and, near the center, on the depth \(u\) of [Eq. (T:0.2)](https://pianistpy.com/docs/reference/technical-notes/principles#tn-eq-delta-u) as given by the inner series ([Section T:1.2.2](https://pianistpy.com/docs/reference/technical-notes/model#tn-sec-series)). Arbitrary-precision arithmetic ([Section T:0.3](https://pianistpy.com/docs/reference/technical-notes/principles#tn-sec-exact)) replaces the default double precision in the three regimes that suffer from numerical cancellation, at small radii (\(R < 10^{-2}\), at every \(k\)), at large radii (\(R > 10^{6}\) for \(k \geq 1\), \(R > 10^{2}\) for \(k \geq 3\)) and, for a Type II model, in the band \(|1 - (R/R_\mathrm{a})^2| < 0.75\) around \(R_\mathrm{a}\) (\(k \geq 2\)), as well as wherever the double-precision value is not finite.

For Method II, the input is a tabulated potential–density pair, so \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}\) has no closed form. PIANISTpy instead uses the integral representation of the truncated density in terms of the Method I model’s DF, [Eq. (T:2.22)](https://pianistpy.com/docs/reference/technical-notes/df#tn-eq-method1-density): the derivatives of \(\hat\varrho\) with respect to \(\hat{P}\) are exact integrals of that DF and of its derivatives, evaluated by quadrature, and those of \(\hat\varrho_\mathrm{aux} = \hat\varrho/g\) follow by the Leibniz rule, without any interpolation of \(\hat\varrho_\mathrm{aux}\) ([Section T:2.6](https://pianistpy.com/docs/reference/technical-notes/df#tn-sec-tabulated)).

Early prototypes of PIANISTpy used the commonly adopted alternative numerical approach of directly interpolating the tabulated \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}\) in \(P\) and differentiating the interpolating spline. There, a spline of degree \(p\) that interpolates a smooth function to \(\mathcal{O}(h^{p+1})\) in the knot spacing \(h\) reproduces its \(k\)-th derivative to no better than \(\mathcal{O}(h^{p+1-k})\) (e.g., [de Boor 1978](https://link.springer.com/book/9780387953663)), an error \(\epsilon\) in a tabulated value of \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}\) enters the \(k\)-th derivative as \(\epsilon/h^{k}\), and any jump in the knot spacing degrades the conditioning of the spline, exciting spurious oscillations in the derivatives of order two and higher even for a smooth and monotone \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}\). Empirically, such amplified errors were found catastrophic and irreducible in the DFs that require the high-order derivatives of the Cuddeford inversion ([Section T:2.1](https://pianistpy.com/docs/reference/technical-notes/df#tn-sec-forms)). The current implementation of PIANISTpy thus takes no spline-based derivative of \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}\) ([Chiang+(2026d)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28758), Appendix C1.1; [Fornberg 1988](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-1988-0935077-0)).

### T:1.2.2 The numerical join radius of the inner series

The analytical inner series expansions of the enclosed mass and the depth \(u\) ([Chiang+(2026d)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28758), Eqs. (A12) and (A11); [Section T:1.1](https://pianistpy.com/docs/reference/technical-notes/model#tn-sec-pair)), summed to four terms in \(R^{\alpha}\), are used from the innermost tabulated radius (`log10_r_min`) out to the “join radius,” the largest radius at which the relative truncation error of the depth series stays below \(10^{-7}\). The exact value of this join radius depends on, and only on, \((\alpha, \beta, \gamma)\). For a truncated model, the Poisson solver uses the same analytical inner series over the same radial range (see also [Section T:3.4](https://pianistpy.com/docs/reference/technical-notes/truncation#tn-sec-fills)).

## T:1.3 Interpolation of the tabulated truncated profile

A truncated model in PIANISTpy is represented by a table of columns (the potential \(\hat{P}\), the density \(\hat\varrho\) and the enclosed mass \(M\)) on the tabulated grid of radii \(R\), accessed by the same scheme, detailed below, in every downstream numerical evaluation. First, \(\ln\hat{P}(R)\) and \(\ln M(R)\) are interpolated by piecewise cubic Hermite polynomials in \(\ln R\), with the local slopes evaluated pointwise by the Poisson equation \(\mathrm{d}\ln \hat{P}/\mathrm{d}\ln R = -M/(R\hat{P})\) and the mass integral \(\mathrm{d}\ln M/\mathrm{d}\ln R = R^3\hat\varrho/M\), bypassing the use of finite differences. Second, \(\ln\hat\varrho(R)\), which has no such closed-form slope, is instead interpolated by a cubic spline in \(\ln R\). Third, for the radius at a given potential, \(R(\hat{P})\), instead of a separate interpolation, PIANISTpy directly inverts the piecewise Hermite polynomial of \(\hat{P}(R)\) by root-finding bisection in \(\ln R\), such that \(R(\hat{P})\) and \(\hat{P}(R)\) are mutual inverses to rounding. For near-center numerical evaluations, \(R \to 0\) inside the innermost tabulated radius, PIANISTpy extrapolates \(\hat{P}\) and \(M\) inward as power laws with their respective slopes at the innermost row, and \(\hat\varrho\) by the end polynomial of its spline.

For a finitely truncated model, dedicated numerical treatments are needed when accessing the table near the truncation boundary \(\hat\varrho = 0\) (\(R = R_\mathrm{t}\)). The “edge” of the model denotes the outermost row of the table, with \(\hat{P} = 0\) and \(M = M_\mathrm{t}\), where \(\ln\hat{P}\) and \(\ln\hat\varrho\) have no finite value, so the aforementioned logarithmic interpolation ends at the penultimate row. Between these two rows, PIANISTpy computes the potential in the point-mass (i.e., Keplerian) form \(M_\mathrm{t}(1/R - 1/R_\mathrm{t})\) and the density as the power law \(\hat\varrho \propto \hat{P}^{q}\) anchored at the penultimate row, with \(q\) the analytical exponent of the density at the edge (see [Section T:2.6.1](https://pianistpy.com/docs/reference/technical-notes/df#tn-sec-edge-law)). At and beyond \(R_\mathrm{t}\), the profile simply returns \(\hat{P} = 0\), \(\hat\varrho = 0\) and \(M = M_\mathrm{t}\); for the same physical reason, PIANISTpy accepts the evaluation of the velocity observables of [Section T:6](https://pianistpy.com/docs/reference/technical-notes/observables#tn-sec-observables), such as \(\sigma_r\) or \(\sigma_t\), only at \(R \leq R_\mathrm{t}\).
