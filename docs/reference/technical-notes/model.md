# T:1 The potential–density pair and the auxiliary density

Source: https://pianistpy.com/docs/reference/technical-notes/model

## T:1.1 Asymptotic forms of the potential–radius relation

The DF near the center ([Sections T:2.2.1](https://pianistpy.com/docs/reference/technical-notes/df#tn-sec-center) and [T:2.5.1](https://pianistpy.com/docs/reference/technical-notes/df#tn-sec-top-limit)) requires \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}(R)\) as an explicit series in the depth \(u\) of [Eq. (T:0.2)](https://pianistpy.com/docs/reference/technical-notes/principles#tn-eq-delta-u). To this aim, starting from the inner (\(P \to P_0\)) analytic expansion of the potential for double-power-law density profiles given in [Chiang+(2026d)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28758), Eq. (A11), we have \(u \propto R^{2-\gamma}\,[1 + \mathcal{O}(R^{\alpha})]\), which in turn gives \(R^{\alpha} \propto u^{\delta}\), where the exponent \(\delta\) is as defined in [Eq. (T:0.2)](https://pianistpy.com/docs/reference/technical-notes/principles#tn-eq-delta-u). Hence, any function of \(R\) that is a series in \(R^{\alpha}\) can be recast into a series in \(u^{\delta}\):

\begin{equation}
  R^{\alpha} \propto u^{\delta}\left[1 + \mathcal{O}\!\left(u^{\delta}\right)\right] .
  \tag{T:1.1}
\end{equation}
Explicitly, the inner expansion gives \(u = c_u R^{2-\gamma}\,[1 + D(R)]\) with \(D\) a series in \(R^{\alpha}\). Here \(c_u = C/[(2-\gamma)(3-\gamma)]\), and \(C\) is the amplitude of the density fixed by the unit normalization of [Widrow (2000)](https://doi.org/10.1086/317367) as defined in [Chiang+(2026d)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28758), Eq. (A6). To express \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}\) in terms of \(u\) rather than \(R\), the above relation \(u(R)\) is solved for \(R\) order by order, giving \(R = t\,[1 + S(t)]\) with \(S\) a series in \(t^{\alpha}\). Here \(t \equiv (u/c_u)^{1/(2-\gamma)}\) is the leading-order estimate of \(R\) from the depth, with \(t(u \to 0) \to R\). Substituting \(R(t)\) into the density and into the anisotropy factor of \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}\) then yields \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}\) as a series in \(u\) whose coefficients are finite closed-form combinations of generalized binomial coefficients and rational functions of \((\alpha, \beta, \gamma)\). Every coefficient is therefore exact at every order, without requiring any numerical fitting. PIANISTpy computes these coefficients by truncated power-series arithmetic (Cauchy products, binomial series and series reversion) and keeps every term up to the power \(3 \times \max(\alpha, 2)\) in \(t\) beyond the leading power, i.e., up to \(u^{3[\max(\alpha,2)/(2-\gamma)]}\) beyond the leading power of \(u\). The exact range in \(\mathcal{K}\) over which the analytic truncated series is used instead of the numerical tabulated inversion is discussed in [Sections T:2.2.1](https://pianistpy.com/docs/reference/technical-notes/df#tn-sec-center) and [T:2.5.2](https://pianistpy.com/docs/reference/technical-notes/df#tn-sec-top).

In parallel, the outer (\(P \to 0\)) asymptotic expansion of the potential of untruncated double-power-law density profiles for \(\beta > 2\) scales as (cf. [Chiang+(2026d)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28758), Eq. (A13))

\begin{equation}
  P \propto \begin{cases} R^{-1} & (\beta > 3),\\
   R^{-1}\ln R & (\beta = 3),\\
   R^{-(\beta-2)} & (2 < \beta < 3). \end{cases}
  \tag{T:1.2}
\end{equation}

## T:1.2 Derivatives of the auxiliary density

### T:1.2.1 Exact derivatives with respect to the potential

The Cuddeford inversion requires the derivatives \(\mathrm{d}^k\varrho_\mathrm{aux}/\mathrm{d}P^k\) up to the order \(k = m + 1\), with \((m, n)\) the order pair of [Eq. (T:0.1)](https://pianistpy.com/docs/reference/technical-notes/principles#tn-eq-order); at \(n = 0\) only up to \(k = m\), and up to \(k = m + 2\) where the order is raised by one to keep the kernel exponent at or below \(1/2\) in magnitude (the replaced order pair of [Eq. (T:2.16)](https://pianistpy.com/docs/reference/technical-notes/df#tn-eq-unified-pair), [Section T:2.3.1](https://pianistpy.com/docs/reference/technical-notes/df#tn-sec-df-form)). Method I, the phase-space truncation of the DF of [Chiang+(2026d)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28758), Section 2.2, reads one order higher still, as the slope of its source and as the source of the slope of the DF; its profile is analytical, so these derivatives are exact. The operator

\begin{equation}
  \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d} P} = -\frac{R^2}{M(R)}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d} R},
  \tag{T:1.3}
\end{equation}
with the closed-form enclosed mass \(M(R)\) of [Chiang+(2026d)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28758), Eq. (A7), is applied symbolically to the closed-form auxiliary density \(\varrho_\mathrm{aux}(R) = \varrho(R)\,h(R)\), where \(h = R^{2\beta_\mathrm{c}}(1 \pm R^2/R_\mathrm{a}^2)^{s}\) is the inverse of the anisotropy factor (\(s = 1\) for OM, \(s = 1 - \beta_\mathrm{c}\) for COM, and \(s = 0\) otherwise). The result is evaluated at \(R(P)\), which we obtain by bisection in \(\ln R\) on the monotonic \(P(R)\) and, near the center, on the subtraction-free depth. The expressions are evaluated in double precision except at radii below \(10^{-2}\), at orders three and above beyond \(R = 10^{2}\), at orders one and above beyond \(R = 10^{6}\), within \(|1 - (R/R_\mathrm{a})^2| < 0.75\) for a Type II model at orders two and above, and wherever the double-precision value is not finite; there they are evaluated in arbitrary-precision arithmetic ([Section T:0.3](https://pianistpy.com/docs/reference/technical-notes/principles#tn-sec-exact)). The potential \(P(R)\) does not enter the derivatives’ expressions, which hold \(M(R)\) only; its closed form, [Chiang+(2026d)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28758), Eq. (A3), enters only through the radius \(R(P)\) at which they are evaluated. A single expression of \(h\) serves every closed-form evaluation of the factor in the package: the Method I sources, the Method II sources, the edge law, the density of the Poisson solve, and the parameter derivatives of [Section T:7](https://pianistpy.com/docs/reference/technical-notes/gradients#tn-sec-gradients); the inner series of [Section T:1.1](https://pianistpy.com/docs/reference/technical-notes/model#tn-sec-pair) and its parameter derivatives expand the same factor as a binomial series in \(R^2/R_\mathrm{a}^2\), and the central deficit of [Section T:3.4](https://pianistpy.com/docs/reference/technical-notes/truncation#tn-sec-fills) writes the factor’s logarithmic step and slope out by hand. In Method II the input is a tabulated pair, and the derivatives are integrals of the Method I model’s DF ([Section T:2.6](https://pianistpy.com/docs/reference/technical-notes/df#tn-sec-tabulated)).

Our first design for the tabulated case was a spline of \(\ln{\varrho_\mathrm{aux}}\) in \(P\) with knots placed by the equidistribution principle ([de Boor 1978](https://link.springer.com/book/9780387953663)). Its derivatives of second and higher order carried the conditioning of the spline, which grows as the square of the ratio of its largest to its smallest knot spacing, into the inversion. The exact and integral forms replaced it.

### T:1.2.2 Inner series expansions and their range of validity

Near the center, the density is its closed form, and the enclosed mass and the depth are the series of [Chiang+(2026d)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28758), Eq. (A12) and Eq. (A11), carried to four terms in \(R^{\alpha}\) and summed term by term with no subtraction from the central value; the potential is \(P_0\) less that depth. The series is trusted out to the largest radius at which its next term, relative to the depth, stays within \(10^{-7}\). This bound follows from the parameters of the profile alone, and no fixed depth is assumed. The Poisson solve of a truncated model starts from the same series, and its inner rows are filled from the series out to that join ([Section T:3.4](https://pianistpy.com/docs/reference/technical-notes/truncation#tn-sec-fills)).

## T:1.3 Interpolation of the tabulated truncated profile

The self-consistent profile of a truncated model is a table of rows (radius, potential, density, and enclosed mass), and a single object owns its reading: every stage and every reader evaluates the profile through the four functions of that object and holds no interpolant of its own. The potential and the mass are cubic Hermite interpolants in the logarithms, with the exact slopes that the rows state, \(\mathrm{d}\ln P/\mathrm{d}\ln r = -M/(rP)\) and \(\mathrm{d}\ln M/\mathrm{d}\ln r = r^3\varrho/M\) (the Poisson equation and the mass integral). The density is a cubic spline of \(\ln\varrho\) in \(\ln r\), since the rows state no density slope. The radius at a given potential is the guarded inverse of the potential interpolant. A spatially finite profile carries its edge as its last row, where the density is zero and the radius is \(R_\mathrm{t}\). On the last interval the potential takes the Kepler form \(M_\mathrm{t}(1/r - 1/R_\mathrm{t})\) and the density follows the edge law \(\varrho_L\,(P/P_L)^{q}\), with the exponent \(q\) stated by the profile; beyond \(R_\mathrm{t}\) the potential continues as \(M_\mathrm{t}(1/r - 1/R_\mathrm{t})\) and the density is zero; the package serves no reading there. Inside the first row, the interpolants continue as the power laws of the end rows. Nothing outside the domain is clipped: a radius that lies beyond the edge by a rounding error is treated as the edge, and anything further out is refused with the reason stated.
