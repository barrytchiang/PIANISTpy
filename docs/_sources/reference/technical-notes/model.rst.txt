.. _tn-sec-model:

T:1 The potential–density pair and the auxiliary density
========================================================

.. _tn-sec-pair:

T:1.1 Asymptotic forms of the potential–radius relation
-------------------------------------------------------

The DF near the center (:ref:`Sections T:2.2.1 <tn-sec-center>` and :ref:`T:2.5.1 <tn-sec-top-limit>`) requires the auxiliary density :math:`\varrho_\mathrm{aux}(R)` as an explicit series in the depth :math:`u` of :ref:`Eq. (T:0.2) <tn-eq-delta-u>`. To this aim, starting from the inner (:math:`P \to P_0`) analytic expansion of the potential for double-power-law density profiles given in |paper|, Eq. (A11), we have :math:`u \propto R^{2-\gamma}\,[1 + \mathcal{O}(R^{\alpha})]`, which in turn gives :math:`R^{\alpha} \propto u^{\delta}`, where the exponent :math:`\delta` is as defined in :ref:`Eq. (T:0.2) <tn-eq-delta-u>`. Hence, any function of :math:`R` that is a series in :math:`R^{\alpha}` can be recast into a series in :math:`u^{\delta}`:

.. _tn-eq-inner-map:

.. math::
   :nowrap:

   \begin{equation}
     R^{\alpha} \propto u^{\delta}\left[1 + \mathcal{O}\!\left(u^{\delta}\right)\right] .
     \tag{T:1.1}
   \end{equation}

Explicitly, the inner expansion gives :math:`u = c_u R^{2-\gamma}\,[1 + D(R)]` with :math:`D` a series in :math:`R^{\alpha}`. Here :math:`c_u = C/[(2-\gamma)(3-\gamma)]`, and :math:`C` is the amplitude of the density fixed by the unit normalization of `Widrow (2000) <https://doi.org/10.1086/317367>`__ as defined in |paper|, Eq. (A6). To express :math:`\varrho_\mathrm{aux}` in terms of :math:`u` rather than :math:`R`, the above relation :math:`u(R)` is solved for :math:`R` order by order, giving :math:`R = t\,[1 + S(t)]` with :math:`S` a series in :math:`t^{\alpha}`. Here :math:`t \equiv (u/c_u)^{1/(2-\gamma)}` is the leading-order estimate of :math:`R` from the depth, with :math:`t(u \to 0) \to R`. Substituting :math:`R(t)` into the density and into the separability factor of :math:`\varrho_\mathrm{aux}` then yields :math:`\varrho_\mathrm{aux}` as a series in :math:`u` whose coefficients are finite closed-form combinations of generalized binomial coefficients and rational functions of :math:`(\alpha, \beta, \gamma)`. Every coefficient is therefore exact at every order, without requiring any numerical fitting. PIANISTpy computes these coefficients by truncated power-series arithmetic (Cauchy products, binomial series and series reversion) and keeps every term up to the power :math:`3 \times \max(\alpha, 2)` in :math:`t` beyond the leading power, i.e., up to :math:`u^{3[\max(\alpha,2)/(2-\gamma)]}` beyond the leading power of :math:`u`. The exact range in :math:`\mathcal{K}` over which the analytic truncated series is used instead of the numerical tabulated inversion is discussed in :ref:`Sections T:2.2.1 <tn-sec-center>` and :ref:`T:2.5.2 <tn-sec-top>`.

In parallel, the outer (:math:`P \to 0`) asymptotic expansion of the potential of untruncated double-power-law density profiles for :math:`\beta > 2` scales as (cf. |paper|, Eq. (A13))

.. _tn-eq-outer-map:

.. math::
   :nowrap:

   \begin{equation}
     P \propto \begin{cases} R^{-1} & (\beta > 3),\\
      R^{-1}\ln R & (\beta = 3),\\
      R^{-(\beta-2)} & (2 < \beta < 3). \end{cases}
     \tag{T:1.2}
   \end{equation}

T:1.2 Derivatives of the auxiliary density
------------------------------------------

.. _tn-sec-derivatives:

T:1.2.1 Exact derivatives with respect to the potential
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

For the Cuddeford inversion, which requires the derivatives :math:`\mathrm{d}^k\varrho_\mathrm{aux}/\mathrm{d}P^k` (:ref:`Section T:2.1 <tn-sec-forms>`), PIANISTpy uses the chain rule (|paper|, Appendix C1.1)

.. _tn-eq-dp-operator:

.. math::
   :nowrap:

   \begin{equation}
     \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d} P} = \left(\frac{\mathrm{d} P}{\mathrm{d} R}\right)^{-1}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d} R} = -\frac{R^2}{M(R)}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d} R},
     \tag{T:1.3}
   \end{equation}

to feed the exact analytical expressions in :math:`R` of the enclosed mass :math:`M(R)` (|paper|, Eq. (A7)) and of the auxiliary density :math:`\varrho_\mathrm{aux}(R) = \varrho(R)/g(R)` into the symbolic derivatives of :ref:`Section T:0.3 <tn-sec-exact>`, bypassing numerical finite differences. Here :math:`g(R)` is the separability factor defined in |paper|, Eq. (B44). Specifically, the expressions are evaluated pointwise at :math:`R(P)`, found by bisection in :math:`\ln R` on the monotonic :math:`P(R)` and, near the center, on the depth :math:`u` of :ref:`Eq. (T:0.2) <tn-eq-delta-u>` as given by the inner series (:ref:`Section T:1.2.2 <tn-sec-series>`). Arbitrary-precision arithmetic (:ref:`Section T:0.3 <tn-sec-exact>`) replaces the default double precision in the three regimes that suffer from numerical cancellation, at small radii (:math:`R < 10^{-2}`, at every :math:`k`), at large radii (:math:`R > 10^{6}` for :math:`k \geq 1`, :math:`R > 10^{2}` for :math:`k \geq 3`) and, for a Type II model, in the band :math:`|1 - (R/R_\mathrm{a})^2| < 0.75` around :math:`R_\mathrm{a}` (:math:`k \geq 2`), as well as wherever the double-precision value is not finite.

For Method II, the input is a tabulated potential–density pair, so :math:`\varrho_\mathrm{aux}` has no closed form. PIANISTpy instead uses the integral representation of the truncated density in terms of the Method I model's DF, :ref:`Eq. (T:2.22) <tn-eq-method1-density>`: the derivatives of :math:`\hat\varrho` with respect to :math:`\hat{P}` are exact integrals of that DF and of its derivatives, evaluated by quadrature, and those of :math:`\hat\varrho_\mathrm{aux} = \hat\varrho/g` follow by the Leibniz rule, without any interpolation of :math:`\hat\varrho_\mathrm{aux}` (:ref:`Section T:2.6 <tn-sec-tabulated>`).

Early prototypes of PIANISTpy used the commonly adopted alternative numerical approach of directly interpolating the tabulated :math:`\varrho_\mathrm{aux}` in :math:`P` and differentiating the interpolating spline. There, a spline of degree :math:`p` that interpolates a smooth function to :math:`\mathcal{O}(h^{p+1})` in the knot spacing :math:`h` reproduces its :math:`k`-th derivative to no better than :math:`\mathcal{O}(h^{p+1-k})` (e.g., `de Boor 1978 <https://link.springer.com/book/9780387953663>`__), an error :math:`\epsilon` in a tabulated value of :math:`\varrho_\mathrm{aux}` enters the :math:`k`-th derivative as :math:`\epsilon/h^{k}`, and any jump in the knot spacing degrades the conditioning of the spline, exciting spurious oscillations in the derivatives of order two and higher even for a smooth and monotone :math:`\varrho_\mathrm{aux}`. Empirically, such amplified errors were found catastrophic and irreducible in the DFs that require the high-order derivatives of the Cuddeford inversion (:ref:`Section T:2.1 <tn-sec-forms>`). The current implementation of PIANISTpy thus takes no spline-based derivative of :math:`\varrho_\mathrm{aux}` (|paper|, Appendix C1.1; `Fornberg 1988 <https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-1988-0935077-0>`__).

.. _tn-sec-series:

T:1.2.2 The numerical join radius of the inner series
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

The analytical inner series expansions of the enclosed mass and the depth :math:`u` (|paper|, Eqs. (A12) and (A11); :ref:`Section T:1.1 <tn-sec-pair>`), summed to four terms in :math:`R^{\alpha}`, are used from the innermost tabulated radius (``log10_r_min``) out to the "join radius," the largest radius at which the relative truncation error of the depth series stays below :math:`10^{-7}`. The exact value of this join radius depends on, and only on, :math:`(\alpha, \beta, \gamma)`. For a truncated model, the Poisson solver uses the same analytical inner series over the same radial range (see also :ref:`Section T:3.4 <tn-sec-fills>`).

.. _tn-sec-profile-object:

T:1.3 Interpolation of the tabulated truncated profile
------------------------------------------------------

A truncated model in PIANISTpy is represented by a table of columns (the potential :math:`\hat{P}`, the density :math:`\hat\varrho` and the enclosed mass :math:`M`) on the tabulated grid of radii :math:`R`, accessed by the same scheme, detailed below, in every downstream numerical evaluation. First, :math:`\ln\hat{P}(R)` and :math:`\ln M(R)` are interpolated by piecewise cubic Hermite polynomials in :math:`\ln R`, with the local slopes evaluated pointwise by the Poisson equation :math:`\mathrm{d}\ln \hat{P}/\mathrm{d}\ln R = -M/(R\hat{P})` and the mass integral :math:`\mathrm{d}\ln M/\mathrm{d}\ln R = R^3\hat\varrho/M`, bypassing the use of finite differences. Second, :math:`\ln\hat\varrho(R)`, which has no such closed-form slope, is instead interpolated by a cubic spline in :math:`\ln R`. Third, for the radius at a given potential, :math:`R(\hat{P})`, instead of a separate interpolation, PIANISTpy directly inverts the piecewise Hermite polynomial of :math:`\hat{P}(R)` by root-finding bisection in :math:`\ln R`, such that :math:`R(\hat{P})` and :math:`\hat{P}(R)` are mutual inverses to rounding. For near-center numerical evaluations, :math:`R \to 0` inside the innermost tabulated radius, PIANISTpy extrapolates :math:`\hat{P}` and :math:`M` inward as power laws with their respective slopes at the innermost row, and :math:`\hat\varrho` by the end polynomial of its spline.

For a finitely truncated model, dedicated numerical treatments are needed when accessing the table near the truncation boundary :math:`\hat\varrho = 0` (:math:`R = R_\mathrm{t}`). The "edge" of the model denotes the outermost row of the table, with :math:`\hat{P} = 0` and :math:`M = M_\mathrm{t}`, where :math:`\ln\hat{P}` and :math:`\ln\hat\varrho` have no finite value, so the aforementioned logarithmic interpolation ends at the penultimate row. Between these two rows, PIANISTpy computes the potential in the point-mass (i.e., Keplerian) form :math:`M_\mathrm{t}(1/R - 1/R_\mathrm{t})` and the density as the power law :math:`\hat\varrho \propto \hat{P}^{q}` anchored at the penultimate row, with :math:`q` the analytical exponent of the density at the edge (see :ref:`Section T:2.6.1 <tn-sec-edge-law>`). At and beyond :math:`R_\mathrm{t}`, the profile simply returns :math:`\hat{P} = 0`, :math:`\hat\varrho = 0` and :math:`M = M_\mathrm{t}`; for the same physical reason, PIANISTpy accepts the evaluation of the velocity observables of :ref:`Section T:6 <tn-sec-observables>`, such as :math:`\sigma_r` or :math:`\sigma_t`, only at :math:`R \leq R_\mathrm{t}`.
