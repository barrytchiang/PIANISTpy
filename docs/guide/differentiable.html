Differentiable observables#

pianistpy.Differentiable

The object#

pp.Differentiable(model) holds a model of one component on a truncated profile (a distribution function inverted on its parent’s profile, as pianistpy.truncate() makes it) and serves its observables as JAX functions of the component’s anisotropy, \(\theta = (\beta_{\rm c}, R_{\rm a})\), passed as theta. Each method takes \(\theta\) first and mirrors the public call of the same name, traceable and differentiable in \(\theta\):

d = pp.Differentiable(model)
d.sigma_r(theta, r)                    # as pp.sigma_r(model at theta, r)
d.sigma_t(theta, r)                    # as pp.sigma_t
d.sigma_los(theta, r_2d)               # as pp.sigma_los
d.sigma_los_aperture(theta, aperture)  # as pp.sigma_los_aperture
d.losvd(theta, r_2d, v)                # as pp.losvd
d.pdf_v(theta, r, v)                   # as pp.pdf_v
d.aperture_losvd(theta, aperture, v)   # as pp.aperture_losvd

Everything is in internal units, whatever the model’s unit system: \(\theta\), as the likelihood’s, the radii and apertures a method reads, and the velocities it returns. d.theta is the model’s own anisotropy.

The families are those of the distribution function’s own rule (End-to-end differentiability): constant anisotropy (\(\beta_{\rm c}\); an isotropic or constant component’s \(R_{\rm a}\) is held at its own, infinite, value with a zero gradient), Cuddeford-Osipkov-Merritt (COM) Type I (\(\beta_{\rm c}\) and \(R_{\rm a}\)), Osipkov-Merritt Type I and Type II (\(R_{\rm a}\)). Refused at construction, each with an error that names it: COM Type II (its interior point at \(P(R_{\rm a})\) moves with both parameters), a superposition or a model of several components (its components are differentiated through the likelihood), a component that is not spatially finite, and one not on a truncated profile that carries its parent’s distribution function.

What a method computes#

A method’s value at \(\theta\) is the public call on the model rebuilt at \(\theta\): at the model’s own \(\theta\), the plain call’s own value, to the byte. Its derivative is the observable’s tangent rows in \(\theta\) on the tables’ own rows, their placement frozen, as the likelihood’s free components carry it: between two placements the value moves by the tables’ tolerance, which the derivative does not carry. Over an aperture the derivative is the aperture’s integral of the tangent rows of the squared dispersion, on the plain call’s own nodes and weights (the integral is linear in the rows). The line-of-sight velocity distribution, the speed distribution and the aperture’s line-of-sight velocity distribution take theirs through the distribution function’s own derivative (End-to-end differentiability), their placement frozen; a member whose distribution function that rule refuses is named at construction, and these three methods raise with it. At a \(\theta\) where the model is refused the value is not a number and its gradient zero. A method is not differentiable in its radii.

The cost#

The first time a method meets a \(\theta\), the host rebuilds the model there: the distribution function inverted at \(\theta\) on the component’s truncated profile, then the dispersion profile and the projected table with their tangent rows. That is the cost of the likelihood’s own rebuild of a free component, about that of the plain call on the model at \(\theta\). The last eight \(\theta\) are kept, so the value and the gradient at one \(\theta\) share one rebuild.

The derivative of losvd and aperture_losvd costs far more: it goes back through the rows of the line-of-sight table, whose transpose recomputes each row and differentiates it in \(\theta\), one row after another in the calling process. On a CPU node of 32 cores (AMD Ryzen Threadripper PRO 5975WX), the derivative of losvd in \(\beta_{\rm c}\) at one projected radius and 21 velocities, with the quick settings and a smaller table, did not finish in 2 h of wall-clock time, with 6 to 7 cores in use on average; the derivative of the example below takes 65 s there. The values of these methods are not affected.

Example#

The derivative of the line-of-sight dispersion at one projected radius in \(\beta_{\rm c}\), by the rule and by a central difference of the same function:

import jax
import numpy as np

import pianistpy as pp

parent = pp.solve(
    pp.Component(pp.DoublePowerLaw.hernquist(), pp.Anisotropy()), pp.Truncation(k_t=0.05)
)
model = pp.solve(pp.Component(parent.profile, pp.Anisotropy(beta_c=0.25)))
d = pp.Differentiable(model)

rule = float(jax.grad(lambda t: d.sigma_los(t, 0.3))(d.theta)[0])
h = np.array([1e-3, 0.0])
difference = float(d.sigma_los(d.theta + h, 0.3) - d.sigma_los(d.theta - h, 0.3)) / 2e-3
print(rule, difference)

The two agree to the difference’s own error; each of the two shifted \(\theta\) is one rebuild.
