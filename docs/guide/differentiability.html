End-to-end differentiability#

pianistpy.inference.likelihood(), pianistpy.df.gradient.make_inversion_table(), pianistpy.df.gradient.table_jacobian(), pianistpy.df.gradient.make_inversion_rows(), pianistpy.df.gradient.rows_jacobian()

What is differentiable#

  • The likelihood. The log-likelihood that pianistpy.inference.likelihood() returns is a JAX function of the components’ weights and of the model’s mass and length scales, which JAX traces and differentiates in each weight and each scale. With free=, the anisotropy \((\beta_{\rm c}, R_{\rm a})\) of each component listed in free is also a parameter of the log-likelihood (see Which models below and the Inference page). With free=, data of the binned distributions ("losvd", "losvd_aperture", "pdf_v") or of the Gauss-Hermite moments ("h3", "h4") raise InputError.

  • The observables. pianistpy.Differentiable provides the observables of a one-component model as JAX functions of the component’s anisotropy \((\beta_{\rm c}, R_{\rm a})\) (Differentiable observables).

  • The distribution function. The derivative of the distribution function in the central anisotropy \(\beta_{\rm c}\) and the anisotropy radius \(R_{\rm a}\) is available at the inversion’s energies and for the full table (see The distribution function below).

The derivative of the distribution function is available for the following anisotropy models, with their parameters:

  • isotropic and constant anisotropy \((\beta_{\rm c})\),

  • Cuddeford-Osipkov-Merritt (COM) Type I (\(\beta_{\rm c}\) and \(R_{\rm a}\)),

  • Osipkov-Merritt Type I and Type II \((R_{\rm a})\).

The following cases raise InputError:

  • COM Type II. The COM Type II inversion has an interior point at the potential \(P(R_{\rm a})\), which moves with both \(R_{\rm a}\) and \(\beta_{\rm c}\), so the derivative is not integrable at \(P(R_{\rm a})\). The log-likelihood of "sigma_los" and "sigma_los_aperture" data still has a derivative in the anisotropy of a free COM Type II component, computed from the Jeans equation. Differentiable raises InputError for a COM Type II component. The distribution function of a COM Type II component and the observables read from the distribution function (losvd, aperture_losvd, pdf_v) have no derivative.

  • A distribution function whose center lies on the critical branch, where the inner slope \(\gamma\) of the density equals \(2\beta_{\rm c}\) and the form of the center changes with \(\beta_{\rm c}\).

  • A component on a truncated profile that does not carry the distribution function of the model the profile was taken from.

Which models#

The anisotropy is a parameter, through likelihood with free= and through pp.Differentiable, only for a Method II model: a component whose distribution function is inverted on another model’s truncated profile. The truncated profile is held fixed, so the profile and the potential do not change with the anisotropy, and the derivative is complete. Other models raise InputError:

  • A Method I truncated model. The truncated profile of a Method I model follows from the model’s own distribution function, so the truncated profile changes with \(\beta_{\rm c}\) and \(R_{\rm a}\). Ver. 1.0.0 does not compute the derivative of the truncated profile in the anisotropy.

  • An untruncated model, or a model that is not spatially finite.

The distribution function#

The derivative of the distribution function is available in two forms:

  • the rows (the distribution function on the inversion’s energy grid), through make_inversion_rows and rows_jacobian, for a component on its own analytic profile only;

  • the table, as the lookup reads it, through make_inversion_table and table_jacobian. The derivative covers every array of the table: the rows (log_df, log_df_slope), the nodes below the table’s first energy (log_df_below, log_df_slope_below), the closed form at the center (central) and the tail (tail). For a Method II component, the arrays are the rows (log_df), the power law at the lowest energies (edge_law) and the closed form at the center (center).

make_inversion_rows and make_inversion_table each return a JAX function of the anisotropy parameters, the names of the parameters (params), and the energies or the table at the component’s own parameters. The value of the returned function is the result of compute_df() at the given parameters, computed on the CPU. The derivative of the returned function is the package’s analytic derivative (Eq. (T:7.1) of the Technical notes). The *_jacobian functions return the values and their derivatives as arrays.

Both returned functions hold the energies at the component’s own parameters. The function that make_inversion_table returns makes the anchor of the closed form at the center and the nodes kept again at each new parameter value, except that a component on a truncated profile also holds the nodes kept at the component’s own parameters. The derivative is thus the derivative of the table that compute_df() returns at the given parameters, on the held energies. Where a discrete choice would change between two parameter values, the value of the returned function differs from a new compute_df() call by up to the tolerance of the inversion.

Accuracy and the record#

The derivative is exact except for the derivative of the inversion’s operator in \(\beta_{\rm c}\). The derivative of the operator in \(\beta_{\rm c}\) has no closed form and is taken by finite differences of the operator’s values at nearby \(\beta_{\rm c}\). rows_jacobian and table_jacobian return a record of the finite differences:

  • the kind of difference: central (a Richardson quotient) where the inversion’s form is the same on both sides of \(\beta_{\rm c}\), or one-sided at a \(\beta_{\rm c}\) where the form changes (integer and half-integer \(\beta_{\rm c}\));

  • the step of the difference;

  • the Richardson residual of the quotient and, for a one-sided difference, the spread between the derivatives from the two sides, relative to the largest \(|\partial F/\partial\beta_{\rm c}|\) in the record of rows_jacobian and absolute in the record of table_jacobian;

  • for a component on a truncated profile, where the component’s energy grid is placed.

The Richardson residual is the accuracy of the derivative, read against the thresholds \(10^{-6}\), \(10^{-4}\) and \(10^{-2}\) of the validation reports (Accuracy). Where the difference is central, the residual is below \(10^{-6}\). At the isotropic value \(\beta_{\rm c} = 0\), the difference is one-sided, and the residual is about \(10^{-5}\) of the largest derivative, between \(10^{-6}\) and \(10^{-4}\). The record gives the residual. A gradient at \(\beta_{\rm c} = 0\) is usable at an error of about \(10^{-5}\). For a gradient with an error below \(10^{-6}\), take a \(\beta_{\rm c}\) away from 0, where the difference is central.

Example#

The derivative of \(\log_{10} F\) at one node of a Hernquist component’s table, by the analytic derivative and by a central difference of the same function:

import jax
import numpy as np

import pianistpy as pp
from pianistpy.df.gradient import make_inversion_table

halo = pp.Component(pp.DoublePowerLaw.hernquist(), pp.Anisotropy(beta_c=0.25))
table, params, values = make_inversion_table(halo, pp.Settings())
node = int(np.searchsorted(values["k"], 0.5))  # the first node at or above K = 0.5


def log_df(theta):
    return table(theta)["log_df"][node]


theta = np.array([0.25])  # params == ("beta_c",)
rule = float(jax.grad(log_df)(theta)[0])
h = 1e-3
difference = float(log_df(theta + h) - log_df(theta - h)) / (2 * h)
print(rule, difference)

The analytic derivative and the central difference agree to the error of the central difference, of order \(h^2\) times the third derivative. The table is made once. Each call of table solves the inversion again at the new parameters, on the held grid.
