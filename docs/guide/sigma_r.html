Radial velocity dispersion#

pianistpy.sigma_r(), pianistpy.LosTable

sigma_r(source, r) returns the intrinsic radial velocity dispersion \(\sigma_{\rm r}\) at 3D radii: the second moment of the radial velocity over the model’s distribution function at a fixed radius. As in Chiang+(2026d), it is the velocity moment of the augmented density \(\varrho_{\rm aug}(P, R)\) taken at the potential of the radius,

\[\sigma_{\rm r}^2(R) = \frac{1}{\varrho_{\rm aug}(P, R)} \int_0^{P} \varrho_{\rm aug}(\mathcal{K}, R)\, {\rm d}\mathcal{K}, \qquad P = P(R),\]

which for the anisotropy models of the package, whose augmented density separates as \(\varrho_{\rm aug}(P, R) = \varrho_{\rm aux}(P)\,g(R)\), is the Jeans integral of \(\varrho_{\rm aux}\) over the potential. The radius \(R\) of Chiang+(2026d) is r in the package.

Arguments and units#

source is a Model (one component, or a superposition) or its prepared LosTable, which holds the same intrinsic profile. r is an array of radii in the model’s unit system: internal units by default (the scale radius is 1), parsecs (a float or an Astropy quantity) after model.to("physical", ...). The result has the shape of r, in the model’s velocity unit (km/s in the physical system).

Of a superposition, whose components share one density, the second moments add with the weights: \(\sigma_{\rm r}^2 = \sum_k w_k \sigma_{{\rm r},k}^2\).

Tiers#

The profile is tabulated once per model in radius, with rows added until every interval between rows reads within profile_tolerance at its midpoint (\(10^{-7}\) at the default tier, \(10^{-6}\) at the quick tier; a tenth of the goal, the profile’s share of the error of what is projected from it). A model beyond the hard limit is refused unless its settings ask for it (write_anyway), as for every observable (Accuracy).

Example#

import numpy as np
import pianistpy as pp

# a Hernquist density with a constant anisotropy of 0.25, truncated at K_t = 0.05
model = pp.solve(
    pp.Component(pp.DoublePowerLaw.hernquist(), pp.Anisotropy(beta_c=0.25)),
    pp.Truncation(k_t=0.05),
    settings=pp.Settings(quick=True),
)
r = np.array([0.1, 1.0, 10.0])
pp.sigma_r(model, r)                          # internal units

physical = model.to("physical", concentration=10.0, mass_0=1e9, r_0=500.0)
pp.sigma_r(physical, [50.0, 500.0, 5000.0])   # parsecs in, km/s out
