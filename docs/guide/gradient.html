End-to-end differentiability#

pianistpy.inference.likelihood(), pianistpy.df.gradient.make_inversion_table(), pianistpy.df.gradient.table_jacobian(), pianistpy.df.gradient.make_inversion_rows(), pianistpy.df.gradient.rows_jacobian()

What is differentiable#

The weights and the unit scales. The likelihood of a model against data, pianistpy.inference.likelihood(), is a JAX function of the components’ weights and of the model’s mass and length scales, traceable and differentiable in both. With free=, the anisotropy \((\beta_{\rm c}, R_{\rm a})\) of the components named there is a parameter of it as well, for a component on a truncated profile with a finite \(R_{\rm a}\); the inference page states how. The two calls serve different members: the rule below serves an untruncated analytic component, while likelihood refuses a member in free= that is not spatially finite, by name, since it reads the distribution function at the model’s edge.

The observables. pianistpy.Differentiable serves a model’s observables as JAX functions of its anisotropy \((\beta_{\rm c}, R_{\rm a})\), each with the public call’s own value and the tangent rows’ derivative: Differentiable observables.

The anisotropy parameters. The distribution function of a component is differentiable in its central anisotropy \(\beta_{\rm c}\) and its anisotropy radius \(R_{\rm a}\), for the families served:

  • constant anisotropy (\(\beta_{\rm c}\)),

  • Cuddeford-Osipkov-Merritt (COM) Type I (\(\beta_{\rm c}\) and \(R_{\rm a}\)),

  • Osipkov-Merritt Type I and Type II (\(R_{\rm a}\)).

Two levels are offered. The inversion’s rows (the distribution function on the inversion’s energy grid) through make_inversion_rows and rows_jacobian; the distribution function’s table, as the lookup reads it, through make_inversion_table and table_jacobian. The table’s derivative covers every part of it: the rows, the nodes below the written floor, the form above the anchor at the center and the tail at the outer end. On a component on the truncated profile of another model (Method II), the table’s parts are the rows, the edge law below the floor and the center’s stated form; the rows’ functions serve a component on its own analytic profile only.

make_inversion_rows and make_inversion_table return a JAX function of the parameter vector with a custom derivative: its value is the package’s own solve, computed on the host, and its derivative is the rule. The *_jacobian functions return the values and their derivatives as arrays. In both, the energy grid and every discrete choice of the solve (the grid’s placement, the inversion’s form, the anchor, the nodes kept) are held at the parameters the function was made at: the derivative is that of the solve with these choices fixed, which is the derivative of the table the package returns there. Where a choice changes between two parameters, the solve at the second one differs from the held one by its own tolerance.

Not served, each refused with an error that names it: COM Type II, a center on its critical branch (where the branch itself changes with \(\beta_{\rm c}\)), and a component on a truncated profile that carries no distribution function of its model.

COM Type II. The inversion’s kernel carries the factor \((P - P(R_{\rm a}))^s\), with \(s = 1 - \beta_{\rm c} - j\) and \(j\) the inversion’s order: its interior point \(P(R_{\rm a})\) moves with \(R_{\rm a}\) and its exponent with \(\beta_{\rm c}\). The derivative of that factor in \(R_{\rm a}\) is not integrable at the interior point for \(s \le 0\), so the rows of the mixed derivative do not carry the derivative there, and the rule refuses the family for both parameters, naming the interior point. A COM Type II model’s second moments take their derivative by the Jeans path (dispersion_profile’s tangents); its distribution function, and the observables read from it (losvd, aperture_losvd, pdf_v), take none.

Where a model’s anisotropy is a parameter. The derivative of a model’s observables in \((\beta_{\rm c}, R_{\rm a})\), through likelihood with free= and through pp.Differentiable, is the full derivative for a Method II model: a component whose distribution function is inverted on another model’s truncated profile, held fixed, so its profile and its potential do not move with the anisotropy. A Method I truncated model is refused by name: its truncated profile is that of its own distribution function, which moves with \(\beta_{\rm c}\) and \(R_{\rm a}\), so neither the Jeans path (a fixed profile) nor the distribution function’s path at a fixed potential carries the full derivative, and the profile’s own motion through the truncation is not served in this version. An untruncated model is refused on both paths, since its edge terms read its distribution function.

On a GPU, the derivative of losvd and aperture_losvd through pp.Differentiable may refuse a row of the line-of-sight table by name (a ResultError: the row recomputed level by level is not the row): its transpose recomputes each row and holds it to the forward’s row bit for bit, which a GPU’s order of summation does not promise. It never returns a wrong number. On the CPU every row is served.

Per call#

  • pianistpy.inference.likelihood: differentiable in the weights, the unit scales and, with free=, the \((\beta_{\rm c}, R_{\rm a})\) of the named components; the families and refusals as above.

  • The distribution function (make_inversion_rows, make_inversion_table): differentiable in \((\beta_{\rm c}, R_{\rm a})\) for the families served.

Each observable is differentiable in \((\beta_{\rm c}, R_{\rm a})\) through pp.Differentiable(model), whose methods mirror the public calls as JAX functions of \(\theta\), for the families above and with the same refusals.

  • sigma_r: differentiable in \((\beta_{\rm c}, R_{\rm a})\) through pp.Differentiable.

  • sigma_t: differentiable in \((\beta_{\rm c}, R_{\rm a})\) through pp.Differentiable.

  • sigma_los: differentiable in \((\beta_{\rm c}, R_{\rm a})\) through pp.Differentiable.

  • sigma_los_aperture: differentiable in \((\beta_{\rm c}, R_{\rm a})\) through pp.Differentiable.

  • losvd: differentiable in \((\beta_{\rm c}, R_{\rm a})\) through pp.Differentiable.

  • aperture_losvd: differentiable in \((\beta_{\rm c}, R_{\rm a})\) through pp.Differentiable.

  • pdf_v: differentiable in \((\beta_{\rm c}, R_{\rm a})\) through pp.Differentiable.

What the record carries#

The rule is exact except for one part, the derivative of the inversion’s operator itself in \(\beta_{\rm c}\) at a fixed source, which has no closed form and is taken from the operator’s values at nearby \(\beta_{\rm c}\). The record returned with the derivative states how:

  • central (a Richardson quotient) inside a form’s region, or one-sided at a corner, where the inversion’s form changes on one side (integer and half-integer \(\beta_{\rm c}\));

  • the step, set by measurement: the kernel’s accuracy balanced against the quotient’s truncation term;

  • the quotient’s Richardson residual and, at a corner, the spread of its two sides, both over the column’s scale;

  • for a component on a truncated profile, where its energy grid is placed.

The residual is the derivative’s accuracy, and it reads in the package’s tiers (Accuracy). Inside a form’s region it is at the goal. At the isotropic corner, \(\beta_{\rm c} = 0\), the quotient is one-sided, and its residual is about \(10^{-5}\) of the column: the ceiling tier, not the goal, which the record states. A gradient there is usable at that accuracy; one at the goal takes a \(\beta_{\rm c}\) off the corner.

Example#

The derivative of \(\log_{10} F\) at one node of a Hernquist component’s table, by the rule and by a central difference of the same function:

import jax
import numpy as np

import pianistpy as pp
from pianistpy.df.gradient import make_inversion_table

halo = pp.Component(pp.DoublePowerLaw.hernquist(), pp.Anisotropy(beta_c=0.25))
table, params, values = make_inversion_table(halo, pp.Settings())
node = int(np.searchsorted(values["k"], 0.5))  # the node nearest K = 0.5


def log_df(theta):
    return table(theta)["log_df"][node]


theta = np.array([0.25])  # params == ("beta_c",)
rule = float(jax.grad(log_df)(theta)[0])
h = 1e-3
difference = float(log_df(theta + h) - log_df(theta - h)) / (2 * h)
print(rule, difference)

The two agree to the difference’s own error (\(h^2\) times the third derivative). The table is made once; each call of table solves the inversion again at the new parameters on the held grid.
