Line-of-sight velocity distribution#

pianistpy.losvd(), pianistpy.LosTable

losvd(source, r_2d, v) returns the line-of-sight velocity distribution \(D(R_{\rm 2D}, V_{\rm los})\) at projected radii: the probability density of the velocity along the line of sight of the tracers seen at a projected distance \(R_{\rm 2D}\) from the center. As in the paper, it is the distribution function integrated over the line of sight and over the two velocity components on the plane of the sky,

\[D(R_{\rm 2D}, V_{\rm los}) = \frac{2}{\Sigma(R_{\rm 2D})} \int_{R_{\rm 2D}}^{R_{\rm t}} \frac{R\,{\rm d}R}{(R^2 - R_{\rm 2D}^2)^{1/2}} \iint F(\mathcal{Z}, L)\,{\rm d}V_{\perp 1}\,{\rm d}V_{\perp 2}, \qquad \Sigma(R_{\rm 2D}) = 2 \int_{R_{\rm 2D}}^{R_{\rm t}} \frac{\varrho(R)\,R\,{\rm d}R}{(R^2 - R_{\rm 2D}^2)^{1/2}},\]

with the inner integral over the plane-of-sky velocities at which the truncated distribution function is positive. It is even in \(V_{\rm los}\), integrates to one over all \(V_{\rm los}\), and is zero beyond the escape velocity along the line of sight. Its second moment is \(\sigma_{\rm los}^2\) (pianistpy.sigma_los()).

Arguments and units#

source is a Model or its prepared LosTable; given a model, its table is built first, so for more than one call build the table once (The prepared table: LosTable, which also says how to call it from a script). r_2d and v are arrays that broadcast against each other: projected radii in the model’s unit of length (internal units by default, parsecs or an Astropy quantity in the physical system) and line-of-sight velocities in its unit of velocity (km/s in the physical system). The result has their broadcast shape, per unit velocity of the model’s unit system.

Of a superposition, each component’s distribution is read from its own table and the components are summed with their weights times their surface densities, \(D = \sum_k w_k \Sigma_k D_k / \sum_k w_k \Sigma_k\).

Tiers#

The distribution is read from the rows of the table between rows by the reading the table certified its rows for, so the tier is that of the table (The prepared table: LosTable): each row’s norm against the surface density and its second moment against the projected dispersion, and the table’s estimate of its reading between rows. A projected radius in an interval the table left uncertified is refused by name.

Example#

import numpy as np
import pianistpy as pp

model = pp.solve(
    pp.Component(pp.DoublePowerLaw.hernquist(), pp.Anisotropy(r_a=1.0)),
    pp.Truncation(k_t=0.05),
    settings=pp.Settings(quick=True),
)
table = pp.LosTable(model)

v = np.linspace(-1.5, 1.5, 3001)
d = pp.losvd(table, 1.0, v)                    # at R_2D = 1, shape (3001,)
dv = v[1] - v[0]
d.sum() * dv                                   # one, to the grid's rule
np.sqrt((v**2 * d).sum() * dv), pp.sigma_los(table, 1.0)

pp.losvd(table, np.array([[0.1], [1.0], [10.0]]), v).shape   # (3, 3001): radii by velocities
