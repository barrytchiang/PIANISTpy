Truncation in phase space#

pianistpy.Truncation, pianistpy.truncate(), pianistpy.solve(), pianistpy.Model

Method I#

truncate(df, truncation) returns a Model, which holds the distribution function truncated at an energy and the self-consistent potential-density pair of the truncated distribution function. If the untruncated distribution function is everywhere non-negative and monotonic, the truncated distribution function inherits both properties, and the truncation boundary is in dynamical equilibrium. A request with no physical model raises UnphysicalModelError, which carries the distribution function, or the model without an edge, for inspection.

At \(\beta_{\rm c} = -1/2\) and a steep inner density slope \(\gamma\), the iteration for the center of the truncated model does not converge, and truncate raises AccuracyError. For example, with \((\alpha, \beta) = (2, 4)\), \(\beta_{\rm c} = -1/2\) and \(\mathcal{K}_{\rm t} = 0.3\), truncate raises AccuracyError from \(\gamma = 1.735\) upward.

Truncation takes exactly one of three inputs:

pp.Truncation(k_t=0.2)                          # the truncation energy, in internal units
pp.Truncation(mass_t=1.0, concentration=10.0)   # a target mass, in units of M_0 (model units)
pp.Truncation(r_t=5.0)                          # a target radius, in units of the scale radius

A target mass is given in units of \(M_0\), so it needs the concentration that defines \(M_0\). A target radius or a truncation energy needs no concentration, and a concentration given with r_t or k_t raises InputError.

solve(component, truncation) runs compute_df and truncate in one call. Without a Truncation, truncate and solve return the untruncated model, or, for a Method II component, the model that ends at the edge of the truncated density that the component uses.

A model holds:

  • the truncated distribution function;

  • the truncated density, potential and enclosed mass on one radial grid (model.profile.r, .rho_trunc, .p_trunc, .mass_trunc);

  • the truncation radius and the total mass;

  • the validation report and the identity string, a digest of the inputs, the settings and the package version.

An untruncated model is not spatially finite. The untruncated model’s spatially_finite is false, and the untruncated model’s truncation radius r_t is infinite. A function that needs a spatially finite model, such as Sampler without r_sample, raises InputError for a model that is not spatially finite (Flags and refusals).

Type II models#

A Type II model (Osipkov-Merritt or Cuddeford-Osipkov-Merritt) must end inside its anisotropy radius \(R_{\rm a}\), where the model’s density \(\varrho_{\rm aux} / (1 - R^2/R_{\rm a}^2)\), with \(\varrho_{\rm aux}\) the auxiliary density, has a pole.

  • The truncation energy must satisfy \(\mathcal{K}_{\rm t} > P(R_{\rm a})\). Where this bound sets k_t_min, k_t_min is the next floating-point number above \(P(R_{\rm a})\).

  • The bound is necessary, not sufficient. The bound keeps inside \(R_{\rm a}\) the radius at which the untruncated potential equals \(\mathcal{K}_{\rm t}\), but the truncated model’s own edge lies farther out than that radius. Where the truncated model’s edge would reach \(R_{\rm a}\), truncate raises UnphysicalModelError with a message that begins TYPE II EDGE and states the potential at \(R_{\rm a}\) or the radius of the edge. A larger \(\mathcal{K}_{\rm t}\) moves the truncated model’s edge inside \(R_{\rm a}\).

Osipkov-Merritt Type I models with a small anisotropy radius#

An Osipkov-Merritt Type I model with a small \(R_{\rm a}\) and a low \(\mathcal{K}_{\rm t}\) can have no edge inside the largest radius at which truncate solves for the potential. truncate then raises UnphysicalModelError with a message that begins NO EDGE. On the Hernquist density with the quick settings, truncate raises UnphysicalModelError (NO EDGE) for the following Osipkov-Merritt Type I models:

  • \(R_{\rm a} = 1\) at every \(\mathcal{K}_{\rm t}\) from 0.005 to 0.3;

  • \(R_{\rm a} = 2\) below \(\mathcal{K}_{\rm t} = 0.2\);

  • \(R_{\rm a} = 5\) below \(\mathcal{K}_{\rm t} = 0.03\);

  • \(R_{\rm a} = 10\) at \(\mathcal{K}_{\rm t} = 0.005\).

The Osipkov-Merritt Type I models on the Hernquist density with \(R_{\rm a}\) from 1 to 10 and \(\mathcal{K}_{\rm t}\) from 0.005 to 0.3 that truncate builds have the tier "recorded", above \(10^{-6}\) and at most \(10^{-4}\) (Accuracy). The check high_k_slope_mismatch of the distribution function of each built model is \(3.4 \times 10^{-6}\), at \(\mathcal{K} = 1\).

Method II#

Method II gives a new anisotropy to a density that is already truncated. The new component takes the truncated profile of a Method I model as its profile. solve, or compute_df and truncate, without a Truncation, build the Method II model from the new component.

truncated = pp.solve(pp.Component(pp.DoublePowerLaw.nfw(), pp.Anisotropy()), pp.Truncation(k_t=0.2))
model = pp.solve(pp.Component(truncated.profile, pp.Anisotropy(beta_c=-0.25)))

A Method II model exists only when its anisotropy is compatible with the edge of the Method I model’s truncated density. Where that density goes as \(\hat{P}^q\) at the edge, with \(\hat{P}\) the Method I model’s truncated relative potential, which is zero at the edge, the central anisotropy of the new component must satisfy \(\beta_{\rm c} > 1/2 - q\). A Component whose \(\beta_{\rm c}\) is not above \(1/2 - q\) raises UnphysicalModelError when the Component is created, and the error message states the bound. The Technical notes derive the condition \(\beta_{\rm c} > 1/2 - q\).
